amoCRM (Kommo) vs Zoho CRM which one fits a business in the UAE

Both are solid CRMs, and both are used by businesses in Dubai. They are built around different ideas, so the right choice depends less on the feature list and more on how your clients reach you and what else the system has to run. Below is the comparison we walk owners through on a review call.

Talk to us

Two different philosophies

amoCRM, sold outside Russia and the CIS under the Kommo brand, is built around a sales pipeline and messengers. A new chat in WhatsApp or Instagram becomes a lead, the conversation stays inside the deal card, and the team moves the deal across stages until the client books or pays. It is a sales tool first.

Zoho CRM is one application in a large business suite. It covers sales too, but its strength is breadth and configuration: custom modules, approval processes, territories and a direct link to Zoho Books, Desk, Campaigns, Analytics and dozens of other Zoho apps. It is closer to a platform you build company processes on.

Neither is better in general. A salon that gets dozens of Instagram chats a day and a trading company that issues quotes and VAT invoices need different tools.

Messengers and automation in practice

For a business that sells in chats, the key question is where the conversation lives. In amoCRM every new chat lands in the incoming leads stage, and the administrator sees the history, the source and the deal on one screen. Salesbot can greet the client, ask a qualifying question, collect a phone number and move the deal on; the digital pipeline creates tasks, sends reminders and changes the responsible person when a deal reaches a stage.

In Zoho the same is possible, but it takes more setup. Messenger channels are added through SalesIQ or extensions, and automation is built with workflow rules and Blueprint. Blueprint is strong where a process must follow strict steps, for example a quote that needs a manager's approval before it goes to the client.

Whichever you lean towards, check these points before you sign:

  • WhatsApp for a business number goes through the official WhatsApp Business Platform; unofficial connectors that log in like WhatsApp Web put the number at risk of being blocked
  • One number can be shared by several administrators, and each chat has one responsible person
  • Telephony: whether your UAE provider has a ready integration with the CRM you choose
  • Website forms and lead ads: a lead should land in the CRM with its source, not in an email inbox
  • A trial account with your real channels and a week of real chats, before you pay for a long period

Which fits which business

This is the fit we see most often. It is a starting point, not a rule: a well-run Zoho account beats a neglected amoCRM, and the other way round.

  • Beauty salon with most leads from Instagram and WhatsApp: amoCRM takes the client up to the booking, the schedule stays in the booking system
  • Aesthetic or medical clinic: amoCRM for consultations and treatment plans sold in chats; Zoho if the group has several branches, a call centre and finance in Zoho Books. Keep medical records in the clinic's medical system, not in the sales CRM
  • Real estate: both are used; amoCRM when most enquiries arrive in WhatsApp from portals and ads, Zoho when the brokerage has several teams, listing management and deeper reporting. Check how leads from the portals you list on will reach the CRM
  • Education and training centres: amoCRM for enrolments that are decided in chats; Zoho if contracts, invoices and student groups also live in the same system
  • B2B services with quotes and long sales cycles: Zoho CRM, often together with Zoho Books

When you need neither

A CRM is not always the first step. A small salon where most clients book online, with one or two administrators and a steady flow of regulars, often gets more from a well-configured booking system such as Altegio or Fresha: client base, reminders and online booking in one place. WhatsApp Business with labels and quick replies covers a small volume of chats.

When a CRM is added, it usually works alongside the booking system: the CRM handles the lead until the booking, the booking system handles the schedule and the visit. A separate CRM starts to pay off when at least one of these is true:

  • Many enquiries do not book on the first contact and need follow-up
  • Several people answer the same WhatsApp and Instagram, and nobody can say who promised what
  • You pay for ads and need to know which source brings paying clients
  • You sell packages, courses or treatment plans that take several conversations
  • The owner wants a daily report without asking the team

FAQ

Which is better for a small business in Dubai: amoCRM or Zoho CRM?

If most clients reach you in WhatsApp and Instagram and the job is to turn chats into bookings or sales, amoCRM (Kommo) is usually faster to launch and easier for the team. If you need several departments, quotes, approvals and accounting with UAE VAT in one ecosystem, Zoho CRM is usually the better base. For a very small salon a booking system may be enough.

Is Kommo the same as amoCRM?

Yes, Kommo is the brand amoCRM uses outside Russia and the CIS. The core logic is the same: a sales pipeline, a messenger inbox, Salesbot and the digital pipeline. Plans, prices and some integrations may differ, so check the current details on the vendor site.

Can Zoho CRM work with WhatsApp?

Yes. WhatsApp can be connected through Zoho's own tools, such as SalesIQ or built-in integrations, or through extensions from the Zoho Marketplace. It takes more setup than in amoCRM, so check which edition and which add-ons your channels need before you buy.
