UAE rules for patient data in a clinic's CRM and WhatsApp

A clinic that brings enquiries into a CRM and answers patients in WhatsApp handles health data every day, often without noticing. In the UAE this data has its own law with strict rules on where it is stored and how it may be used, and medical advertising in Dubai has its own standards, including messages in WhatsApp. This guide explains in plain words what goes into the CRM, what stays in the medical system, when you need written consent and how to write messages that stay within the rules. It is practical guidance, not legal advice: confirm the setup for your clinic with your medical director or lawyer.

Which rules apply

Several sets of rules meet in a clinic's chats and CRM. The main one for patient data is Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 on the use of information and communication technology in health fields. It applies to health facilities across the UAE, including the free zones, and it defines health data broadly: information collected about a patient in connection with health services can fall under it, not only diagnoses.

The UAE personal data protection law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, does not apply to health data that is regulated by its own legislation, but it still matters for contact details and direct marketing. Dubai Healthcare City, DIFC and ADGM have their own data regimes, so first check which one covers your licence.

  • Federal Law No. 2 of 2019, Article 13: health data related to health services provided in the UAE is stored and processed inside the country unless an exception applies
  • Ministerial Resolution No. 51 of 2021: lists the exceptions, for example treatment abroad, insurance claims, samples sent to laboratories abroad, telemedicine, research and a patient's own request, each with its own conditions such as consent or encryption
  • The same law: health data may not be used for non-health purposes without the patient's written consent
  • Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021: consent for marketing must be clear and specific, and a person can object to direct marketing at any time
  • DHA standards for medical advertisement content on social media: they cover Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp and other platforms used by DHA-licensed facilities and professionals
  • Cabinet Resolution No. 56 of 2024 on telemarketing: marketing calls only from 9:00 to 18:00, from company numbers, with the consumer's consent and frequency limits

What goes into the CRM and what stays in the medical system

The simplest safe setup is to split roles. The medical system holds everything clinical. The CRM holds what the front desk needs to answer, book and follow up, and nothing more.

Because even a name with an appointment at a clinic can count as health data, ask your CRM provider where your account is hosted. Zoho opened data centres in Dubai and Abu Dhabi in 2026; for any CRM, check with the vendor whether your account can be hosted in the UAE, and agree the setup with your lawyer. Chats: what the front desk and the bot do with medical questions

Patients write in WhatsApp the way they talk: they describe symptoms, send photos and lab results, ask whether a treatment is right for them. The front desk and the bot need written rules for this, otherwise clinical information ends up scattered across personal phones and CRM notes.

  • The bot never diagnoses, never assesses contraindications and never discusses test results; it offers a consultation or hands the chat to a doctor or nurse
  • If a patient describes an acute condition, the bot advises calling an ambulance on 998 and alerts a staff member at once
  • The front desk does not ask for medical details, Emirates ID or card details in chat; documents go through the clinic's booking or medical system
  • If a patient sends a medical file anyway, it goes into the medical record by the clinic's procedure and is not discussed in the chat
  • Chats live in the clinic's business account and CRM, never on personal phones
  • Access is by role: the front desk sees bookings and contacts, marketing sees statistics, only the owner exports the database

Reminders, offers and consent

Reminders about an appointment the patient booked are part of the service. Offers, win-back messages and news are marketing. For marketing to existing patients you need their consent: the health data law requires written consent to use health data for non-health purposes, and the personal data law gives everyone the right to refuse direct marketing. Keep the two apart and do not slip an offer into a reminder.

On WhatsApp, messages outside the 24-hour window after the patient's last message go only through the WhatsApp Business Platform with templates approved by Meta, and WhatsApp's policy also requires opt-in and an easy way to opt out.

  • Collect consent separately for reminders and for offers, in plain words, with a date and the channel
  • Put a short way to stop messages in every offer, and honour it the same day
  • Do not build mailing lists from medical records: a segment such as patients after a specific treatment is safe only with written consent to marketing
  • Keep promotional calls within the telemarketing rules, or better, offer to continue in WhatsApp
  • Check the opt-out list before every campaign

Texts that follow the DHA standards

Dubai's standards for medical advertisement content on social media apply to DHA-licensed facilities and professionals on all major platforms, WhatsApp included. In practice this covers broadcast texts, bot answers about treatments and posts by doctors that name the clinic.

  • No absolute or exaggerated claims such as the best, 100% or instant results
  • No claims that one treatment or clinic is better or safer than another
  • Before-and-after images only of the same person, unedited, with a note that results may vary
  • Patient photos, videos and testimonials only with documented written consent for that purpose and period
  • Promotional content that names the clinic is signed off by its medical director
  • Facts are fine: services, opening hours, contacts, qualifications of doctors and accreditations

Checklist before you launch a CRM and a bot

  • Check which regime covers your licence: onshore UAE, Dubai Healthcare City, DIFC or ADGM
  • Write down what the CRM holds and what stays in the medical system, and agree it with the medical director
  • Ask the CRM and bot providers where data is hosted and who can access it
  • Write the bot's red lines: no diagnosis, no test results, emergencies to 998, medical questions to a doctor
  • Set up separate consent fields for reminders and for offers, with an opt-out in every offer
  • Check every template and every bot answer about treatments against the DHA advertising standards
  • Give access by role and close the accounts of staff who leave on their last day
  • Read a sample of chats every week and update the rules

FAQ

Can a clinic keep patient data in amoCRM or another cloud CRM?

Keep the CRM to contacts, enquiries and bookings, and leave clinical information in the medical system. UAE law requires health data to be stored and processed in the country unless an exception applies, so ask the vendor where your account is hosted and agree the setup with your medical director or lawyer.

Do we need consent to send WhatsApp offers to former patients?

Yes. The health data law requires written consent to use health data for non-health purposes, the personal data law gives a right to refuse direct marketing, and WhatsApp requires opt-in and an easy opt-out. Reminders about a booked appointment are part of the service, but keep offers out of them.

Do DHA advertising rules apply to WhatsApp messages?

The DHA standards for medical advertisement content on social media list WhatsApp among the platforms they cover. Broadcasts and bot answers about treatments should avoid absolute claims, comparisons and patient content without written consent.
