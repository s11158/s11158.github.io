How to hire an executive assistant in Dubai a step-by-step plan for employers

An executive assistant in Dubai works inside your calendar, inbox and confidential decisions, so a wrong hire costs more than the salary: missed meetings, leaked information and weeks of retraining. This guide walks through the process in the order that works: define the scope, set the budget, find candidates, build a shortlist, interview, test the skills on real tasks, make the offer and manage probation. At the end it explains plainly what TLNT does and what stays with you.

Step 1 and 2: define the scope and set the budget

Most weak hires start with a vague brief like help me with everything. Write down what the person will actually do in a typical week and which of those tasks only this person can do. Then set the budget from the market, not from a wish.

  • List the tasks in three groups: calendar and travel, inbox and documents, and everything else (household, vendors, events, follow-ups with your team)
  • Decide the level of access: your email, your calendar, your payments approvals, your documents. Higher access means higher trust and a higher salary
  • Decide the working pattern: office, hybrid, at your home or business, evenings, weekends, travel with you
  • Decide languages: English is the base; Arabic helps with government and local partners; Russian helps if you and your circle are Russian-speaking
  • Budget orientation (market estimate, AED per month): executive assistant to an owner or director about 12,000 - 20,000; assistant to a C-level executive or a private family office about 20,000 - 30,000; a coordinator-level role lower. See our office staff salary guide for the full table
  • Add the cost on top of salary: visa and Emirates ID, health insurance, gratuity provision, annual leave, equipment

Step 3: sourcing candidates

Good assistants are rarely unemployed for long, and many do not apply to public postings. Use several channels at once and keep the brief the same everywhere.

  • Your own network: ask other owners and directors whose assistant is excellent; the best ones are often known by name
  • Job boards and LinkedIn: fast to publish, but expect a high volume of unqualified applications that you have to filter
  • A hiring consultancy: it searches and screens for you against criteria you agree at the start, so you only meet people who meet them
  • Candidates from abroad: possible when the person is willing to relocate; the employer sponsors the visa, see our guide to hiring from abroad
  • Whatever the channel, keep one written brief so every candidate hears the same scope and package

Step 4 and 5: shortlist and interview

A shortlist of three to five people is enough. More candidates slow the process and make the decision harder, not better.

  • Screen the CV for stability and growth: tenure of two years or more, increasing responsibility, a named executive they supported
  • First call of 20 minutes: availability, current package, notice period, visa status, motivation for leaving
  • Main interview of 45 to 60 minutes with you or the person they will support. Use scenario questions, not just a list of strengths; our interview questions guide has a full question bank
  • Ask for two references from people who managed them directly, and call them, see our guide to reference checks in the UAE
  • Judge chemistry honestly: an assistant spends more working hours near you than almost anyone else on the team

Step 6: a practical skills assessment

Interviews show how a person talks. A short practical task shows how the person works. Give the same task to every finalist and compare the results.

  • Email triage: 15 sample emails in your style of inbox, ask for a priority order and a short reply to the three most important ones
  • Calendar conflict: three overlapping meetings and a flight, ask what the person would move, whom they would inform and how
  • Confidentiality scenario: a colleague asks for your schedule or a document, ask how the person would respond
  • Writing sample: a short message to a client or partner on your behalf
  • Keep it to 30 to 45 minutes and tell the candidate in advance what it is. Do not use real confidential data

Step 7 and 8: the offer, the contract and probation

A clear written offer within a few days of the final interview keeps strong candidates from accepting someone else.

  • Write basic salary, allowances, bonus rules, working hours, leave and notice as separate lines in the offer and the contract
  • Agree the terms with the candidate yourself: you are the employer and you decide
  • The person is employed directly by your company; the employer sponsors the visa and handles the Emirates ID and insurance steps
  • Set a probation period in the contract and agree 30, 60 and 90 day goals in writing; see our guide to probation and notice in the UAE
  • Plan the first week: access to systems, an introduction to your key contacts, your preferences for communication and approvals

FAQ

How long does it take to hire an executive assistant in Dubai?

On your own it often takes four to eight weeks, because of the notice period of a person who is already employed (often one to three months in the contract). Through TLNT a role usually takes about two weeks to a shortlist on average, which is an average and not a promise. The notice period of the chosen person still applies.

What should I test before hiring an executive assistant?

Test the real work: prioritising an inbox, resolving a calendar conflict, writing on your behalf and handling a confidentiality situation. Use the same task for every finalist, tell them in advance, and never use real confidential data.
