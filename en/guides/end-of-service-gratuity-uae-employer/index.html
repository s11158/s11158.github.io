End-of-service gratuity in the UAE how an employer calculates and budgets it

Gratuity is the payment a private-sector employer in the UAE owes an employee at the end of employment. It is not a bonus and not optional: it grows every month the person works for you. This guide shows owners of salons, clinics and small companies who is entitled, how the amount is calculated from basic salary, worked examples in AED and how to set money aside every month so the payment does not hit cash flow.

Who is entitled

The rules below are from the UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021), which covers mainland companies and most free zones. Check your own setup with your PRO or lawyer, because a few regimes work differently.

  • A full-time employee who has completed at least one year of continuous service; under one year there is no gratuity
  • Under the 2021 law the reason for leaving, resignation or termination by the employer, does not reduce the amount the way the old law did
  • Days of unpaid leave are not counted as service
  • DIFC and ADGM have their own employment laws; in the DIFC employers pay into a workplace savings scheme instead of a gratuity
  • UAE nationals are generally covered by the pension system rather than gratuity; confirm the rules for your case
  • Since 2023 a private employer may join an approved voluntary savings scheme instead of accruing gratuity; joining is the employer's choice, check current terms with MOHRE

The formula

Gratuity is calculated from the last basic salary, not from the total package. The common practice is to divide the monthly basic salary by 30 to get one day's wage, then multiply by the number of days earned.

How to budget it every month

The simplest way to avoid a large unplanned payment is to set aside a fixed share of basic salary each month and keep it as a provision in your accounts.

  • Years 1 to 5: 21 days a year is about 5.83% of basic salary per month
  • From year 6: 30 days a year is about 8.33% of basic salary per month
  • Example: basic 8,000 AED gives a provision of about 467 AED per month in the first five years
  • Keep a list of start dates and basic salaries; update it when a salary changes, because the last basic salary is used
  • When you compare candidates, add the monthly provision to the cost of the role together with visa, health insurance and leave

Common mistakes

Most disputes about gratuity start with the contract, not with the calculation.

  • Counting gratuity on the total package: allowances such as housing and transport are not part of basic salary
  • Writing commission or bonuses vaguely: whether they count depends on how the contract describes them, so have the wording checked
  • Setting basic salary very low to reduce gratuity: candidates notice it in the offer, and it undermines trust in the job
  • Paying late: the end-of-service dues, including gratuity and unused leave, are due within 14 days after the employment ends
  • Not keeping records of unpaid leave, which is excluded from service

FAQ

How is end-of-service gratuity calculated in the UAE?

From the last basic salary: 21 days of basic salary for each of the first five years of service and 30 days for each year after that, with partial years counted pro rata. One day's wage is commonly the monthly basic divided by 30. The total cannot exceed two years' wage, and there is no gratuity under one year of service.

Does an employee get gratuity after resigning in the UAE?

Yes, if they completed at least one year of continuous service. Under the 2021 Labour Law the reason for leaving does not reduce the amount the way the old law did. DIFC, ADGM and UAE nationals follow different rules, so check those cases separately.

Is gratuity paid on basic salary or total salary?

On basic salary. Housing, transport and other allowances are not included. Whether commission counts depends on how the contract describes it, so the split between basic, allowances and commission should be clear in the offer.
