When your assistant quits the first hour, the handover and a backup plan for Dubai employers

An assistant who resigns takes with them the one thing nobody else in the business has: the full picture of your calendar, inbox, passwords, pending payments and half-finished errands. The pain is not the resignation letter, it is the Tuesday after, when a supplier calls about an invoice you have never seen and a visa renewal you did not know about is due on Friday. This guide is written for that day: what to secure in the first hour, what to collect before the person leaves, who holds the work in the meantime, how long a replacement realistically takes and how to make sure one resignation never puts your week at risk again.

The first hour: secure what only the assistant controlled

Do this on the day you receive the resignation, calmly and without accusation. A good assistant will help; a weak process is what hurts you, not the person leaving.

  • Accounts and passwords: list every account the assistant logs into on your behalf (email, calendar, bank or card portals, travel platforms, government portals, delivery apps, social media). Change the passwords you control and move the rest into a password manager that belongs to the business, not to the person
  • Two-factor codes: check which accounts send verification codes to the assistant's phone or email and move them to your number or a shared business number
  • Calendar: export or share the full calendar to your account and ask for a written list of every commitment in the next 30 days with the contact person for each
  • Pending payments: ask for a list of invoices awaiting approval, standing orders, renewals (trade licence, visas, insurance, car, school, memberships) and anything paid from a company or personal card the assistant holds. Collect the card
  • Keys, access cards, SIM cards, laptop, company phone: write a one-page list and agree the return date
  • People: tell the two or three contacts who deal with the assistant daily (your team lead, your accountant, your family) whom to call from now on

The first week: handover notes that actually help

Most handover documents are too long to read and miss the one detail that matters. Ask for the practical version and review it together while the person is still here.

  • Open loops: every task not finished, with the next step, the deadline and the person waiting for it
  • Recurring tasks: what happens daily, weekly, monthly and yearly, with the date of the next occurrence (renewals, payroll inputs, school fees, car registration, insurance)
  • Contacts: suppliers, drivers, doctors, schools, PRO, bank manager, with phone numbers and what each contact is for
  • Your preferences: how you like meetings scheduled, which emails you want to see, how you travel, what you never agree to. This is the part a new assistant needs most
  • Where things live: folders, shared drives, the filing system for receipts and documents
  • Do the handover in two sessions: one for the assistant to write, one for you to ask questions. Thirty minutes each is usually enough

Interim cover: who holds the work meanwhile

Nobody can take over the whole role tomorrow, so split it. The goal for the gap is that nothing expires and nothing important is missed, not that everything runs as before.

  • Calendar and inbox: take them back yourself for the gap, but only at two fixed times a day; a shared inbox rule with your team lead for anything urgent
  • Payments and renewals: hand the list to your accountant or office manager with the dates, and ask for a weekly reminder
  • Household and family logistics: the housekeeper, driver or a family member takes the fixed items (school runs, deliveries); book the rest in advance for the month
  • A remote assistant for a few weeks: a virtual assistant can take email triage, bookings and reminders quickly; our guide to virtual vs in-house assistants explains what fits remote work and what does not
  • If the assistant works a notice period, agree in writing what they finish and what they hand to you; a departing assistant who still owns tasks is the most common source of missed deadlines
  • Write down what hurt most during the gap: that list becomes the brief for the next hire

How long a replacement takes, and how to run the search

Plan for the realistic timeline, not the hopeful one. The search and the new person's notice period are two separate clocks.

  • Search to shortlist: on your own, usually several weeks of posting, filtering and interviewing while you still do the assistant's job. Through TLNT a role usually takes about two weeks to a shortlist on average, which is an average and not a promise
  • Notice period of the person you choose: often one to three months if they are employed in the UAE, sometimes immediately available if they are between jobs or on a flexible visa. Ask on the first call
  • Use the gap to rewrite the brief: what the role really was, what you want to add or remove, the real working hours, the access level. Our job description guide has a template
  • Set the budget from the market, not from the last salary: market estimate for a personal assistant about 5,000 - 10,000 AED per month, an executive assistant about 12,000 - 20,000, a hybrid multi-assistant covering business, personal and household tasks about 10,000 - 18,000. Total package, plus visa, insurance and gratuity provision paid by the employer
  • Test finalists on your real pain: give the same practical task to each (email triage, a calendar conflict, a renewal with a deadline) and compare
  • Overlap if you can: even three days with the old and the new assistant together saves weeks of guessing

How to never depend on one person again

The resignation hurt because knowledge lived in one head. Change that once and the next change of assistant becomes an ordinary event.

  • Business-owned accounts: every login in a company password manager, every two-factor code on a business number, every subscription on a company card. Access is granted to the person, not created by the person
  • A living handover file: a shared document the assistant updates every Friday in ten minutes (open loops, next renewals, key contacts). You read it once a month
  • A renewals calendar: one shared calendar with every licence, visa, insurance, vehicle and school date, visible to you and your accountant
  • A second pair of hands: an office manager, a remote assistant for a few hours a week, or a team member who knows the basics. One person on holiday or on notice should not stop the business
  • Written rules: how to approve a payment, what the assistant can decide alone, whom to call if you are unreachable. Rules make a replacement faster to onboard
  • Treat the assistant well while they are here: clear scope, boundaries and growth; our guide on why assistants leave covers the reasons that are in the employer's hands

FAQ

My assistant resigned. What should I do first?

In the first hour secure what only the assistant controlled: change or move passwords into a business password manager, move two-factor codes to your number, get the full calendar and a list of pending payments and renewals, and collect cards, keys and devices. Then ask for a short handover file with open loops, recurring tasks, contacts and your preferences, and review it together.

How long does it take to replace an assistant in Dubai?

Two clocks run in parallel: the search and the new person's notice period. On your own the search often takes several weeks. Through TLNT a role usually takes about two weeks to a shortlist on average, which is an average and not a promise.
