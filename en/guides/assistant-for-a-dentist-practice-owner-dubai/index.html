Assistant for a dentist who owns the practice in Dubai what to hand over when you still treat patients all day

An owner-dentist in Dubai has the hardest version of the owner's problem: the chair pays the bills, so the business work happens between patients and after the last one leaves. Lab cases, implant kits, the hygienist's licence renewal, an insurance query, the marketing agency waiting for approval, a report that nobody has built. This guide is about an assistant for the owner, not a clinic manager and not a treatment coordinator: what hurts without one, what to delegate, which type fits and what it costs, how to check the person and where the boundaries are.

Talk to us

A typical week without an assistant

Two to four chairs, you and one or two associates, a hygienist, two nurses and a receptionist. The practice is full, which is exactly why the owner's work is late.

  • Monday: the lab calls about a crown shade while you are mid-procedure; the receptionist does not know the case, so the call waits until you do
  • Tuesday: the implant kit for Thursday was never ordered because the supplier email sat in your inbox; you pay for express delivery or move the patient
  • Wednesday: the hygienist mentions that her licence expires next month; nobody tracked it, and the clinic's own licence and malpractice insurance renew in the same quarter
  • Thursday: the insurance officer needs three documents for a pre-approval, the marketing agency has waited five days for a yes on two posts, and a patient wants to speak to the owner about an invoice
  • Friday: you still do not know this month's production per chair or the lab spend; the accountant asks for supplier invoices you have in WhatsApp photos
  • Weekend: your own CME course registration, a dental congress abstract, the children's dentist (yes, another one), and a car service you have postponed twice

What the assistant takes over

Administrative and coordination work only. Treatment, treatment plan explanations and anything the regulator reserves for licensed staff stay with you, your associates and the treatment coordinator. Agree the three lists in writing.

  • Business: suppliers and lab: orders from a par-level list you approve, lab case tracker with due dates and shade notes, delivery confirmations, price comparisons for two or three suppliers, invoices collected for the accountant
  • Business: the licence calendar of the practice and the team: facility licence, each clinician's licence and CME credits, malpractice insurance, municipality and civil defence inspection dates, staff visas and Emirates IDs; the assistant tracks and prepares the file, the responsible person submits, see our compliance calendar guide
  • Business: insurance paperwork support: documents gathered for pre-approvals and claims, status of rejected claims chased with the insurance officer; coding and clinical justification stay with the clinician and the officer
  • Business: marketing coordination: a content calendar, approvals collected from you in one batch, photos with written consent, review reply drafts, all within the regulator's advertising rules
  • Business: reports: a one-page weekly summary from the practice software, bookings, no-shows, production per chair, lab spend, outstanding claims, open complaints
  • Personal: your own licence and CME, congresses and courses, personal insurance and paperwork, personal travel
  • Household: schools, home maintenance, family travel, drivers and nannies coordinated, if you want one person for all of it
  • Keep to yourself: clinical decisions and treatment plans; patient records; signing contracts and payroll; hiring and dismissals; refunds and complaint outcomes; bank approvals

Which type fits and what it costs

The owner's assistant is not a clinic manager. A clinic manager runs the daily operation and the staff; a dental treatment coordinator works with patients and plans; the owner's assistant works for you. One assistant is enough for a practice with up to two or three clinicians. With four or more clinicians or a second branch you usually need a clinic manager for operations plus an assistant for the owner. How to find and check

Good candidates are assistants of other clinic or salon owners, dental or medical receptionists who are organised and want to leave the front desk, and office administrators from supplier or dental lab companies who already know the vocabulary. Check on tasks.

  • Lab case tracker: give a messy WhatsApp thread with six cases and ask for a table with patient initials, lab, due date, status and what to chase today
  • Supplier dispute: a wrong delivery and an overdue invoice in one email; the candidate writes the reply and tells you what they would check in the order history first
  • Licence calendar: a list of six team members with licence, insurance and visa dates; ask for a calendar with reminder points and a note on which renewals you must sign yourself
  • Profession-specific task: book your trip to a dental congress abroad in a week when the hygienist's licence renewal and a municipality inspection fall; ask what they would move and who covers
  • Red flags: talks about previous employers' patients by name; no habit of written confirmations with suppliers; wants purchasing and payment in one pair of hands; no Excel or practice software skills at all
  • Probation plan: month one, suppliers and lab in reporting mode; month two, licence calendar and the weekly report; month three, insurance paperwork support and marketing coordination; written scope by day 90

Risks and boundaries

The owner's assistant sits next to patient data, supplier money and your staff's personal documents. The boundaries below protect you and the assistant.

  • Patient data: access to the practice software limited to administrative modules; no exports, no forwarding of patient files, a written confidentiality clause and NDA, see our patient data guide
  • Purchasing: two quotes above an agreed amount, orders approved by you, payments made by you or the accountant; the person who orders should not be the person who pays
  • Money: a card with a monthly limit for small items, receipts attached to every line, a monthly reconciliation you read
  • Staff documents: passports, visas and contracts in a locked folder with access on a need-to-know basis; HR decisions are never discussed with the assistant in front of the team
  • Dependence: one shared calendar and one tracker that you can open without them; supplier and lab contacts documented; a handover note updated monthly
  • Backup: during the assistant's leave the receptionist covers supplier confirmations only, and you keep the licence calendar; agree it in advance

FAQ

Does a dentist who owns a practice in Dubai need an assistant?

If you treat patients most of the day and also run the practice, yes, usually before you need a clinic manager. The signs: supplier and lab issues reach you in the chair, a staff licence or insurance renewal has slipped, the marketing agency waits days for approvals, and you cannot say this month's production per chair without asking the accountant.
