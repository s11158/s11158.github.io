Russian-speaking assistant in Dubai when it makes sense and how to hire

Many owners in Dubai run businesses where part of the clients, suppliers or family communication is in Russian. An assistant who is fluent in Russian and works in business English can take a lot of daily friction off the owner. But language alone does not make a good assistant. This guide explains when a Russian-speaking assistant makes sense, what English and Arabic level to ask for, how to verify the language before the offer and how to onboard the person.

When a Russian-speaking assistant makes sense

Language is a tool for the job, not the job itself. It helps most in these situations.

  • A large share of your clients, patients or guests speak Russian and write to you in WhatsApp or Telegram
  • You work with suppliers, partners or advisers in Russian-speaking countries and need calls and documents handled without you
  • The owner or the family is Russian-speaking and wants private matters, schools, bookings and paperwork handled in the language they think in
  • You manage a Russian-speaking team and want one person who can brief, follow up and translate instructions precisely
  • It matters less if your clients are almost all English or Arabic speaking; then ask for Russian as a bonus, not as a requirement

English and Arabic: what level to ask for

Even for a Russian-speaking assistant, English is the working language of Dubai. Decide the level by the tasks, not by a certificate.

  • English at business level is a must: emails to landlords, banks, suppliers and authorities, calls and meeting notes
  • Arabic is a plus for government contacts and Arabic-speaking partners; if the role includes front desk or PRO support, it can become important
  • Written English matters more for an assistant than accent; read a real email the person wrote
  • Write the languages in the vacancy as skills with a level, for example "Russian-speaking, fluent Russian; business English"
  • Language is a skill, not a nationality: do not exclude or favour people by origin, look at what they can do

How to verify the language

A CV line "fluent Russian and English" proves nothing. Check on real tasks.

  • Hold part of the interview in each language the job needs and switch mid-conversation to see how the person copes
  • Give a short written task in each language: reply to a client complaint, write a meeting summary, confirm a booking
  • Ask the person to translate a short real passage from your business, with a term or two that only someone who works in your field would know
  • Run a phone call test: a supplier asks to move a delivery and the assistant has to agree a new date
  • Check references on communication: how the previous employer used the person in each language

Onboarding a Russian-speaking assistant

The first month decides whether the person becomes your right hand or a messenger. A simple plan works.

  • Week 1: access to calendar, email, shared files and WhatsApp business numbers, with rules on what is shared
  • Week 1: a list of recurring tasks, who the key contacts are and which messages are urgent
  • Weeks 2-3: the assistant handles a part of the diary and drafts replies for your approval
  • Week 4: review what worked, move more tasks over and agree reporting, for example a short daily or weekly summary
  • Write down confidentiality rules and decide which accounts and documents stay with you

FAQ

Where can I find a Russian-speaking assistant in Dubai?

Through your own network, professional communities, job boards or a hiring consultancy. Whatever the channel, test the language on real tasks and check references, because a CV line about fluency does not show how the person writes to a client or speaks to a supplier.

Do I need Arabic for an assistant in Dubai?

Not always. English at business level is the working base. Arabic helps with government contacts and Arabic-speaking partners and becomes more important if the role includes front desk or PRO support. Decide by the tasks.
