Competitor analysis for a small business in the UAE: a practical two-week method

Owners usually know their competitors by name and by Instagram. Far fewer know how fast those competitors answer a WhatsApp message, what they say about price, or what their clients complain about. This method takes about two weeks of part-time work and gives facts you can act on: where you lose clients, where you can stand out and what to do in the next 90 days.

Choose 5-10 competitors

The plan is simple: week one is for collecting facts, week two for comparing them, finding the gaps and writing the plan.

Choose competitors the way your client does, not by size. Search Google Maps and Instagram for your main service in your area, note who your clients mention, and see whose ads appear when you search. A useful list has three kinds:

  • Direct: same service, same area, similar price - the ones clients compare you with
  • Aspirational: a step above in price or brand, where clients go when they want more
  • Cheaper: where price-sensitive clients go; they show the floor of the market
  • If your clients book there, one or two online-first players or home-service apps
  • Keep it to 5-10 names: more turns the work into a catalogue that nobody reads

Mystery shopping by WhatsApp and phone

This is the most useful part, and the one owners skip. Contact each competitor the way a client would: a WhatsApp message, an Instagram direct message and a call. Use the same scenario everywhere, for example a question about the price of the main service and a free slot this week. Ask questions and stop before paying; do not book slots you will not use.

Often the biggest gap is not the price but the speed and quality of the first answer. Record in a table:

  • Time to the first reply in each channel, in working hours and in the evening
  • Who replies: a person, a bot or an auto-message, and in which language
  • Whether they asked you questions or just sent a price list
  • How they answer the price question and the objection that it is expensive
  • Whether they offered a specific time and asked you to book
  • Follow-up: did anyone write again the next day or later, and how

Price matrix and Google Maps reviews

Build one table: rows are competitors, columns are your 5-7 key services. Compare the price for the same thing, not the headline price: what is included, duration, level of the master or doctor, packages and memberships, first-visit offers. Mark where prices are given only on request; that is a fact too.

Then read the Google Maps reviews: the latest 30-50 for each competitor and every negative review in full. Sort what clients write into topics, praise and complaints separately. Look also at whether and how the owner replies.

  • Praise: what clients value enough to write about - a specific master, cleanliness, punctuality, the result, the atmosphere
  • Complaints: waiting, a rude reception, a price different from the one quoted, pushy selling, the result
  • The same words at several competitors: these are market expectations, not a difference
  • Complaints that repeat everywhere: an opening for you, if you can visibly solve that problem
  • Your own reviews, read with the same method

What to check, where, and what it tells you

Content and ads show what competitors want to be known for and where they spend money. Both can be checked with public tools. The Meta Ad Library shows a page's active ads on Facebook and Instagram; the Google Ads Transparency Center shows ads by advertiser and region. In search, check who appears for your main queries on Google and Google Maps, and whether a client can book on a competitor's website in a few taps.

From findings to positioning and a 90-day plan

Facts without decisions are a report that sits in a folder. In the second week, put everything on one page: where you are stronger, where you are weaker, and which client need nobody covers well. Then choose a positioning you can prove in one sentence: who you are for, what you do better and why the client should believe it. It should rest on something real, such as response speed, a specific expertise, a language or a service format.

The 90-day plan turns this into actions with an owner and a date: usually response speed and scripts first, then prices and packages, then content and ads. Measure the same things you measured at competitors, so you can repeat the check in three to six months.

What the owner should receive at the end:

  • A list of competitors with the reason each one is on it
  • A mystery shopping table: response times, quotes from the answers, follow-up
  • A price and packages matrix for your key services
  • A review summary: the main praise and the main complaints in the market
  • An overview of content and ads, with examples
  • A one-page positioning and a 90-day plan with owners and dates

FAQ

How do I do a competitor analysis for a small business?

Pick 5-10 competitors your clients actually compare you with, contact each one as a client by WhatsApp and phone, build a price and packages matrix, read their Google Maps reviews, check their content and ads in the Meta Ad Library and the Google Ads Transparency Center, then turn the gaps into a positioning and a 90-day plan. It takes about two weeks of part-time work.

How can I see what ads my competitors are running?

Use two free public tools: the Meta Ad Library for active ads on Facebook and Instagram, and the Google Ads Transparency Center for Google ads by advertiser and region. They show the creative and the offer, not the budget or the results.

How often should a small business repeat a competitor analysis?

A full analysis once or twice a year is usually enough, plus a short check every quarter: response speed by mystery shopping, prices of the key services and new reviews. Repeat it earlier if a strong new competitor opens nearby or your leads drop.
