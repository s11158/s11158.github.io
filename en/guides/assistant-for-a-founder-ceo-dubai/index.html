Assistant for a founder or CEO in Dubai inbox, board, investors, PRO and the EA versus chief of staff question

A founder of a company with 10 to 150 people in Dubai usually hires an assistant too late: after the trade licence renewal was noticed by the bank, after an investor update went out three weeks behind, after a week of meetings booked back to back across two emirates. This guide is for the founder or CEO who still runs the company hands-on: what a week without an assistant looks like, what an executive assistant takes over in business, personal and household tasks, whether you need an EA or a chief of staff, what it costs, how to check candidates and where the boundaries are.

A typical week without an assistant

You are the bottleneck and everyone knows it. Not because you are slow, but because every small decision and every booking waits for the same person.

  • Monday: 200 unread emails, three of them from a bank and a free zone authority; you answer the fastest ones, not the important ones
  • Tuesday: a board call ends with eleven action items in four heads; nobody writes them down, so the next call starts with the same points
  • Wednesday: the PRO service asks for two documents for a new hire's visa; the establishment card renewal is due the same month and nobody has a list of what expires when
  • Thursday: two investor meetings and a supplier negotiation booked in Abu Dhabi and Dubai on the same afternoon; the driver finds out at eleven
  • Friday: the investor update is late again because the numbers sit in three spreadsheets; the monthly update becomes a quarterly one
  • Every week: your own visa, car, insurance and school papers handled from the car; a late fee paid; the one call you promised to return never made

What the assistant takes over

An executive assistant works on your behalf inside limits you set. Decisions stay with you. Split the tasks into business, personal and household and write them down in the first month.

  • Business: inbox triage with a daily shortlist, drafts of replies in your voice, follow-ups scheduled so promises are kept
  • Business: calendar with real travel times between emirates, buffers before investor meetings, a weekly plan on Sunday and a daily brief in the morning
  • Business: board and investor follow-ups: action lists sent within an hour of each call, owners and dates chased, the investor update assembled from the team's numbers for you to edit and send, the data room kept tidy
  • Business: travel: flights, hotels, visas, agendas, expense reports; a travel folder per trip
  • Business: PRO and licence coordination: a renewal calendar for the trade licence, establishment card, lease, insurance and staff visas; documents collected and the PRO officer or PRO service chased; submissions and signatures stay with the authorised person, see our PRO officer page
  • Business: meeting notes and a decisions log, so that what you agreed is findable a month later
  • Personal: your own visa and Emirates ID renewals, car, insurance, bank paperwork, medical appointments, gifts and personal travel
  • Household: school admin, home maintenance, household staff coordinated with the house manager or directly, family travel documents
  • Keep to yourself: decisions and commitments to investors; signing; money transfers and payroll approval; salaries, dismissals, equity and other confidential HR; anything you would not want read out in a board meeting

Which type fits and what it costs

The common confusion is EA versus chief of staff. An executive assistant runs your time, communication and logistics. A chief of staff runs projects and meetings on your behalf, prepares decisions and sometimes manages people; they do not book flights. Up to about 50 people one strong EA is enough. During fundraising, a merger or fast growth beyond that, founders often add a chief of staff and keep the EA. Market estimate, AED per month, total package (basic salary plus allowances); the employer also pays visa, health insurance and a gratuity provision.

How to find and check

The best EAs come from other founders and executives, from hotel and airline operations where people are used to pressure and precision, and from office managers who have outgrown the office. Interview on tasks, not on charm.

  • Inbox triage: 30 real anonymised emails, 40 minutes; ask for a shortlist of five that need you today and the drafts of two replies
  • Calendar conflict: a board member, an investor and a key client all want Thursday afternoon, one of them in Abu Dhabi; ask for the proposal and the three messages
  • Follow-up discipline: give a 20-minute meeting recording or transcript and ask for the action list with owners and dates, then ask what they would do on day three when two owners are silent
  • Profession-specific task: plan a week of investor meetings in London that overlaps the trade licence renewal deadline and a staff visa medical; what gets delegated, to whom, and what you must sign before you fly
  • Red flags: tells stories about a previous boss's finances or private life; cannot give a written sample; answers "it depends" to every question about systems; has never kept a decisions log or a renewal calendar
  • Probation plan: month one, inbox and calendar in draft mode; month two, travel, board follow-ups and the renewal calendar; month three, investor update assembly and personal admin; written scope and limits by day 90, see our executive assistant hiring guide

Risks and boundaries

Your EA will know your cap table, your bank balance, your lawyer and your family's schedule. Design the access before day one.

  • Confidentiality in the employment contract and a separate NDA covering investors, employees, finances and family
  • Accounts: delegate access to inbox and calendar, a company card with a limit, viewer access to the data room; no personal passwords, no banking tokens, no authority to approve payroll
  • Signing and commitments: nothing is signed or promised on your behalf without a written rule; the EA drafts, you decide
  • HR: salaries and dismissals are handled with the HR manager, not through the assistant's inbox
  • Dependence: a shared renewal calendar, a decisions log and a contact book that live in company systems, not on the assistant's phone
  • Backup: an office manager or a second assistant who can hold the calendar for two weeks; agree it before the first leave

FAQ

Does a founder or CEO in Dubai need an executive assistant?

If you run a company with more than about ten people, travel, report to investors or a board and still handle your own inbox, calendar and licence renewals, yes. The usual signs: emails from banks and authorities answered late, action items lost between meetings, an investor update always behind, personal paperwork done from the car.

How much does an executive assistant for a CEO cost in Dubai?

Market estimate: an executive assistant 12,000 - 20,000 AED per month, 20,000 - 30,000 for a C-level or group role; a chief of staff, which is a different job, 25,000 - 40,000 as an orientation; a hybrid assistant for business, personal and household 10,000 - 18,000, senior 18,000 - 25,000.
