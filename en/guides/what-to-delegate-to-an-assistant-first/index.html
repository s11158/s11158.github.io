What to delegate to an assistant first a ladder for week 1, month 1 and month 3

The most common reason a new assistant does not work out is not the person. It is that the owner hands over either nothing ("I will explain later") or everything at once ("just take care of it"). This guide gives a delegation ladder: what to hand over in the first week, the first month and by month three, separate lists for business, personal and household tasks, what should never be delegated and how to write a task so it comes back done.

The delegation ladder

Hand over tasks in the order of low risk and high volume first. Each step adds trust and access; do not skip steps because the person seems good.

Business tasks to hand over

These are the tasks that eat an owner's day and do not need the owner's judgement. Start from the top of the list.

  • Calendar: scheduling, moving and confirming meetings, blocking focus time, flagging conflicts before they happen
  • Inbox: sorting into act, read and ignore; drafting replies for approval; a short daily digest of what needs you
  • Follow-ups: notes after every meeting with the next step and the date, sent the same day; a list of open promises, yours and other people's
  • Documents: one folder structure, scanned copies of licences, contracts, visas and insurance, with expiry dates in a tracker
  • Suppliers: quotes compared in one table, orders within an agreed limit, delivery confirmations, invoice collection for the accountant
  • Travel: flights, hotels, visas where needed, one itinerary with addresses and contacts, and a plan B for a cancelled flight
  • Team coordination: collecting updates, chasing deadlines, booking rooms and calls; the assistant chases, you decide

Personal and household tasks to hand over

If you want one person for business and private life, write the private list separately and decide what the assistant may see. For the full question of one hybrid person versus several, see our guide on one assistant or several.

  • Personal: medical and dental appointments, car service and registration, bank and government appointments, gifts and reservations, personal subscriptions and renewals
  • Family: school calendar and forms, activities and tutors, travel documents for the family, a shared family calendar maintained by the assistant
  • Household: the house staff schedule, maintenance and repairs, utility and service payments prepared for approval, deliveries, inventory of what the house needs each month
  • Property: tenancy renewals, service charges, a contractor list, insurance renewals, a maintenance log for each property
  • Keep medical details, children's data and financial statements on a need-to-know basis; share what is needed for the task, not the whole picture
  • If the household side is large, a house manager may be the better fit than one hybrid person; the roles and costs are compared in the guide above

What not to delegate

However good the person is, some things stay with you. Write this list down and give it to the assistant on day one.

  • Signing anything that creates a legal commitment: contracts, bank forms, powers of attorney
  • Money transfers and card payments without a written rule: amount limit, approved payees, approval in writing before payment
  • Hiring, dismissals, salary decisions and anything from confidential HR or a personal conflict in the team
  • Clinical, legal, tax or investment advice to your clients or to your family; the assistant prepares the file, the licensed person decides
  • Relationships that are yours: a key client, an investor, a regulator; the assistant schedules and prepares, you talk
  • Passwords to your personal accounts; use delegate access and a password manager, see our guide on confidentiality, NDA and access

How to write a task so it comes back done

A task that comes back half done usually went out half written. Use the same five lines every time; after a month the assistant will fill them in for you.

  • Result: what "done" looks like in one sentence, for example "three hotel options with price and distance to the venue in one message"
  • Deadline: a date and time, not "when you can"; and say whether it is urgent compared with the other open tasks
  • Limits: what the assistant may decide alone, for example "book if under 1,500 AED per night, otherwise ask"
  • Context: why the task matters and who else is involved, so the assistant can judge the small decisions
  • Format: how you want the answer, for example a table, a one-line WhatsApp message or a calendar entry
  • Then stop interfering: one check-in at the midpoint for a long task, not a message every hour

FAQ

What should I delegate to an assistant first?

Start with the calendar, meeting confirmations, document filing and supplier reminders in week one, with every outgoing message approved by you. Add inbox triage with draft replies, bookings, travel and payments prepared for approval in weeks two to four. By month three the assistant should own the calendar, inbox routine, renewals, travel and household coordination.

Can one assistant handle business and personal tasks?

Yes, up to a point. A hybrid multi-assistant can run the business calendar, your personal errands and the household coordination if the lists are written separately and the private information is shared on a need-to-know basis. When the household has several staff or properties, a house manager next to a business assistant usually works better; the market estimate for a hybrid person is 10,000 - 18,000 AED per month, orientation.
