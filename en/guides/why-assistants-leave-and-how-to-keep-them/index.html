Why assistants leave and what keeps a good one in Dubai

Employers usually hear the polite reason: a better offer, family circumstances, a move. The real reason is almost always older than the offer. The assistant has been doing a bigger job than the one that was agreed, answering messages at midnight, guessing what matters today and hearing about mistakes but never about what went well. By the time the resignation arrives, the decision was made months earlier. This guide names the reasons honestly and shows what an employer can change: rules, a regular review, a visible path and pay steps that follow the market, not the mood.

The real reasons, named plainly

None of these is about a bad person. They are about how the role was run. Most employers recognise at least two of them.

  • Scope creep without pay: the role started as calendar and inbox and now includes the household, the children's school, two side businesses and the owner's parents, at the same salary
  • No boundaries at night and at weekends: every message expects an answer within minutes, holidays are interrupted, and nothing in the contract says so
  • No growth: three years of the same tasks, no new responsibility, no training, no change in title or package
  • Disrespect: being shouted at, being blamed in front of others, being treated as invisible, or having private information shared casually
  • Unclear priorities: ten urgent tasks a day with no ranking, decisions reversed without explanation, the assistant blamed for choosing wrongly
  • No access to the decision maker: the assistant is expected to speak for you but does not know what you think; every answer has to be checked twice
  • Pay that fell behind: the market moved, the person's skills grew, the salary stayed. A good assistant knows the numbers in our assistant salary guide better than most employers

The early signs you can still act on

Resignations are rarely sudden. These signals usually appear months before, and each one still has an answer.

  • Replies become correct but short; initiative disappears; the person stops suggesting improvements
  • Sick days and late starts after a period of late-night messages
  • The assistant asks what the role will look like next year, or asks about a title, and gets no clear answer
  • Mistakes in areas that were always reliable, usually a sign of overload rather than carelessness
  • The person updates their CV or LinkedIn profile; you may not see it, but a direct question asked kindly often gets an honest answer
  • What to do: one honest conversation about scope, hours, pay and the next twelve months, with notes, and a change within a month. Words without a change make it worse

Rules and boundaries that keep a good assistant

Rules protect both sides. The assistant knows what is expected; you stop paying for guesses.

  • A written scope: what is in the role, what is occasionally in it and what is not. Review it twice a year and change the pay when the scope changes
  • Hours and reachability: the normal working hours, the window for urgent messages, what counts as urgent, and real days off. If you need evening cover, say so in the contract and pay for it
  • Priorities: a ten-minute morning call or message with the three things that matter today. Everything else waits unless you say otherwise
  • Decision rights: what the assistant can decide alone (a booking under a certain amount, a meeting move within a day), what needs a quick yes, what is never theirs to decide
  • Respect in practice: feedback in private, thanks in public, no sharing of the assistant's private matters, no blame for a decision you reversed
  • Confidentiality works both ways: the assistant protects your information and you protect theirs, including their salary and personal circumstances

Review, growth and pay steps

A good assistant does not expect a promotion every year. They expect to be seen, to learn something and to be paid in line with the market as their value grows.

  • A short review every quarter: what worked, what to change, one thing the person wants to learn. Fifteen minutes with notes is enough
  • Growth inside the role: project follow-up, vendor negotiation, preparing board or investor materials, supervising household staff, a second language course. Each adds value to you and to the person's CV
  • A path that is visible: assistant to senior assistant, to executive assistant, to chief of staff, house manager or office manager, depending on the person. Say which path is realistic in your business
  • Pay steps from the market, market estimate in AED per month, total package: administrative assistant 5,000 - 8,000, senior 8,000 - 11,000; personal assistant 5,000 - 10,000, senior 12,000 and above; executive assistant 12,000 - 20,000, EA to a C-level executive or a private family office 20,000 - 30,000; hybrid multi-assistant 10,000 - 18,000, senior 18,000 - 25,000. The employer also pays visa, health insurance and the gratuity provision
  • When the scope grows into a different role, pay for the different role. An assistant who runs your business calendar, your household and two companies is a hybrid multi-assistant or an EA, not an administrative assistant at an administrative salary
  • A bonus tied to a few observable goals (no missed renewals, follow-ups closed on time, your quarterly feedback) works better than a surprise envelope

When letting go is the right answer

Keeping someone is not always right. The question is whether the problems are about how the role is run or about the fit.

  • Fix the role first: if the scope, hours and pay are unclear, no replacement will do better until they are fixed
  • Fit problems are different: repeated careless errors after clear feedback, breaches of confidentiality, or a pattern of unreliability. Document them, give specific feedback and a timeline, and decide
  • Part respectfully: notice as per the contract and the current UAE labour rules, a clean handover, a reference if deserved. Dubai is a small city; the way you let people go is known
  • Before you search again, write down what you learned about the role; our guide on hiring an executive assistant and our why staff quit guide cover the general picture

FAQ

Why do executive and personal assistants quit in Dubai?

The most common reasons are in the employer's hands: the scope grew without a change in pay, no boundaries on evening and weekend messages, no growth or training, disrespect in daily communication, and unclear priorities that make the assistant guess and then take the blame. A better offer is usually the last step, not the cause.

How much should I raise my assistant's salary?

Compare the current scope with the market, not with last year's figure. Market estimate in AED per month: personal assistant about 5,000 - 10,000, senior 12,000 and above; executive assistant about 12,000 - 20,000, 20,000 - 30,000 for a C-level or family office role; hybrid multi-assistant about 10,000 - 18,000.
