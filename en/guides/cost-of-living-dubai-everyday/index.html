Cost of living in Dubai: everyday prices groceries, transport, eating out, phone, gym and fun, with the places where it costs less

The sentence "Dubai is expensive" usually comes from a tourist week: a hotel breakfast, taxis everywhere, a beach club, dinner with a view. A resident's Dubai is a different city, with budget supermarkets, a cheap and clean metro, free beaches, cafeterias where a full meal costs what a coffee costs downtown, and the same brands as at home at comparable prices. Everything is available here; what you pay depends on whether you know the places. This guide walks through the everyday lines of a newcomer's budget, gives orientation ranges for autumn 2026 and, for each line, the legitimate ways residents spend less. Check current prices yourself with the methods at the end; your month will differ from any example.

Groceries: the store tier decides the bill

Dubai has every tier of supermarket, often within the same block. The same weekly basket can differ by half between tiers, which is why "groceries are expensive" and "groceries are cheap" are both said by honest people. Orientation, autumn 2026, check current prices.

  • Budget and local tier: Union Coop, Nesto, Viva, Al Maya, local cooperative societies and the fruit and vegetable sections of hypermarkets; local and regional produce, large packs, weekly deals; a single person cooking at home spends roughly 800 to 1,200 a month here
  • Mid tier: Carrefour and Lulu, the two big hypermarket chains, with both imported and regional brands, strong online delivery and loyalty apps; roughly 1,000 to 1,500 a month for one person
  • Premium tier: Spinneys, Waitrose, Choithrams, organic and specialty stores; imported European brands, ready meals, premium meat and fish; roughly 1,500 to 2,500 for the same basket
  • What costs more than at home for most newcomers: imported dairy and cheese, alcohol (sold only in licensed shops and venues), some fresh berries; what costs the same or less: rice, chicken, local vegetables, eggs, bread, regional fruit, nuts and spices
  • Legitimate ways to pay less: shop the hypermarket app deals, buy fruit and vegetables at Waterfront Market or a local cooperative, use store brands, cook rice and chicken-based meals, and skip bottled water by using a filter or large refill bottles
  • How to check: build your real weekly list in the Carrefour and Lulu apps before you move; the total times four is your grocery line, with no guessing

Eating out: from cafeteria to rooftop

Dubai eats out a lot, and the price range is one of the widest of any city: the same evening can cost 20 AED or 2,000. The resident's trick is knowing that the cheap end is excellent, not a compromise. Orientation, autumn 2026, check current prices.

  • Cafeterias and street food: a shawarma, a plate of biryani, a dosa or a karak tea with a sandwich at 10 to 30 AED; Karama, Al Rigga, Satwa and Deira are full of them, and they are where many residents eat lunch
  • Casual chains and food courts: 35 to 70 per person for a meal; malls have food courts at this level everywhere
  • Mid-range restaurants: 80 to 150 per person without alcohol; this is where most birthday dinners happen
  • Fine dining, hotel restaurants, beach clubs: 300 and above per person; the postcard, for occasions
  • Happy hours and ladies' nights as a concept: many hotel bars and restaurants have weekday hours with reduced prices on drinks and food; brunch on weekends is a Dubai institution at every price level; the Entertainer-type discount apps and bank card offers cut restaurant bills noticeably
  • Coffee: 15 to 30 for a specialty coffee, 5 to 10 at a cafeteria or a petrol station; delivery apps (Talabat, Deliveroo, Careem) are cheap to use but the habit adds up fast

Transport: metro, bus, taxi, car

The metro and buses in Dubai are clean, air-conditioned, safe and inexpensive; the only question is whether your home and your workplace are on the line. Taxis are affordable by big-city standards and a car is a choice, not a necessity, in the first year. Orientation, autumn 2026, check current prices.

  • Metro and tram with a Nol card: fares by zones, a single ride roughly 3 to 8 AED; a monthly unlimited pass costs a few hundred AED and pays off from about ten working days of commuting; check the RTA app for current fares
  • Buses: the same Nol card, very cheap, slower; they cover areas the metro does not reach (JVC, International City, Discovery Gardens, Silicon Oasis)
  • Taxi (RTA taxis, Careem, Uber, Hala): a short 10 to 15 minute ride roughly 15 to 30 AED, across the city 60 to 100; a safe fallback at night and a budget killer if used daily
  • Car: a used car can be bought for a few thousand AED upward and a rental from roughly 1,500 a month; add insurance, registration, fuel (cheap compared with Europe), Salik toll gates, and parking; most newcomers wait a few months before deciding
  • Walking and cycling: realistic from November to March, almost not from June to September; plan the summer commute with air conditioning in mind
  • Rule of thumb for a budget: live near the metro or near work and keep transport at 200 to 400 a month; live far from both and the taxi line alone can reach 1,500

Phone, internet, gym, beauty, entertainment

The lines people forget until the first month's statement. None of them is expensive if chosen consciously. Orientation, autumn 2026, check current prices.

  • Mobile: prepaid plans from the two operators from roughly 50 to 150 a month with enough data for maps and messaging; eSIM and tourist plans exist for the first days
  • Home internet: 300 to 400 a month when you pay it yourself; often included in rooms and furnished rentals, so ask before adding this line
  • Gym: 150 to 400 a month for a chain gym, less with annual payment; many residential buildings have a gym and a pool included in the rent, and the beach, parks and the running tracks along the Canal and at Kite Beach are free
  • Beauty and personal care: a men's haircut 30 to 80 at a neighbourhood barber, a manicure 50 to 120 at a neighbourhood salon, more in malls and hotels; the beauty industry is large, so quality at the neighbourhood level is high and competition keeps prices honest
  • Entertainment: public beaches free, cinema 40 to 60, museums and parks 20 to 100, desert and sea trips from 100 to 300 with a group; the weekly costly habit is not the activity but the taxi and the dinner around it
  • Healthcare: covered by the employer's health insurance for the employee; co-payments and the network of clinics depend on the plan, so ask HR which clinics are in your network before you need one

How to check prices yourself

Every number above will be out of date by the time you read it in some direction. The method does not age.

  • Groceries: your real weekly list in the Carrefour and Lulu apps, then the same list on a premium chain's site; multiply by four
  • Transport: the RTA app and website for Nol fares by zone, monthly passes and taxi tariffs; Google Maps for your actual commute from the area you consider
  • Eating out: the menus of three places in your area are public on delivery apps and Google; look at a cafeteria, a casual chain and a mid-range restaurant
  • Phone and gym: the operators' sites and the gyms' sites publish prices; ask about annual payment and corporate discounts through the employer
  • Talk to people who live there: ask the employer for a colleague in a similar role; residents' communities for Russian-speaking professionals in Dubai answer such questions daily, with the usual caution about anonymous advice
  • Put the result against the offer: our salary index 2026 and the role salary guides show the market estimate for your role; a budget that works on paper with a 20% margin is the one that works in life

FAQ

Is 8,000 AED a month enough to live in Dubai?

For one person, yes, with choices: a room in a shared apartment at 1,500 to 3,500 AED and a careful to comfortable everyday budget of 1,800 to 4,000 leaves room to save; a studio of your own at 3,500 to 7,000 a month equivalent is possible but leaves a thinner margin. There is no income tax on the salary and the employer provides health insurance, which changes the arithmetic compared with many cities. Orientation for autumn 2026; check current prices.

Where do residents in Dubai buy cheaper groceries and eat cheaper?

Groceries: Union Coop, Nesto, Viva and the cooperative societies for the budget tier, Carrefour and Lulu with their app deals for the mid tier, Waterfront Market and local shops for fruit and vegetables. Food: cafeterias in Karama, Al Rigga, Satwa and Deira serve full meals at 10 to 30 AED; weekday happy hours, discount apps and bank card offers cut restaurant bills. Public beaches, parks and building gyms are free.
