How much does a salon owner earn in Dubai: unit economics

Owner income is what remains after payroll, commission, rent and everything else, and it swings with one number more than any other: how full the schedule is. Here is how to calculate it for your salon.

Talk to us

Capacity: what the salon can sell

Capacity is the number of station or room hours available per month. Example: 8 stations x 10 opening hours x 26 days = 2,080 station-hours a month. This is the ceiling, and no salon sells all of it.

Utilisation and average ticket

Revenue = capacity x utilisation x revenue per booked hour. In the illustrative example, at 50% utilisation the salon sells 1,040 hours. If revenue per booked hour is 200 AED, monthly revenue is 208,000 AED. These are example numbers to show the method, not a Dubai benchmark: take yours from the booking system.

  • Utilisation is measured per master and per room, weekly
  • Revenue per booked hour mixes service price and service duration, so it shows which services really pay
  • Retail and add-ons raise the ticket without using more hours

Cost structure: payroll and rent

Payroll with commission is usually the largest line, rent is usually the second. A common planning orientation, not a Dubai statistic, is to keep total payroll including commission somewhere around 35-50% of revenue and rent around 10-20%. If your plan is above that, check pricing, utilisation or the rent itself.

  • Variable costs: commission, consumables, card fees
  • Fixed costs: base salaries, rent and service charges, visas and insurance spread by month, software, marketing, accounting
  • Owner's own salary if the owner works in the salon: count it as a cost

Break-even and profit: worked example

Illustrative numbers: fixed costs 110,000 AED a month, variable costs 35% of revenue. Break-even revenue = 110,000 / (1 - 0.35) = about 169,000 AED, which in the example is about 41% utilisation. At 50% utilisation and 208,000 AED revenue, contribution after variable costs is about 135,000 AED and profit before tax is about 25,000 AED a month. At 60% the profit roughly doubles; at 35% the salon loses money. That sensitivity is the whole story of salon profitability.

What this means for the owner

  • Profit grows fastest from utilisation and repeat clients, not from cutting small costs
  • A plan that needs more than 60-70% utilisation to break even is fragile
  • Payback = opening investment divided by monthly profit after break-even
  • Corporate tax and VAT apply above the legal thresholds; plan them with your accountant

FAQ

How much does a beauty salon owner make in Dubai?

It depends mainly on utilisation, average ticket, payroll share and rent. Calculate revenue as capacity x utilisation x revenue per booked hour, subtract variable and fixed costs, and you get profit before tax. Small changes in utilisation change profit a lot.

What share of revenue should salon payroll be?

A common planning orientation is around 35-50% including commission, with rent around 10-20%. Treat these as orientation, not rules, and check against your own numbers.

At what utilisation does a salon break even?

Divide fixed costs by the share of revenue left after variable costs, then convert that revenue into hours. In our illustrative example it is about 41%. If your plan needs 60-70% or more, it is fragile.
