Dubai vs other big cities: compare your own costs an 8-line framework you fill in for Dubai and your current city, plus the facts that are safe to rely on

Every table on the internet that compares Dubai with Moscow, Istanbul, Almaty, Warsaw, London or Berlin is wrong for you personally: it compares an average person with an average basket in a month that has already passed. The question you actually have is different: with my salary offer, my way of living and my habits, will I be better or worse off in Dubai than where I am now? That question has an honest answer, and you can calculate it in an evening. This guide gives the framework: eight line items, how to fill each one for Dubai and for your city, which facts about Dubai are safe to rely on without a number, and why "Dubai is expensive" is true and false at the same time.

The 8 line items: fill them in for both cities

Take a sheet with three columns: the line, your current city, Dubai. Fill in monthly figures in one currency. For Dubai use the orientation ranges from our everyday cost and renting guides and then check them against live listings and apps; for your city use your last three months of actual spending, not your memory.

The facts about Dubai that are safe to rely on

Prices change monthly; some things do not. These qualitative points hold without a number and shift the arithmetic for most newcomers.

  • No personal income tax on salaries in the UAE: the figure in the offer letter is the figure that arrives, which is why a gross-to-gross comparison with a European or CIS salary is misleading; compare net to gross
  • Employer-provided health insurance for employees is standard and mandatory for employers in Dubai; for many people this removes a line that is significant at home
  • The employer sponsors the work visa for the person they hire and pays the related process costs; the right to work is free for the candidate
  • Public transport is affordable, clean and safe; a metro-based life in Dubai is cheaper than a car-based one by a wide margin, and the choice is yours
  • Rent is the biggest line and the one that varies most: the same salary buys a room in a shared apartment or a studio in a prime tower, and that single decision moves the whole comparison
  • Groceries vary a lot by store tier, by half or more for the same basket; the "expensive groceries" story and the "cheap groceries" story are both told by honest people shopping in different places
  • Many jobs in beauty, clinics and hospitality come with staff accommodation, a housing allowance or transport, which can be worth more than a salary difference; read the whole package, not the headline number
  • Gratuity: UAE employees accrue an end-of-service payment under current labour rules, paid when the employment ends; the exact calculation depends on the contract and the rules at the time, check MOHRE

Why "Dubai is expensive" is true and false at once

Both statements are correct, about different cities that share a name.

  • True: the Dubai of hotels, beach clubs, taxis, imported cheese, international schools and prime-tower rent is among the more expensive cities of the world, and a newcomer who copies the tourist month will feel it in the first statement
  • False: the Dubai of a metro-side studio, a budget supermarket, cafeterias, free beaches and an employer's health insurance costs, for many specialists, about what a comparable life costs in the big city they came from, sometimes less, with no tax taken from the salary
  • The difference between the two is not luck or income; it is a set of choices about housing, transport and eating out made in the first three months, which is why those months deserve a plan
  • What is genuinely more expensive for most newcomers: alcohol, international schooling, premium imported food, car ownership with parking in the centre, and anything bought at a hotel
  • What is genuinely cheaper or free for most newcomers: tax on salary, health insurance as an employee, fuel, public transport, phone data, beaches and parks, personal services at the neighbourhood level, and the time saved by a city that works 24 hours
  • The honest conclusion: Dubai rewards people who plan and punishes people who improvise; the framework above is the plan

Reading the offer against the sheet

Once both columns are filled, the decision becomes arithmetic plus a few questions to the employer. Do this before accepting, in writing, so that the answers are part of the package you agree to.

  • Add up the Dubai column with the housing option you would really choose and subtract it from the gross offer; the remainder is your monthly margin; compare it with your current margin after tax at home
  • Ask the employer which costs are covered: accommodation or allowance, transport, flights home, visa for family, medical insurance for dependants, a salary advance for the first rent; each yes changes the sheet
  • Check the salary against the market: our salary index 2026 and the role guides (assistant salaries, office staff salaries, doctor salaries) give a market estimate for most roles we work with; an offer far below the range is a question, not a disaster
  • Build in a 20% margin for the first six months: unknown costs, a late first salary, a flight home for a family matter; the margin is what keeps a serious decision from becoming a stressful one
  • Decide what success looks like in money terms before you move: a monthly saving, a debt paid, a sum sent home; a goal makes the everyday choices easy
  • If the sheet does not work, say so and negotiate or wait; a move that works on paper with a margin is the move that stays a good adventure, and a better offer often comes to a candidate who asked good questions

What this framework does not do

Being honest about the limits is part of the method.

  • It does not predict prices: every Dubai figure must be checked in the month you move, with the apps and listings named in our everyday cost and renting guides
  • It does not replace legal or immigration advice: visa, family sponsorship and labour rules change, and the current versions live on official portals (ICP, GDRFA, MOHRE); TLNT helps you meet an employer who sponsors the visa and does not advise on immigration
  • It does not compare quality of life: safety, climate, distance from family, career growth and the feeling of a city are real factors that belong in your decision next to the sheet, not inside it
  • It does not fit a family automatically: schooling, a second income or its absence, and dependants' visas and insurance change the picture a lot; fill the sheet for the whole household, honestly
  • It does not tell you to move: it tells you what the move costs and what it leaves you, so that the decision is yours, made calmly, with the responsibility understood and the upside seen clearly

FAQ

Is Dubai more expensive than Moscow, Istanbul, Warsaw or London?

It depends on how you live, which is why we give a framework instead of a table. Rent and eating out can be higher or lower than in your city depending on the area and tier you choose; salaries carry no personal income tax in the UAE and the employer provides health insurance, which lowers the real cost of a month for an employee. Fill in the 8 line items for both cities with your own numbers, net at home against gross in Dubai, and the answer is yours, not an average.

Can I bring my family to Dubai on a work visa?

Family sponsorship by an employed resident exists and depends on current rules, including salary and accommodation conditions, and on the costs of the dependants' visas and insurance, which the employer may or may not cover. Check the current requirements on the official portals (ICP, GDRFA) and ask the employer in writing what is included. TLNT does not give immigration advice; we help you meet an employer who sponsors your own work visa.
