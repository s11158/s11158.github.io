Assistant for a doctor in Dubai what a practising doctor can hand over and what it costs

A doctor or surgeon who works across two or three clinics in Dubai carries a second job nobody sees: the diary, the licence and CME calendar, conference registrations and flights, referral letters and emails, a publication deadline, and the family's school run and travel. A clinic receptionist or a patient coordinator does not do this work; they work for the clinic, not for you. This guide is about a personal or executive assistant for the doctor as a person: what hurts without one, what to delegate (non-clinical only), which type fits, what it costs and how to check the person before you trust them with your calendar.

A typical week without an assistant

The pattern is similar for a dermatologist with two clinics, a dentist who consults in three places or a surgeon with operating days in different hospitals. None of the items below is dramatic on its own; together they eat the evenings and the weekends.

  • Monday: a clinic asks to move your afternoon slot, you agree in WhatsApp and forget that the second clinic has already booked a consultation for the same time
  • Tuesday: a reminder that your licence renewal window opens; the CME certificates are in three email accounts and two folders, and nobody has counted the credits
  • Wednesday: a conference you wanted to speak at closes abstracts in two days; the flight and hotel for the one you already registered for are still not booked
  • Thursday: forty unread emails from colleagues, a referral request, a journal reviewer invitation, a query about your own health insurance policy
  • Friday: the school sends a form, the car registration is due, a family member's visa medical appointment needs booking, and you are in theatre until seven
  • Every week: small fees paid late, an early-bird conference discount missed, a document re-requested twice, an hour of consultation time spent on admin that pays nothing

What the assistant takes over

Everything below is non-clinical. The assistant does not touch diagnosis, treatment, prescriptions or patient records beyond what the clinic's own staff give them to coordinate. Split the list into business, personal and household tasks and agree it in writing.

  • Business: one master calendar across clinics and hospitals, with travel time and buffers; slots confirmed with each clinic's coordinator; a weekly plan sent to you on Sunday evening
  • Business: the licence and CME file: what is due when, certificates collected after each course, credits counted against the regulator's requirement, reminders well before the renewal window; the application itself is submitted by you or the clinic's licensing person
  • Business: conferences and courses: abstract deadlines, registration, flights, hotels, visas, speaker forms, expense claims
  • Business: correspondence: inbox triage, drafts of routine replies for your approval, referral and thank-you letters prepared from your notes, follow-ups you promised and forgot
  • Business: publications and teaching: formatting, references, submission portals, co-author follow-ups, deadlines tracked
  • Personal: your own appointments, insurance, car and bank paperwork, personal travel, gifts, restaurant and sports bookings
  • Household: schools and activities, home maintenance visits, driver and nanny coordinated, deliveries, family travel documents
  • Keep to yourself: anything clinical; signing documents; payments beyond an agreed limit and list; your licence portal login and e-signature; confidential HR matters at the clinic; conversations with patients

Which type fits and what it costs

Choose by the mix of tasks, not by the title. One person is enough for a doctor with one household and two or three sites. A second person, usually a house manager or a driver, is needed when the household alone takes a full day or when you travel more than a week a month and someone must stay with the calendar. How to find and check

Candidates come from three places: assistants of other doctors and clinic owners who want a bigger role, experienced personal assistants from other fields who can learn medical vocabulary, and clinic coordinators ready to work for one principal. Test on tasks, not on a conversation.

  • Email triage: 20 real anonymised emails and 30 minutes; ask for three groups, answer today, draft for me, ignore, with a one-line reason for each
  • Calendar conflict: two clinics and a hospital have booked the same Thursday; the candidate proposes a resolution and writes the three messages
  • Profession-specific task: book a week-long conference trip to Europe that overlaps your licence renewal deadline and a CME course with limited places; ask what they would check first
  • Vocabulary: give them a conference programme and ask which sessions match your specialty; you are checking care and reading, not medical knowledge
  • Red flags: "I will handle everything" without a single question; no system for recurring deadlines; details of a previous doctor's patients or income shared in the interview; no written English sample
  • Probation plan: month one, calendar and inbox in draft mode; month two, licence file and travel; month three, personal and household tasks; by the end a written list of what they own and what they must ask about, see our probation guide

Risks and boundaries

An assistant to a doctor sees colleagues' names, your income, your family's addresses and sometimes patient names in your inbox. Treat access as a decision, not a default.

  • Confidentiality clause in the employment contract plus a separate written NDA; explain that patient information in your inbox falls under the clinic's rules and is never forwarded or discussed
  • Accounts: delegate access to the calendar and a separate work inbox, not your personal passwords; the regulator's licence portal and your e-signature stay with you
  • Money: a card with a limit for bookings and small fees, a monthly statement you reconcile yourself, no transfers from your accounts
  • Dependence: a shared handover document with recurring deadlines, logins in a password manager you control, contacts of each clinic's coordinator
  • Backup: who watches the calendar when the assistant is on leave; agree it before the first holiday, not during it
  • Boundaries: working hours and when a late message is acceptable; a surgeon's assistant often works a split day, so write it in the contract

FAQ

Does a doctor in Dubai need an assistant?

A doctor who works in one clinic with a good coordinator usually does not. A doctor or surgeon with two or more sites, a licence and CME calendar, conferences, publications and a family in Dubai usually loses several hours a week to admin that no clinic employee covers. If you regularly do paperwork after nine in the evening or have missed a deadline this year, an assistant pays for itself in consultation time.

How much does an assistant for a doctor cost in Dubai?

Market estimate: a personal assistant 5,000 - 10,000 AED per month, senior 12,000 and above; a doctor's admin assistant who runs the licence file, correspondence and conferences 8,000 - 12,000, senior 12,000 - 18,000; a hybrid assistant covering business, personal and household 10,000 - 18,000, senior 18,000 - 25,000.
