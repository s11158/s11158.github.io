Lead response time how fast to reply and how to fix it

A person who writes to you in WhatsApp or Instagram is often writing to two or three competitors at the same time. The first useful reply usually wins the conversation. This guide covers why leads go cold, how to measure first response time in a CRM, what target to set and how to bring the number down without adding people to the team.

Why leads go cold

Interest is highest at the moment someone sends the message. An hour later they may have booked elsewhere, found the answer on a competitor's page or simply moved on with their day.

The best-known evidence is an audit of how US companies handled web leads, published in Harvard Business Review in 2011 ("The Short Life of Online Sales Leads"): companies that tried to contact a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify it as those that waited longer. The study looked at web forms and calls, before messengers became the main channel. In chats, where clients expect a reply within minutes, the window is likely even shorter; that is our observation, not a figure from the study.

In salons, clinics and real estate the pattern is the same: the lead is not lost in negotiation, it is lost while waiting. Typical reasons for a slow first reply:

  • Messages arrive in several places: personal phones, Instagram, a website inbox
  • Nobody is assigned, so everyone assumes someone else has replied
  • The administrator is busy with a client at the desk
  • Evenings, weekends and public holidays have no cover
  • The first reply is a price list with no question, so the conversation stops there

How to measure first response time in a CRM

You cannot fix what you do not measure. First response time is the time between the lead's first message, call or form and the first reply from a person. Set it up like this:

  • Every channel creates a lead in the CRM: WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, website forms, lead ads and missed calls
  • Two moments are recorded: when the lead arrived and when a person first replied; some CRMs report this out of the box, otherwise an automation writes both times to fields
  • The auto-reply is counted separately: it holds the client, but it is not a first response from your team
  • Look at the median, not only the average, and at the share of leads answered within your target; one lead answered after ten hours spoils the average but tells you less than the share
  • Split the numbers by working hours and off hours, by channel and by employee, to see where the delay actually sits
  • Read ten random chats a week: the number shows speed, the chats show whether the reply was useful

What target to set

There is no official standard. The targets below are our recommendation for businesses that sell in messengers, based on how clients behave in chats; adjust them to your volume and team. Track the share of leads that hit the target, for example the share of WhatsApp chats answered within five minutes, and raise it month by month.

How to fix a slow first reply

Speed comes from setup, not from asking the team to try harder.

  • An auto-reply that asks one useful question: not "thank you for your message", but "Which treatment are you interested in, and which day suits you?" The client answers, and the administrator starts from a real request
  • Routing: each new lead gets one responsible person at once, by channel, language, service or in turn
  • Notifications: new leads show up on the administrator's screen and in the CRM mobile app, and a lead left without a reply past the target alerts the manager
  • Quick replies and templates for prices, location, parking and preparation, so an answer takes seconds
  • An AI bot for nights, weekends and peak hours: it answers typical questions like a good administrator, collects the details, offers a time and hands the conversation to a person when needed
  • A daily control report for the owner: yesterday's leads, median first response time, leads without a reply and leads without a next step

Weekly checklist for the owner

Fifteen minutes once a week is enough to keep response time under control.

  • Median first response time this week against last week, in working hours and off hours
  • Share of messenger leads answered within the target
  • Leads that never got a reply from a person, and why
  • Response time by employee and by channel: where the delay sits
  • Ten random chats read in full: was the first reply useful, was a next step offered
  • Leads the bot handed over to a person: how fast the person picked them up
  • One change for next week: a template, a routing rule or a shift adjustment

FAQ

How fast should a business reply to a WhatsApp inquiry?

Our recommendation for messenger leads is a first reply from a person within 5 minutes in working hours, and outside working hours an instant auto-reply or AI bot, with a person following up at the start of the next working day. This is a working target, not an official standard.

Does an auto-reply count as a first response?

For measurement, no. An auto-reply that asks one useful question keeps the client in the conversation, but measure the first reply from a person separately: that is the number that shows whether the team keeps up.

How do I measure lead response time in a CRM?

Connect every channel so that each message, form and missed call creates a lead, then record when the lead arrived and when a person first replied. Look at the median and at the share of leads answered within your target, split by channel, employee and working hours.
