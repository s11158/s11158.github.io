Assistant for a yacht owner in Dubai crew, marina, trips and documents: what to delegate and what it costs

A yacht in Dubai is a pleasure that comes with a small company attached: a crew to coordinate, a marina with its own rules and invoices, a maintenance calendar that does not wait, registration and insurance documents, trips with guests who change their minds, provisioning for every weekend. Owners who also run a business or a family rarely have the hours for it, and the captain should not be the owner's personal assistant. This guide explains what an assistant to a yacht owner takes over, where the line between the assistant, the captain and a yacht management company runs, which type of assistant fits, what it costs as a market estimate, how to check the person and how to limit the risks.

A typical week without an assistant

Most of these pains land on the owner's phone on a Thursday evening, right before the weekend the boat was bought for.

  • Monday: the captain asks for a decision on a repair quote, the marina sends a berth invoice with a new fee line, and both wait while the owner is in meetings
  • Tuesday: a crew member's visa, medical or certificate expiry is noticed late, and the weekend trip depends on who can legally be on board
  • Wednesday: the annual survey or a service date clashes with the trip that was promised to guests; nobody saw both dates in one calendar
  • Thursday: twelve guests become eighteen, two of them do not eat seafood, the provisioning list is in three WhatsApp chats, and the chef hears about it at 22:00
  • Friday: the insurance renewal document is in a mailbox nobody checks; the owner finds out when the marina or a charter partner asks for it
  • Weekend: the owner spends the trip answering calls about fuel, cleaning and the next week's schedule instead of being a guest on their own boat

What the assistant takes over

The assistant coordinates, the captain commands the vessel, licensed specialists do the technical work, and the owner decides. Write the business, personal and household split and the limits down.

  • Business and vessel: crew coordination: rosters and leave, contracts and documents list with expiry dates, visas and medicals coordinated with the PRO or the management company, onboarding of new crew with the captain; see our yacht crew page
  • Business and vessel: marina and maintenance calendar: berth contract and invoices, service and survey dates, repair quotes collected for the owner's approval, contractors scheduled with the captain, warranty and spare parts tracking
  • Business and vessel: documents coordination: registration, insurance, crew certificates, port and marina paperwork collected and filed; the authority, the surveyor and the insurer are dealt with by the people who are authorised to do so
  • Trips and guests: trip calendar, guest lists and preferences, dietary needs, transfers to and from the marina, music, photographer, children's needs, a one-page brief for the captain and the chef per trip
  • Provisioning: standing lists for food, drinks, linens, toiletries and consumables, orders to suppliers, deliveries timed with the crew, stock checks after each trip
  • Personal and household: the owner's calendar, travel, family appointments, cars, homes and household staff, so the boat does not compete with the rest of life
  • What should NOT be delegated: navigation, safety and crew decisions on board, which belong to the captain; signing contracts and insurance; money transfers without a written rule; hiring and dismissal of crew, which the owner and the captain decide; anything a regulation reserves for a certified person

Which type fits and what it costs

For an owner who uses the boat privately, a personal assistant with a vessel brief or a lifestyle manager is usually enough. For an owner with a business, a family and a boat, a hybrid multi-assistant or an executive assistant plus a lifestyle manager works better. Technical management of the vessel is a yacht management company or the captain, not the assistant. Salaries are a market estimate in AED per month, total package, basic salary plus allowances; the employer also pays visa, health insurance and gratuity provision.

How to find and check

Look for people who have worked around yachts or luxury hospitality: yacht or charter office coordinators, former crew who moved ashore, lifestyle managers, concierges from hotels and private clubs, personal assistants to owners of boats. Test on tasks that look like your season.

  • Email and WhatsApp triage: 15 messages from the captain, the marina, a contractor, two guests, the insurer and the family; sort by urgency, draft three replies
  • Calendar conflict: a service slot, a guest trip and a crew member's leave fall on the same weekend; what moves, who is asked, what the owner must decide
  • Profession-specific task: build a season calendar from a messy list of survey, service, insurance, berth and crew document dates, plus three trips, and flag what collides
  • Provisioning: write a brief for a day trip for 14 guests with two dietary restrictions, including timings for deliveries and a question list for the chef
  • Red flags: wants to instruct the captain on the vessel, promises to "handle registration" or the authority alone, careless about guests' privacy, no questions about budgets and limits
  • Probation plan: weeks 1-2 the calendar and the documents list with every outgoing message approved, weeks 3-4 marina and maintenance coordination with the captain, weeks 5-8 trips, provisioning and a weekly summary, then a written scope

Risks and boundaries

An assistant to a yacht owner knows where the owner is every weekend, who the guests are, what the boat costs and how the family lives. Privacy is the first boundary.

  • Confidentiality: an NDA or a contract clause covering guests, trips, the owner's location, costs, crew and family matters; no photos or posts without the owner's explicit permission
  • Access: a shared mailbox for the vessel, a shared calendar and a document store with role-based access; a password manager; bank access read-only or none, approvals with the owner
  • Money: a written rule for provisioning and small repairs with a monthly limit; every quote above the limit goes to the owner; a weekly summary of what was paid
  • Chain of command: the captain is responsible for the vessel, safety and crew on board; the assistant does not give orders on the boat and does not override the captain's technical decisions
  • Dependence on one person: a vessel file with documents, dates, suppliers, contractors, marina contacts and recurring tasks; the captain or the management company as backup for leave and sickness
  • Scope: when the technical and regulatory side grows, use a yacht management company or a dedicated manager; the assistant stays the owner's coordinator, not the vessel's manager

FAQ

Does a yacht owner in Dubai need an assistant?

If the owner also runs a business or a family and the boat is used most weekends, usually yes: crew documents, marina invoices, service and survey dates, insurance renewals, guests and provisioning need one calendar and one coordinator. The captain commands the vessel and should not be the owner's personal assistant; a management company handles the technical side; the assistant is the owner's single point of coordination.

How much does an assistant for a yacht owner cost in Dubai?

Market estimate: an experienced personal assistant 8,000 - 10,000 AED per month and 12,000+ for a senior one, a lifestyle manager or personal concierge 10,000 - 18,000 AED as an orientation, a hybrid multi-assistant who also covers the owner's business and family 10,000 - 18,000 AED, senior 18,000 - 25,000 AED.
