Working hours, overtime and Ramadan for clinics and salons

Clinics and salons work long days and split shifts, and every year before Ramadan owners ask how many hours staff may work and how overtime is paid. This guide gives the principles of the UAE labour rules and a practical way to plan shifts. It does not quote premiums or penalties: MOHRE publishes the current rules, so confirm them before you fix a schedule.

The basic rules in plain words

The UAE labour law sets limits on normal working hours in the private sector, rest breaks and a weekly rest day. The details can be updated, so treat the points below as orientation and check them with MOHRE.

  • There is a daily and a weekly limit on normal working hours, and the contract should state the schedule
  • A break is required after a continuous stretch of work, and the contract or company policy describes it
  • Every employee has at least one weekly rest day
  • Breaks and time at the workplace between split-shift parts are treated differently, so write down how you count them
  • Some categories of staff and some sectors have their own arrangements, so check yours

Overtime: how to handle it correctly

Overtime is work beyond the agreed hours. The law allows it within limits and requires it to be paid at a premium over the basic wage, with a higher rate for night hours. We do not quote percentages here because they are set in the rules and can change.

  • Agree overtime in advance and record it, so there is no dispute at the end of the month
  • Do not fold overtime into a vague allowance; show it as a separate line in the payroll
  • Pay through the wage system, as for any other salary
  • If a role is managerial or paid by commission, check with MOHRE or your accountant how the rules apply before you assume overtime does not count
  • Plan the rota so overtime is the exception: regular overtime usually means the clinic or salon is short of one person

Ramadan: shorter hours and how to plan

During Ramadan the working day of private sector employees is shortened by a fixed amount, announced each year by the authorities. The dates move with the moon, so plan from the official announcement.

  • Ask your team in advance who observes the fast, but apply the shortened hours to everyone as the rules require
  • Move heavy treatments and long procedures to the hours when the team and the clients are most active
  • Expect more evening bookings in salons and a different pattern of appointments in clinics, and open the diary accordingly
  • Do not fill the lost hours with unpaid extra time; if you need more cover, schedule an extra person or paid overtime within the limits
  • Tell clients the Ramadan opening hours in WhatsApp, on Google Maps and at the front desk

Planning shifts in a small clinic or salon

Most problems are not about the law but about a rota that nobody can follow. A simple method works for teams of three to twenty people.

  • Build the rota from the busiest hours of your diary, not from the staff preferences
  • Keep a fixed weekly rest day per person and publish the rota at least a week ahead
  • Cover the front desk and the clinical or master roles separately, so a gap in one is visible
  • Keep a record of actual hours, even a simple sheet, to answer questions on overtime and leave
  • Review the rota every quarter against sales and no-show data

FAQ

How many hours can an employee work per day in the UAE?

The labour law sets a limit on normal daily and weekly working hours in the private sector, plus a break and a weekly rest day. Overtime is allowed within limits and is paid at a premium. Confirm the current figures with MOHRE and write the schedule in the contract.

Are working hours shorter during Ramadan?

Yes, the working day of private sector employees is shortened during Ramadan by an amount announced each year. Plan the rota from the official announcement and apply the shortened hours to all staff.

Can a salon or clinic ask staff to work overtime?

Yes, within the limits of the law, if it is agreed, recorded and paid at the required premium through payroll. Regular overtime is usually a sign that the team needs another person.
