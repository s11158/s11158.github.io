Documents to prepare before moving to Dubai what to collect, legalise and translate at home, and what to leave in order

Most delays in a relocation to Dubai are not caused by the visa; they are caused by a document that is still in a drawer in another country. A diploma that needs legalisation, a police clearance that someone must collect in person, a marriage certificate without a translation, a driving licence left at home. All of these are easy while you are still at home and slow, expensive or impossible from Dubai. This guide lists what a specialist should prepare before the flight: the passport, diplomas and attestation as a concept, translations, police clearance where required, medical records, the driving licence, photos, references, the licence paperwork for medics, digital copies, and what to leave in order at home. Which documents your case needs exactly depends on your role, your country and your employer; confirm the list with the employer's HR or PRO and the official portals (ICP, GDRFA, MOHRE, and DHA or DOH for medics).

Passport, photos and the basics

The short list that everyone needs regardless of role.

  • Passport: check the validity, many processes expect at least six months remaining, and the free pages; renew at home if it is close, because renewing abroad takes longer and your visa is linked to the passport number
  • Passport photos: a set on a white background, in the format your employer or the typing centre asks for; keep a digital version too
  • Birth certificate: not always needed for the employee alone, but required for children and useful for some procedures; legalised and translated if you bring family
  • Marriage certificate: needed to sponsor a spouse and for some housing and school matters; legalised and translated, as our live guides state
  • Your signed offer and contract, printed and as PDF, and the employer's visa document when it arrives
  • A short document index: a one-page list of what you have, where the original is and which version (legalised, translated) exists; it saves you from checking the same folder ten times

Diplomas, attestation and translations

Attestation (also called legalisation) is the process by which a document issued in one country is recognised as genuine in another. The exact chain depends on your country and on what the document is for, so the steps below describe the concept, not a fixed recipe; your employer, a licensing regulator or the official portals tell you which documents need it in your case.

  • What usually gets attested: university and college diplomas and transcripts, professional certificates that your role depends on, marriage and birth certificates for family sponsorship, sometimes experience letters
  • The chain as a concept: certification in the issuing country (the issuing institution or ministry, and the foreign ministry or an apostille, depending on the country), then the UAE embassy or consulate in that country, then attestation by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs after you arrive; the order and the names of the offices differ by country, and some employers use attestation services to run it
  • Start at home: the first steps of the chain can only be done in the issuing country, by you or by someone with a power of attorney, and this is the step people regret leaving
  • Translations: documents in Russian or another language need an official translation into Arabic or English, done by a legal translator recognised for use in the UAE; a translation made at home may be accepted for some purposes and not for others, so ask before paying twice
  • Names: make sure the spelling of your name in Latin letters is identical across passport, diplomas, translations and the employer's forms; a one-letter difference creates weeks of correction
  • For medics: the regulator's verification (DataFlow) checks the primary source of each document, so collect diplomas, internship and residency certificates, licences and experience letters early; see our DataFlow and DHA licence guides for the steps

Police clearance, medical records and the driving licence

Three documents that are often requested for specific roles or purposes, and all three are far easier to obtain at home.

  • Police clearance (certificate of good conduct, certificate of no criminal record): required for some roles and licences, in healthcare and education in particular, and sometimes by the employer; it is issued by the authorities of the country where you lived, usually has a limited validity, and may need legalisation and translation. Ask the employer whether your role needs it and from which countries, then obtain it as late as possible before the flight so it is still valid when used
  • Medical records: a summary of chronic conditions and current treatment, vaccination records (for you and the children, schools ask), recent test results, and prescriptions with the generic names of medicines; some medicines are controlled in the UAE and need a prescription or a permit to bring in, so check the Ministry of Health rules before packing them
  • Health insurance: the employer provides health insurance for employees; bring your records so the new clinic has a history, not because you will need to buy cover yourself
  • Driving licence: bring the original and, if your country issues it, an international driving permit; whether the licence can be converted to a UAE licence without tests depends on the country, check the current rules on the RTA portal. Keep a copy of your driving record if your insurer at home can issue one
  • For the children: school records, report cards and a transfer certificate from the current school, often attested and translated; vaccination records; schools in popular areas fill early, so start before you fly
  • Keep originals together in one travel folder and never hand an original to anyone outside an official process

References, work history and the paperwork for medics

Dubai employers and regulators both ask for proof of what you did and where. Collect it while your former employers still remember you.

  • Experience letters: for each relevant job, a letter on the company's letterhead with dates, title, duties and the signatory's contact; regulators for medical roles want exactly this format, and ordinary employers like it too
  • References: two or three people who can be called or emailed, warned in advance, with their current contact details; write in the CV that references are available on request, see our Dubai CV format guide
  • Portfolio for beauty and creative roles: before and after photos with the client's consent, certificates from courses and brands, a short video of your work; employers assess a master on a model, but a portfolio gets you to that stage
  • For doctors, dentists, nurses and allied health: the regulator's steps are primary source verification of your documents (DataFlow), an eligibility check against the requirements for your title, an assessment where required, and licence activation under the employing facility; documents typically include diplomas, internship and specialisation certificates, licences and registrations from every country where you practised, a certificate of good standing and experience letters. Fees, pass marks and processing times depend on the case, so take them from the regulator's portal, not from this page
  • For medics, read our live guides first: DataFlow primary source verification, the DHA licence for doctors and the DHA licence for nurses; the doctor relocation guide shows the order of licence, job and visa
  • Employment history document for yourself: a one-page timeline with dates, titles, employers and reasons for leaving; you will fill it into several forms and want the same dates each time

Digital copies and what to leave in order at home

The last two tasks are the ones people skip because they are not urgent. They become urgent at the worst moment.

  • Digital copies: scan every document, original and translation, in good quality, name the files clearly and store them in a cloud folder with two-factor protection plus an offline copy on a drive; share access with one trusted person at home
  • Power of attorney: a notarised power of attorney for a trusted person at home to collect documents, deal with your bank, property or tax office and sign on your behalf; it is the document that saves a flight back for a signature
  • Banks and payments at home: tell the bank you are moving, keep at least one account and a card that works abroad, set up online access and a way to receive codes without your home SIM, and decide what to do with loans and subscriptions
  • Tax and registration at home: find out what your home country expects from a resident who moves abroad (notifications, tax residence rules, property, pension records); a short consultation at home costs less than fixing it later. We do not give tax advice; ask a specialist in your country
  • Home SIM and accounts: keep the home number active if your bank or government services send codes to it, or move them to an app-based method before you leave; losing access to codes is the most common lock-out of the first month
  • Property, car and pets: who manages the flat, where the car is, whether the pet travels (with its own vaccination, microchip and import rules to check officially) or stays; written agreements with the people who help, even if they are family

FAQ

Do I need to attest my diploma to work in Dubai?

It depends on the role and the employer. Attestation is often required for professional and regulated roles (medical staff, teachers, some office positions where the degree is a condition) and for family sponsorship documents, and less often for roles where the employer hires on skills and a practical assessment. Ask the employer's HR or PRO which documents need attestation in your case, and start the first steps at home: they can only be done in the country that issued the document.

Do I need a police clearance certificate to move to Dubai for work?

Not for every role. It is often required for healthcare and education roles, for some licences and by some employers as part of their own checks. It is issued by the country where you lived, usually has a limited validity and may need legalisation and translation. Ask the employer whether your role needs it and from which countries, and obtain it close to the flight so it is still valid when used.
