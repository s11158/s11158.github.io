Salary vs cost of living in Dubai: how to calculate a method that answers "is X AED enough" for your own case

"Is 8,000 AED enough to live in Dubai?" is the most asked question before a move, and every honest answer starts with "it depends on how you live". Dubai has everything at every price: a shared room and a sea-view apartment, a bus pass and a leased SUV, a supermarket where groceries cost less than in Moscow and a deli where they cost more than in London. So this guide gives you a method rather than a verdict: how to read the offer package, how to list fixed and variable costs with orientation ranges, how to set a savings target and how to see in ten minutes whether a given salary works for you. Three examples by salary tier show the method in action; they are examples, not benchmarks. The live salary pages give the market ranges by role.

Step 1: read the package, not the headline number

A Dubai offer is a package. Two offers with the same headline can differ by 3,000 AED a month in real terms once you look at what is included. Write the package as separate lines before you compare it with anything.

  • Basic salary: the part that gratuity (end-of-service pay) and most calculations are based on; some employers set it low and add allowances, which is legal and common, but matters for the end-of-service payment
  • Allowances: housing, transport, phone, sometimes food; paid in cash monthly or provided in kind
  • Accommodation in kind: shared staff housing (common for beauty masters, junior clinic staff and household roles) removes the largest cost line but means living with colleagues; ask how many people per room and where it is
  • Transport in kind: a staff bus or a company car; without it, budget a transport line
  • Health insurance: the employer covers the employee by law in Dubai; ask about the plan level and whether family is included
  • Flights: an annual ticket home is common in many contracts; check whether it is a ticket or a cash allowance
  • Commission or bonus: for masters, sales and real estate this can be half of real income; ask for the scheme in writing and for what a typical colleague earned last quarter, then count only the conservative figure
  • No personal income tax on salaries in the UAE: the number in the offer is the number you receive; your home country's tax residency rules are a separate question to check yourself
  • Our guides on the employment contract and on probation and notice explain what else to read in the offer

Step 4: set a savings target and run the formula

The move makes sense when the salary covers life and leaves what you came for: savings, remittances, a deposit on something, a buffer. Decide the target before you look at the offer, otherwise the offer decides it for you.

  • Formula: monthly income you can rely on (basic plus cash allowances plus the conservative commission) minus fixed costs minus variable costs equals your monthly margin
  • Compare the margin with your target: if you want to send 2,000 AED home and save 1,500, the margin must be 3,500 or more after a realistic rent line
  • Add the one-off costs of the first three months on top: deposit, agent commission, furniture, SIM and connections, a first grocery stock; divide them by 12 to see the real first-year monthly cost
  • Stress test: what happens if rent rises at renewal within the RERA index, if commission falls by a third, if a family member needs a flight; the offer should survive at least one of these without debt
  • Compare the margin, not the salary, with your current city: a specialist in Moscow, Almaty, Istanbul, Warsaw or Berlin should put their own current lines in the same table; the honest comparison is "what is left at the end of the month", not the headline
  • Qualitative points that do not fit a table but matter: no personal income tax on salaries, employer-paid health insurance, affordable public transport, a cheap and safe way to travel across the region, and the fact that most people's second contract in Dubai pays better than the first

Where to check real prices before you decide

Do not rely on this page or on any single source for prices; check the lines that matter most for you in the week before you accept the offer.

  • Rent: Bayut, Property Finder, Dubizzle; filter by area and by furnished or unfurnished; read the RERA rental index for the area
  • Utilities: DEWA tariff pages; ask the landlord whether cooling is on DEWA or a separate chiller bill
  • Transport: RTA site for Nol fares and passes; rental companies for cars; fuel prices are published monthly
  • Groceries: Carrefour, Lulu and Spinneys online stores show live prices; compare the same ten items
  • Schools and nurseries: the KHDA directory lists school fees and ratings
  • Salaries: our salary index 2026, doctor salaries, office staff salaries and assistant salary pages give market estimates by role so you can tell whether the offer itself is in range
  • If the numbers do not work, say so to the employer: a housing allowance, a higher basic or shared accommodation are normal things to discuss before signing; you agree the terms with the employer directly, and we advise on the market package when we are involved

FAQ

Is 8,000 AED a month enough to live in Dubai?

For one person it depends on housing. With employer-provided accommodation 8,000 AED leaves a comfortable margin. Renting a studio alone in an outer district (orientation, autumn 2026: roughly 3,500 - 6,000 AED per month plus utilities) makes it workable but tight for savings; a shared room (roughly 1,500 - 3,000) makes it comfortable again. Run the method on this page with your own lines: fixed costs, variable costs, savings target, and check prices on Bayut, DEWA and RTA before you decide.

What salary do I need to live comfortably in Dubai?

There is no single number; it depends on housing, family and the lifestyle you want. Use the formula: reliable monthly income minus fixed costs minus variable costs equals your margin, then compare the margin with your savings target. Our examples show one person with staff housing on 6,500 AED keeping a large margin, one renting a studio alone on 11,000 keeping a few thousand, and one on 25,000 with a car and a one-bedroom keeping about a third.
