Medical director salary in Dubai: monthly pay and full cost

How much a clinic in Dubai usually pays a medical director, and what the role costs the owner in total. The ranges below are market estimates that we use when advising clinic owners; the real figure depends on the specialty, the DHA title and on whether the director also treats patients.

Monthly salary by type of role

Monthly package in AED, market estimate for Dubai. There is no personal income tax on salaries in the UAE, so the amount in the contract is what the doctor receives.

RoleTypical settingSalary, AED/month
Practising physician with the medical director titleSmall clinic, the director keeps a full patient list40,000 - 60,000

Group medical directorSeveral clinics or a hospital group90,000 - 130,000+

What moves the number up or down

Two medical directors can differ by tens of thousands of dirhams a month. The difference usually comes from the following.

  • Specialty and DHA title: a consultant in a high-demand specialty is paid more than a general practitioner with the same title
  • Patient time: a director who still sees patients often earns part of the income from a revenue share
  • Facility type: day surgery, diagnostics and multi-specialty centres carry more clinical risk than a small outpatient clinic
  • Team size: number of doctors, nurses and allied health staff the director leads
  • Inspection record: DHA audits, incident handling and policies they have run themselves
  • Languages for your patient base: English is expected, Arabic or Russian is often a plus

Pay models clinics use

The fixed salary is only one way to pay a medical director. These are the models we see most often; write the formula into the contract.

  • Fixed salary only: simple and predictable, common for full-time directors who do not treat patients
  • Clinical salary plus a monthly allowance for the director title: common when a practising doctor takes on the role
  • Fixed part plus a revenue share on the director's own patients: common in aesthetic and dental clinics
  • Fixed part plus an annual bonus on a few KPIs: audit results, incidents closed on time, patient complaints, clinic revenue
  • Tie part of the bonus to clinical quality and compliance, not only to revenue: the person who signs off clinical standards should not be paid only for volume

What the package includes besides the basic salary

Doctors compare whole packages. These are the usual parts.

  • Basic salary and allowances (housing, transport): end-of-service gratuity is calculated on the basic salary, so the split matters
  • Medical malpractice insurance: mandatory for practising in the UAE, and the law places the premium on the facility, so it is not deducted from the salary
  • Work permit, residence visa, medical test and Emirates ID: paid by the employer
  • Health insurance: mandatory, paid by the employer
  • DHA licence renewal and continuing medical education: often paid by the clinic, depends on the contract
  • Paid annual leave: 30 calendar days after a full year of service; a flight ticket home depends on the contract
  • End-of-service gratuity after at least one year: 21 days of basic salary for each of the first five years and 30 days for each year after that

Full monthly cost for the clinic

A worked example helps to plan the budget: a full-time medical director on 75,000 AED per month, of which 45,000 AED is basic salary.

Gratuity accrues at 21 days of basic salary per year, which here is 31,500 AED a year or about 2,600 AED a month. On top of that come malpractice insurance, health insurance, visa and work permit costs spread over the visa term, licence renewal if the clinic pays it, and the bonus if targets are met. Premiums and visa amounts depend on the specialty, the insurer and the company category, so ask your broker, PRO or accountant for the figures for your clinic.

  • Fixed part: monthly package agreed in the contract
  • Accruals: end-of-service gratuity on the basic salary
  • Mandatory extras: malpractice and health insurance, visa and work permit
  • Variable part: revenue share or bonus
  • Hidden cost of an empty seat: a DHA-licensed facility needs a licensed physician responsible for clinical matters, so a gap can affect the licence, not only the team

Full-time director, practising doctor with the title or clinic manager

The title changes the pay, so decide first which role you need. A medical director is a licensed physician responsible for clinical governance: policies, incident reporting, infection control, doctors' privileges and inspections. A clinic manager runs the business side and does not replace the medical director. DHA requirements for the medical director depend on the facility type and change over time, so check them for your licence before you agree the role.

  • Small outpatient clinic: a practising doctor with the title is often enough
  • Day surgery, several specialties or frequent inspections: consider a full-time director
  • Several branches: a group medical director with a medical lead in each clinic

FAQ

How much does a medical director earn in Dubai per month?

Market estimate: about 40,000 - 60,000 AED per month for a practising physician who holds the medical director title, 60,000 - 90,000 AED for a full-time medical director and 90,000 - 130,000 AED or more at group level. Specialty, DHA title and patient time move the figure.

Does a small clinic in Dubai need a full-time medical director?

Not always. DHA-licensed facilities need a licensed physician responsible for clinical matters, and in many small clinics this is a practising doctor who holds the title. The exact requirement depends on the facility type, so check it with DHA for your licence.

Who pays malpractice insurance for a medical director in Dubai?

Malpractice insurance is mandatory for practising in the UAE, and the law places the premium on the facility. Set it out in the offer and the contract and do not deduct it from the salary.
