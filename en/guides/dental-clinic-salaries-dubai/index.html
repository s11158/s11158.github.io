Dental clinic salaries in Dubai what a clinic owner pays in 2026

A dental clinic in Dubai usually runs with the same team: general dentists, one or more specialists, a hygienist, dental assistants at every chair, a front desk that knows insurance and a coordinator who turns treatment plans into booked visits. This guide gives market estimates of monthly pay for each role, explains how fixed salary and commission on collections work for dentists, and shows the cost on top of salary, so you can plan the budget of a new chair or a new clinic before the search starts.

Fixed salary or commission: how dentists are paid

Most dentists in Dubai private clinics are not paid a flat salary only. The model you choose changes both the cost and who you attract.

  • Fixed salary: predictable for the doctor, suits a new clinic that is still building its patient flow, but the clinic carries the risk of empty hours
  • Commission on collections: the dentist receives a share of what the clinic actually collects for their work, often around 30-40% for general dentists and specialists; materials and lab costs are usually agreed separately
  • Fixed minimum or commission, whichever is higher: the most common compromise, it protects the doctor in slow months and rewards a full schedule
  • Visiting specialists: some clinics pay an endodontist or oral surgeon per session or per case instead of a monthly salary
  • Write the rule in the contract: what counts as collections, how insurance rejections and refunds are treated, when the commission is paid

What moves the number

Two dentists with the same title can differ in pay by half or more. These factors usually decide where in the range a person sits.

  • Licence category: a DHA specialist or consultant licence costs more than a general dentist licence, and the category decides which procedures the doctor may do
  • A licence already active in Dubai: the doctor can start through a transfer, which saves the clinic weeks of empty chair time
  • Own patients: a dentist or orthodontist who brings a patient base or a strong social media following is often paid more through commission
  • Digital skills: intraoral scanning, guided implants, clear aligners and digital smile design are in demand in premium clinics
  • Languages: Arabic, Russian or Hindi for the patients of your clinic often matter as much as a year of extra experience
  • Insurance mix: in clinics with many insured patients the front desk and coordinator need approval experience, which raises their pay

The cost on top of salary

Salary is not the full cost of a dental team member. Add these items when you compare candidates and plan a new chair.

  • Visa and Emirates ID: paid by the employer; the amount depends on the company type and the free zone
  • DHA licensing: verification through DataFlow, the assessment if required and activation of the licence under your clinic; amounts depend on the case
  • Malpractice insurance for licensed clinicians and health insurance for every employee; the premium depends on the plan and the person's age
  • Gratuity provision: about 5.83% of basic salary per month in the first five years, see our gratuity guide
  • Annual leave of 30 calendar days after the first year: plan who covers the chair and the recall list
  • For dentists on commission: the time until the schedule fills, when a fixed minimum is paid without matching collections

How to build the team without overpaying

The right order of hires keeps payroll in line with the number of patients.

  • Start with general dentists and assistants; add a hygienist when there are enough patients for regular recall visits
  • Bring specialists in as visiting doctors first and move to full time when the referrals justify a fixed salary
  • Hire a front desk person with insurance experience early: rejected claims cost more than the difference in salary
  • Pay the coordinator a bonus on treatment plans that turn into booked visits, not on the number of calls
  • Agree the terms with each candidate yourself and send a written offer within a few days after the final interview, before the doctor accepts another clinic

FAQ

How much does a dentist earn in Dubai per month?

Market estimate: a general dentist in a private clinic earns about 15,000 - 25,000 AED per month or works on commission; specialists such as orthodontists, endodontists, periodontists and prosthodontists earn about 30,000 - 45,000 AED, and senior specialists with their own patient base up to about 55,000 AED. Many clinics pay a fixed minimum or around 30-40% of collections, whichever is higher.

How much does a dental hygienist earn in Dubai?

Market estimate: about 9,000 - 12,000 AED per month for a dental hygienist and up to about 14,000 AED for a senior hygienist. A dental assistant earns about 4,500 - 7,000 AED, and an experienced assistant for a specialist chair up to about 9,000 AED.

Should I pay my dentists a salary or commission?

A new clinic with few patients often starts with a fixed minimum, so it can attract good doctors while the schedule fills. Once the flow is stable, a fixed minimum or commission on collections, whichever is higher, links pay to results. Define in the contract what counts as collections and how insurance rejections are treated.
