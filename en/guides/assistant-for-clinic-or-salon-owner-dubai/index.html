Assistant for a clinic or salon owner in Dubai what to delegate and the first 30 days

A clinic or salon owner in Dubai often spends the day on small tasks: the calendar, suppliers, insurance questions, staff messages, social media, a patient complaint. An owner's assistant can take much of this off, but only if the role is defined and the owner knows what to hand over and what to keep. This guide lists what an assistant can handle, how to delegate, a first 30 days plan and what not to delegate.

Talk to us

What an assistant can take off the owner

The tasks below are administrative and coordination work. Clinical decisions, medical advice and treatment choices always stay with licensed staff.

  • Calendar: owner's meetings, supplier visits, staff one-to-ones, personal commitments, with conflicts flagged early
  • Suppliers: orders, reminders, delivery confirmations, price comparisons for the owner to approve
  • Insurance and licence paperwork support: collecting documents, tracking renewal dates, preparing files for the person who is responsible, see our compliance calendar guide
  • Patient and client escalations: logging a complaint, collecting facts, scheduling a call; the owner or manager decides the answer
  • Reporting: a short weekly summary of bookings, no-shows, revenue and open issues from the systems you already use
  • Social media coordination: a content calendar, collecting photos with consent, sending drafts for approval; claims and posts follow your regulator's advertising rules, see our advertising rules guide

First 30 days plan

A simple plan that lets the assistant learn the business without risk.

  • Days 1-5: access to calendar, shared files and key contacts; a list of the owner's recurring tasks; rules on what is private
  • Days 6-10: take over the calendar and supplier reminders; the owner approves each outgoing message
  • Days 11-20: add paperwork tracking and a weekly report; the owner approves only exceptions
  • Days 21-30: patient and client escalations in logging mode; review what worked, what to drop, what to hand over next
  • Day 30: a written scope of the role, a list of things the assistant owns and a list of things they must ask about

What not to delegate

Some things stay with the owner, the licensed professional or the manager, whatever the assistant's experience.

  • Clinical advice, diagnosis and treatment decisions, and anything the regulator reserves for licensed staff
  • Sending, changing or approving patient records without the responsible team member
  • Signing documents, bank approvals and anything that creates a legal commitment
  • Final decisions on complaints and refunds, hiring and dismissals
  • Giving a patient or client information that you would not give them yourself

Hiring mistakes to avoid

The same mistakes repeat when an owner looks for an assistant for the first time.

  • Hiring a front desk person and expecting them to act as the owner's right hand; they are different roles, see the clinic receptionist and clinic manager pages
  • No test on real tasks: ask the person to build next week's calendar from a messy list, draft a supplier reply and a patient apology
  • Skipping a conversation about confidentiality, patient data and what the assistant may and may not see
  • No budget for the right level: see our assistant salary guide, the owner's assistant range is a market estimate of 7,000 - 14,000 AED per month

What can an assistant do for a clinic owner in Dubai?

Clinical decisions, patient records and signing stay with licensed staff and the owner.
