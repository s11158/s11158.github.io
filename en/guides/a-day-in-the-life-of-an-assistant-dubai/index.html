A day in the life of an assistant in Dubai two composite days, hour by hour: an executive assistant in a company and a hybrid assistant to a family

People who consider becoming an assistant usually imagine either a glamorous life next to a wealthy principal or a servant who is called at midnight. Both pictures are wrong, and the truth is more interesting. This article from our School of assistants series walks through two composite days hour by hour: an executive assistant in a Dubai company and a hybrid assistant who runs the business and household affairs of a family. Both days are composite examples assembled from what the roles typically involve; they describe no real employer and no real person. We show the parts that make people love this job and the parts that make it hard, so you can decide with open eyes.

Talk to us

How to read these two days

An assistant's day is shaped by the principal's rhythm, so no two days are identical. The two below are typical rather than real: the hours and tasks are what the roles usually contain in Dubai, compressed into one working day each. Three things are true for both.

  • The job is a sequence of small decisions made on someone else's behalf, most of them invisible when done well
  • The hard parts are rarely the tasks themselves; they are the interruptions, the ambiguity and the emotional weather of the principal
  • The satisfying parts are concrete: a week that ran without a single dropped item, a problem solved before the principal knew it existed, a thank-you that was earned
  • Working hours, overtime and days off are set by the written contract and current UAE labour rules; what the day below shows is the rhythm, not a legal standard
  • Pay for these two roles as a market estimate: executive assistant 12,000 - 20,000 AED per month, up to 30,000 with a C-level executive or a private family office; hybrid multi-assistant 10,000 - 18,000, senior 18,000 - 25,000 (orientation, total package, basic plus allowances; the employer also pays the visa, health insurance and gratuity provision)

Day one: executive assistant to the managing director of a Dubai company (composite)

A trading and services company with about 60 staff, a managing director who travels two weeks a month, an executive assistant with four years in the role. The day is a Tuesday in a week without travel.

Day two: hybrid assistant to a family (composite)

A family with two school-age children, a father who runs a small holding company, a mother who manages property and a foundation, household staff of three. The hybrid assistant has been with the family for two years and works from a home office in the villa and from a laptop on the move.

What makes these days satisfying

Ask experienced assistants why they stay in the profession and the answers repeat.

  • Visible effect: a founder who gets three hours back a day builds something with them, and the assistant sees it
  • Trust: being the person a family or an executive relies on when something goes wrong is a form of respect that few junior roles offer
  • Variety: a legal document, a florist, a flight and a difficult conversation in one day; boredom is rare
  • Access to how decisions are made: assistants watch owners negotiate, hire, fire and plan, which is a business education no course gives
  • Growth: the ladder from administrative assistant to executive assistant, hybrid, family office and chief of staff is real, and pay grows with scope, see our career ladder article
  • Portable skills: calendar, writing, logistics, vendor management and judgement are useful in operations, events, HR and management, in Dubai and anywhere else

What makes them hard, and how experienced assistants cope

The hard parts are real and it is better to know them before you say yes. None of them is a reason to avoid the job; each has a working answer.

  • Interruptions: the plan breaks by 09:30 most days; experienced assistants plan in blocks with slack and keep one list where everything lands
  • Ambiguity: "handle it" without context; the answer is a short clarifying question up front, not a guess that costs a day
  • Emotional weather: a stressed principal is short with the nearest person; the skill is not to take it personally and to raise a pattern calmly later, see our boundaries and burnout article
  • Invisibility: a perfect week looks like nothing happened; keep your own record of what was prevented and show it in reviews, see our salary negotiation article
  • Blurred edges in hybrid roles: business, family and household tasks compete; agree the split and the emergency rules early and revisit them every few months
  • The late message: it is a line, not an evening, only if the rule was agreed; where it was never agreed, agree it now rather than resent it
  • Being new in Dubai adds a layer: a new city, new rules, no network yet; our guides on moving to Dubai for work and on the first 90 days of a new assistant cover that side

FAQ

What does an executive assistant do all day in Dubai?

A typical day is calendar and conflict resolution, inbox triage and drafting replies in the principal's voice, travel planning, meeting minutes and follow-ups, visitors, a few personal tasks that were agreed in advance, and a short end-of-day summary. The day above shows it hour by hour as a composite example. The hard parts are interruptions, ambiguity and the principal's mood, not the tasks themselves.

What are the working hours of an assistant in Dubai, and will I be called at night?

Working hours, overtime and days off are set by your written contract and current UAE labour rules; check the current rules on the MOHRE portal and read the contract before you sign. In practice a good role has an agreed emergency list and a rule for late messages, so a late message about tomorrow's logistics is a one-line answer, not an evening of work. Agree these rules at the offer stage, not after the first late call.
