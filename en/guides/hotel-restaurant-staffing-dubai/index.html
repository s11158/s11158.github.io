Hotel and restaurant staffing in Dubai: a 2026 hiring guide

Opening a venue, seasonality, AED salaries, certificates and practical checks - what an owner should know before the first interview.

A hotel or restaurant in Dubai lives on its team. You can copy an interior or a menu, but a well-drilled shift of kitchen, bar, floor and housekeeping is the hardest thing to build. Below is how hospitality hiring works in 2026: regulators, certificates, AED salaries, seasonality, typical mistakes, and the step-by-step plan we use on opening projects ourselves.

Who regulates it and which documents you need

A restaurant operates under a DET (Department of Economy and Tourism) trade licence and a Dubai Municipality food establishment permit. A hotel also goes through DET classification, and serving alcohol requires a separate venue licence. Mainland hiring runs through MOHRE, free zone hiring through the zone itself. The employer is the sponsor: the company arranges and pays for the visa, medical insurance and Emirates ID, and these costs cannot be deducted from salary (Federal Decree-Law No. 33/2021). The steps are covered in our work visa and permit guide.

Anyone who touches food or drinks has extra requirements:

  • Dubai Municipality food safety (food handler) training - for kitchen, bar and floor.
  • Person in Charge (PIC) - every shift needs a trained responsible person, usually a sous chef or shift manager.
  • Occupational health check and health card before the first shift.
  • For roles serving alcohol - responsible service rules, part of bartender and floor manager training.

Build these into the schedule: for someone coming from abroad, 3-5 weeks between offer and first shift is normal.

Which roles you need and what they cost

Indicative 2026 base salaries in Dubai, excluding housing, transport, service charge and bonuses. The range depends on hotel category, concept and scale.

  • Hotel general manager: AED 35,000-80,000 for 4-5 star, lower for boutique hotels, often plus a GOP bonus.
  • F&B manager: AED 18,000-32,000, responsible for revenue across restaurants, bar, room service and banquets.
  • Restaurant manager: AED 12,000-22,000 plus a revenue bonus.
  • Executive chef: AED 20,000-40,000; higher for chef-driven concepts.
  • Sous chef: AED 9,000-16,000.
  • Chef de partie: AED 5,000-9,000.
  • Pastry chef: AED 6,000-15,000, executive pastry chef up to AED 20,000.
  • Bartender: AED 3,500-7,000 plus service charge and tips; head bartender up to AED 10,000.
  • Waiter: AED 2,500-4,500 plus service charge and tips, housing often provided.
  • Housekeeping supervisor: AED 4,500-8,000.
  • Room attendant: AED 1,800-3,000 plus housing, transport and meals.
  • Event manager: AED 14,000-28,000; event coordinator: AED 7,000-12,000.

On top of salary, the budget covers visa and insurance, housing or an allowance, transport, duty meals, uniforms, training and an annual flight ticket. The full breakdown is in our guide on what it costs to hire in Dubai.

Seasonality: when to hire

Dubai's peak hotel season runs October to April, with spikes around New Year, GITEX, the Dubai Airshow (odd years) and holiday weeks. Good cooks and bartenders are busy then, and poaching costs more. The best window for line staff is May to August: the market is looser and there is time to train before high season. For an October opening, close management roles by June or July.

Housing, transport and retention

In Dubai hospitality, housing and transport for line roles are effectively the market standard. Large hotels run staff accommodation in Al Quoz, Jebel Ali or Al Barsha and bus shifts in; smaller restaurants more often pay an allowance of around AED 800-1,500 for housing and AED 300-500 for transport. Decide this before you go to market: candidates compare the total package, not the base rate.

Think through service charge and tips separately. A transparent distribution scheme (tronc, points by role) retains floor and bar staff better than a one-off raise. For managers, a revenue or GOP bonus with clear metrics works. People who get a mentor and a predictable schedule in their first month leave far less often: a 30-day onboarding plan costs less than hiring again.

Languages are part of the profile too. Fluent English is usually the baseline for the floor in Dubai, while Arabic, Russian or Chinese is an advantage if that matches your guests. Write it into the requirements as a language, not as the candidate's background.

Typical hospitality hiring mistakes

  • Hiring the chef after the menu is signed off and the kitchen is bought. The chef should shape the layout and equipment.
  • Judging only by CV and brand names. A cook from a chain hotel and a cook from an a la carte restaurant work at different rhythms; only a practical check shows it.
  • Ignoring housing and transport. Without them, good line staff leave for competitors within three months.
  • Hiring the whole team two weeks before opening. You need time for training, soft opening and training services.
  • Stating gender, age or nationality in the job ad. That conflicts with MOHRE rules; describe requirements through languages (English, Arabic, Russian) and skills.
  • Forgetting certificates: without food handler training a person cannot work in the kitchen or behind the bar.

A step-by-step hiring plan for an opening

  • 4-6 months out: GM or venue manager, head chef, F&B manager. They design the shift structure and payroll budget.
  • 3 months out: org chart and headcount plan, full hiring budget, decisions on housing and transport.
  • 8-10 weeks out: sous chefs, floor and housekeeping supervisors, head bartender, event manager.
  • 4-6 weeks out: line staff: section cooks, pastry, bartenders, waiters, room attendants, coordinators.
  • 2-3 weeks out: training, food handler and health checks, friends-and-family services.
  • Soft opening: 1-2 weeks on reduced covers, review of issues, shift adjustments.
Practical check. Kitchen: cook 2-3 dishes from your menu and check mise en place. Bar: classics plus 1-2 cocktails from your list, speed and cleanliness. Floor: table service and handling a complaint. Housekeeping: a timed room clean to your standard. Keep it short, agreed in advance and without guests; the person works real shifts only once the paperwork is done.

Checklist before the offer

  • The role is defined: duties, schedule, shifts, level of cuisine or service.
  • Salary range, service charge, housing, transport and meals are agreed.
  • Practical check done and a conversation with the future line manager held.
  • References from recent employers checked.
  • Candidate status is clear: in the UAE or abroad, visa change needed or not.
  • Food handler training, health check and start date are scheduled.
  • A 30-day onboarding plan is ready.

Frequently asked questions

How early should I start hiring before a hotel or restaurant opening?

Fill key roles (GM, head chef, F&B manager) 3-4 months before opening, sous chefs and supervisors 6-8 weeks before, and line staff 3-5 weeks before. Allow 2-4 weeks for the visa and certificates if the person is coming from abroad.

Does every restaurant employee in Dubai need a food handler certificate?

Anyone handling food or drinks - kitchen, bar, servers - needs Dubai Municipality food safety training, and each shift needs a trained Person in Charge. The occupational health check is done before the person starts work.

Who pays for staff accommodation and transport?

The employer decides. In hospitality it is common to provide housing and transport for line roles, or pay an allowance instead. Terms go into the offer and contract. Candidates are never charged anything for the search.

Can I test a candidate's skills before hiring?

Yes, a practical check is standard: cooking for the chef, a service round, cocktails at the bar. Keep it short, agreed in advance and without guests: the person does not work real shifts before the work permit.
