eClaimLink without the panic

eClaimLink is the Dubai Health Authority portal clinics use to send insurance claims. The portal is simple - the money is won or lost by the people who work with it.

By the TLNT hiring team · Published 11 Oct 2026 · Updated 25 Oct 2026

In short: eClaimLink is the DHA e-claims platform where Dubai clinics check patient eligibility, request prior approvals, submit claims and receive payments from insurers. Login is only on eclaimlink.ae. Rejected claims usually start with people, not the portal: a typical clinic team for it is a receptionist with insurance experience (7,000 - 10,000 AED a month), an insurance officer (6,000 - 10,000 AED) and a medical coder (8,000 - 14,000 AED), per the TLNT Salary Index 2026.

eClaimLink in 30 seconds

  • What it is: the electronic platform for health insurance transactions in Dubai: eligibility checks, prior authorisations, claims, resubmissions and remittances. Transactions pass through the DHA Health Post Office (DHPO).
  • Who runs it: the Dubai Health Authority (DHA). The portal states it is built in partnership with Dimensions Healthcare.
  • Who uses it: DHA-licensed clinics, hospitals, pharmacies and labs on one side; insurers and TPAs on the other.
  • What is there: login for providers and payers, account activation, official code lists (CPT, Dubai Drug Code, clinician list), the provider guide and the DHA medical coding guidelines.

Sources: eclaimlink.ae, eClaimLink code lists, DHA medical coding guidelines (PDF), dha.gov.ae. Abu Dhabi uses a different system (Shafafiya under DOH).

A day in the life of an insurance claim

Five steps from the patient's arrival to the money. Each one has a typical mistake and a person who owns it.

1
The patient arrives

Where it often goes wrong: the card is not checked, or the service is outside the patient's plan or network.

Owner: receptionist or patient coordinator

2
The policy is checked and approval requested

Where it often goes wrong: no prior authorisation, or the service delivered differs from what was approved.

Owner: insurance coordinator / officer

3
The doctor documents and the visit is coded

Where it often goes wrong: the notes do not support the codes, or diagnosis and procedure codes do not match.

Owner: the doctor and the medical coder

4
The claim goes to eClaimLink

Where it often goes wrong: late submission, or prices outside the list agreed with the insurer.

Owner: medical coder or insurance officer

5
Money arrives, or a rejection

Where it often goes wrong: rejections are not read, not corrected and not resubmitted in time.

Owner: billing lead, controlled weekly by the clinic manager

Checklist: the clinic is opening

What has to be in place on the people side before the first insurance patient.

FAQ

What is eClaimLink and who uses it?

eClaimLink is the Dubai Health Authority platform for electronic health insurance transactions: eligibility checks, prior authorisations, claims, resubmissions and remittances, passing through the DHA Health Post Office (DHPO). It is used by DHA-licensed healthcare facilities on one side and insurers and TPAs on the other.

Where is the eClaimLink login?

Only on the official site, eclaimlink.ae. TLNT has no login form and no access to the portal; for account activation and passwords, use the official site and DHA support.

Who in a clinic should handle insurance claims?

Reception checks eligibility before the visit, an insurance coordinator or officer handles approvals, remittances and resubmissions, a medical coder codes the visit and submits the claim, and the clinic manager reviews rejections and receivables every week. In a small clinic one person can combine the coordinator and coder roles.

How much does a medical billing specialist cost in Dubai?

According to the TLNT Salary Index 2026: an insurance officer earns about 6,000 - 10,000 AED a month, a medical coder 8,000 - 14,000 AED, and a revenue cycle manager 15,000 - 25,000 AED.

Does a small clinic need a separate insurance person?

If only a few patients come with insurance, a receptionist with insurance experience is often enough. Once about half of the patients are insured or there are three or more doctors, a dedicated insurance coordinator usually pays for themselves by cutting rejections.

How long does hiring take and what if the person does not fit?

The search usually takes about 2 weeks. If the person does not work out, we find a replacement free of charge within one month after they start. You make the hiring decision and the specialist is employed directly by your clinic.
