Finance, tax, AML and legal: hiring in Dubai

Who to hire now that corporate tax is here, how DIFC differs from mainland, which certifications to check and what to pay in 2026.

A few years ago many small Dubai businesses got by with an outside bookkeeper once a quarter. That has changed. VAT at 5% has applied since 2018, corporate tax at 9% applies to financial years starting on or after 1 June 2023, and anti-money laundering (AML) duties now reach real estate brokers, precious metals dealers, auditors and corporate service providers. E-invoicing is on the way. A wrong return or a missed goAML report can cost more in penalties than a specialist's annual salary, so finance, tax and legal roles are now hired as core roles, not afterthoughts.

Which roles you need and how they differ

Start by naming the function. A common mistake is to look for "a chief accountant who knows everything" and discover later that the person is strong in bookkeeping but has never prepared a corporate tax return.

  • Accounting and payments. An accounts payable specialist handles invoices, reconciliations and the payment calendar. A VAT accountant makes sure every document carries the right tax, files quarterly returns in EmaraTax and prepares the business for e-invoicing.
  • Tax. A tax consultant earns their place when you have a group structure, a qualifying free zone entity, transfer pricing or open questions with the FTA.
  • Assurance. An auditor, internal or from an audit firm; audited accounts are mandatory for many free zones and regulated sectors.
  • Credit and risk. A credit analyst assesses counterparties and borrowers, while a risk manager owns the whole risk framework and the regulator relationship.
  • AML. An AML analyst runs KYC and CDD, monitors transactions and drafts suspicious transaction reports in goAML.
  • Insurance. An insurance advisor sells and services policies, and a claims officer handles claims from notice to settlement.
  • Legal and governance. A lawyer covers contracts and disputes, a paralegal prepares documents and tracks deadlines, and a company secretary keeps licences, the UBO register, board minutes and renewals in order.

Regulators: what the role must comply with

Candidate requirements depend on where the company is licensed. On the mainland, financial institutions answer to the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE), capital markets to the SCA, tax to the Federal Tax Authority (FTA), and AML reports go to the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit through goAML. DIFC has its own regulator, the DFSA, and its own common law framework; ADGM in Abu Dhabi has the FSRA. Virtual assets in Dubai are licensed by VARA.

In practice, a lawyer with only DIFC experience is not always ready for Arabic-language disputes in the Dubai courts, and a compliance officer from a CBUAE-regulated bank needs time to adjust to the DFSA rulebook. Experience under the right regulator is one of the strongest filters you can apply.

For legal roles: rights of audience in the onshore courts are generally limited to licensed advocates, and foreign lawyers usually work as legal consultants. If you need court work, that changes the profile and the candidate market from day one.

Certifications worth checking

  • Accounting and audit: ACCA, CPA, CMA, CA; signing auditors must be registered with the Ministry of Economy.
  • Tax: ADIT, CTA, UAE corporate tax courses; FTA-registered tax agent status is a plus.
  • AML and compliance: CAMS, ICA, CGSS; for an MLRO, direct regulator experience matters.
  • Risk and credit: FRM, CFA, PRM.
  • Insurance: CII; the broker or agency licence sits with the company.

A certificate proves the foundation, not the experience. We always ask candidates to show what they actually did: which returns they filed, how many goAML reports they prepared, what transaction volumes they handled.

Seasonality: when it is easier to hire

Demand for tax people peaks before corporate tax deadlines (nine months after the financial year end, so 30 September for calendar-year companies) and at every quarter end because of VAT. Auditors are fully booked from January to April. Banks and the Big Four pay annual bonuses in the first quarter, so the strongest candidates are most open to a move from March to May. Summer and Ramadan slow decisions down, so allow an extra week or two.

Common employer mistakes

  • One person for bookkeeping, tax and AML - these are three different skill sets.
  • Checking the degree and certificate but skipping a practical task.
  • No requirement for experience under the right regulator (CBUAE, DFSA, FSRA).
  • A salary band below market, so strong candidates drop out on the first call.
  • Slow approvals: a good tax specialist gets another offer within a week.
  • Reference checks done as a formality, although for finance roles they are essential.

Step-by-step hiring plan

  • 1. Role profile. First 90 days of work, regulator, transaction volume, system (Zoho Books, Odoo, SAP, Oracle) and languages, for example English plus Arabic or Russian for client-facing work.
  • 2. Budget. A market band plus the employer's visa and insurance costs - see our work visa guide.
  • 3. Shortlist. Three to five candidates with verified experience, not fifty CVs.
  • 4. Practical check. A case: fix an invoice with a VAT error, risk-rate a client under KYC, spot the exposure in a contract.
  • 5. References and verification. Calls to former managers and certificate checks in official registers.
  • 6. Offer and start. You agree the terms with the candidate directly; we advise on the market package. The wider process is in our guide on how to hire staff in Dubai.

Checklist before you start the search

  • The function is clear: accounting, tax, AML, risk or legal
  • The regulator and jurisdiction are clear: mainland, DIFC, ADGM, free zone
  • Must-have and nice-to-have certifications are listed
  • A market salary band and bonus are set
  • A practical task exists, and someone owns its review
  • The start date allows for the candidate's notice period (often 30-90 days)

FAQ

Does a small Dubai business need an in-house tax specialist?

If turnover is above the VAT registration threshold (AED 375,000) and the company is within corporate tax, you need at least an accountant who is confident with VAT and EmaraTax. A dedicated tax consultant makes sense for group structures, qualifying free zone entities or related-party transactions.

Which certifications matter for an AML specialist in the UAE?

Employers usually look for CAMS or ICA, and for an MLRO also direct experience with a regulator (CBUAE, DFSA or FSRA) and with goAML reporting. A certificate without hands-on suspicious transaction reporting is a weak signal.

How is hiring in DIFC different from the mainland?

DIFC has its own common law framework, its own regulator (the DFSA) and its own employment law. Candidates need experience in that regime, and the employer handles the visa and permit through DIFC.

How long does it take to hire a finance specialist?

We usually deliver the first shortlist in 2 days on average. After that it depends on how fast you interview and on the candidate's notice period, which in finance is often 30 to 90 days.
