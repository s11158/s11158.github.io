Doctor specialties we help hire in Dubai an overview by field

A clinic owner in Dubai rarely hires one doctor in isolation: a dental clinic needs a dentist, specialists and a hygienist, an aesthetic clinic needs an aesthetic doctor, a nurse injector and a dermatologist, a polyclinic needs a family doctor and several consultants. TLNT is a hiring consultancy and helps employers find and assess medical staff across all these fields. Below is the full map of specialties, grouped by field, with the role page for each one. Every role page shows the market salary estimate, the licence steps and what we test in a practical skills assessment.

How the specialties are grouped

Medical staff in Dubai work under licences issued by the health regulator of the emirate: DHA in Dubai, DOH in Abu Dhabi and MOHAP in the northern emirates. A doctor is licensed as a specialist in one field, and the clinic itself holds a facility licence that lists the services it may offer. For this reason we group roles by field of practice, not by job title only.

For every specialty the same logic applies. The doctor's documents go through DataFlow primary source verification, the regulator checks eligibility against its requirements, an assessment follows where it is required, and the licence is activated under the employing facility. The exact route and the time depend on the case: the country of training, the experience and whether the person already holds a UAE licence.

  • Dental market estimate: general dentist about 15,000 - 25,000 AED per month or a share of collections; specialists such as orthodontists, endodontists and implantologists about 30,000 - 50,000 AED and above

General, family and surgical medicine

Polyclinics and multi-specialty centres need a wide base of doctors and a set of consultants who see referred patients.

General and family medicine. General practitioner (/en/gp-doctor/), pediatrician (/en/pediatrician/), nurse (/en/nurse/), home care nurse (/en/home-care-nurse/), IV therapy nurse (/en/iv-therapy-nurse/), nutritionist (/en/nutritionist/).

Internal and surgical consultants. Cardiologist (/en/cardiologist/), endocrinologist (/en/endocrinologist/), gastroenterologist (/en/gastroenterologist/), neurologist (/en/neurologist/), allergist and immunologist (/en/allergist-immunologist/), urologist (/en/urologist/), ENT doctor (/en/ent-doctor/), ophthalmologist (/en/ophthalmologist/), orthopedic surgeon (/en/orthopedic-surgeon/), general surgeon (/en/general-surgeon/), anesthesiologist (/en/anesthesiologist/).

  • Surgical roles depend on the facility: a day-surgery or hospital licence allows what a small clinic cannot, so confirm the facility scope before you start the search

What to check before you start a search

The same questions save time for every specialty. Answer them before the first interview, because they decide the shortlist.

  • Facility licence: which services and specialties your clinic is licensed for, and whether the doctor's scope fits it
  • Licence status of the candidate: already licensed in Dubai, licensed in another emirate, or coming from abroad; each route has its own steps
  • Compensation model: fixed salary, share of collections or revenue, or a mix, and who owns the patient base
  • Equipment and support: a surgeon or an implantologist needs the right room, devices and assistant before the first patient
  • Language: a doctor who speaks fluent Russian, Arabic or English matches your patient flow; we treat language as a requirement, not as a nationality filter
  • Replacement plan: who covers the patients if the doctor leaves, and what the contract says about notice
