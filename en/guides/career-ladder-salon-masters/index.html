Training and career ladder for salon masters

Good masters leave when they see no next step. A simple ladder with clear criteria, higher prices for higher levels and a real education budget keeps them growing inside your salon.

Talk to us

Three levels with clear criteria

  • Junior: works under supervision, a limited service list, builds speed and quality; the salon fills their book
  • Senior: full service list, own regular clients, good rebooking and reviews, can train juniors
  • Lead or top master: sets technical standards, runs training, handles complex cases and new services
  • Move up by numbers and skills: rebooking rate, reviews, speed, technical check by the lead, not by years only

Price tiers and pay

Many salons charge more for senior and lead masters, so clients pay for the level and the master's income grows with it. Link the ladder to the commission plan: a higher level can mean a higher commission rate or a higher base. Write the criteria and the pay for each level in one document everyone can see.

Education budget

  • Set an annual budget per master or a share of the salon's revenue; the amount depends on your margin, so treat it as a decision, not a benchmark
  • Mix external courses with internal training run by the lead master
  • After a course, the master teaches the team or launches the new service within a set time
  • For expensive courses, some salons use a training agreement where part of the cost is repaid if the master leaves early; enforceability depends on the wording and the case, so have it reviewed by a UAE lawyer

Why it reduces turnover

Masters in Dubai often change salons for a better commission or a chance to grow. A visible ladder gives them a reason to stay, a clear answer when they ask "what next", and a path to higher income without moving. Review each master's level twice a year in a short one-to-one meeting.

FAQ

How do I create a career path for salon staff?

Define three levels (junior, senior, lead) with measurable criteria such as rebooking, reviews and technical checks, link each level to prices and commission, and review levels twice a year.

How much should a salon spend on staff training?

There is no universal figure. Set an annual budget per master based on your margin, combine external courses with internal training and require that new skills are shared or launched as services.

Does training reduce staff turnover in salons?

Yes, when it is part of a visible ladder with higher income at each level. Masters stay when they can grow inside the salon instead of moving to a competitor.
