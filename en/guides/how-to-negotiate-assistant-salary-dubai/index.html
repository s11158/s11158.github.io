How to negotiate an assistant salary in Dubai the market frame, package versus basic, how to present achievements, when to ask for a raise and what to say

Assistants are good at negotiating for other people and often bad at negotiating for themselves. The reasons are familiar: the job is about serving, the results are invisible, and nobody tells you what the market pays. This article from our School of assistants series fixes the three gaps. It gives the market frame by type of role as a market estimate, explains how a Dubai package is built and what the difference between basic and total means for you, shows how to turn an invisible job into a list of achievements, tells you when a raise request lands and when it does not, and gives scripts for the offer, the raise and the counter-offer. It also says plainly what employers can add to a package and what they cannot, so you ask for the right things.

Negotiation starts with knowing the range. Package versus basic: how a Dubai offer is built

In Dubai the number in the vacancy is usually the total package, and the contract splits it into basic salary and allowances. The split matters to you, so ask about it before you accept.

  • Basic salary: the fixed core; several contract-based calculations, including end-of-service gratuity, are usually tied to the basic, so a low basic with high allowances is not the same as a high basic with the same total; check how your contract defines it and the current rules on the MOHRE portal
  • Allowances: housing, transport, phone are the common ones; they are part of your monthly cash but are named separately
  • What the employer pays on top of the package: the work visa and its renewals, health insurance as required for employees, the gratuity provision under the contract and current UAE labour rules; these are the employer's obligations, not perks to negotiate away
  • What is often negotiable beyond the package: an annual flight ticket allowance, insurance cover for family members, a phone and laptop, a performance bonus with written criteria, an education budget, flexible or remote days, a title that reflects the real scope, a review date after probation
  • What an employer cannot do, whatever is said in the room: pay you something that is not in the written contract and expect it to count; a verbal promise of a raise after probation is a hope, a line in the contract or an offer letter is an agreement
  • What you cannot do: ask for a salary to be paid in a way the contract does not state, or treat the visa as a favour to repay; the employer sponsors the work visa for the person they hire, and candidates never pay anyone for a job
  • There is no personal income tax on salaries in the UAE, so the number you negotiate is close to the number you receive; cost of living, especially rent, is the real question, see our guide on moving to Dubai for work
  • Always ask for the offer in writing with the split shown, and read the contract clauses on hours, probation, notice and gratuity before you sign; our employment contract guide explains them

How to present achievements in an invisible job

An assistant's results are things that did not go wrong. To negotiate you must make them visible, with numbers where you can and with concrete stories where you cannot. Start the record today, whatever stage you are at.

  • Keep a monthly list of what you prevented, saved or built: a double booking caught, a supplier error of a given amount returned, a travel budget reduced, a renewal that would have been missed
  • Translate tasks into outcomes: not "managed the calendar" but "ran the calendar of a director with 25 meetings a week for three years with no missed commitment"
  • Quantify where honest: hours of the principal's time protected per week by your own estimate, number of vendors managed, size of the budgets you prepared for approval, number of trips per year
  • Show scope growth: the tasks you own now that you did not own a year ago; this is the strongest argument for a raise
  • Collect words from others: a thank-you from a partner, a note from the principal, a reference from a previous employer; quote them briefly
  • Bring proof of the market, not a demand: "The market estimate for this scope in Dubai is X to Y; I am at the lower end with an expanded scope"
  • Keep the list in your CV as well; our Dubai CV format guide and the free CV builder show how to structure it

When to ask for a raise, and when not to

Timing decides half the result. The same request lands or fails depending on the week.

  • Good moments: the end of probation if it was agreed as a review point; the annual review; right after your scope grew permanently; after a visible success the principal noticed; when a new title is being discussed
  • Bad moments: during a crisis in the business; right after your own mistake; in a corridor or by message; when the principal is tired or travelling; the day a colleague resigned
  • Ask for a meeting with a subject: "I would like 20 minutes to review my role and compensation", and give the principal a few days to prepare
  • Once a year is normal; twice in a year only when the scope changed radically
  • If you are new in the role, build the record for at least six months before asking, unless the role changed materially
  • If the answer is not now, agree the date and the criteria for the next conversation, in writing, by a short follow-up email

Scripts for the conversation

Short, calm, factual, with a number and a reason. Rehearse once out loud; assistants who draft these for their principals every day can do it for themselves.

  • At the offer stage: "Thank you, I am very interested. Based on the scope we discussed, the market estimate for this role in Dubai is X to Y, total package. I would be glad to accept at Z. Could you also show me the split between basic and allowances in the offer letter?"
  • Asking for a raise: "Over the last year my scope has grown from A to B (three concrete examples). I have kept the quality you expect (two results). The market estimate for this scope is X to Y, and I am at W. I would like to move to Z from next month."
  • Counter-offer when the figure is lower: "I appreciate the offer. The gap to the market frame for this scope is about N. Is there room to close it, or could we agree a review at six months with the criteria written into the offer?"
  • When the answer is no: "Understood. What would need to be true for this to change, and when can we review it? I will send a short note with what we agreed."
  • When another employer approaches you: do not use it as a threat; if you want to stay, say so: "I have been approached and I would prefer to stay. Can we review my package against the market before I answer them?"
  • When the principal asks what you want: name a number, never a range alone; a range without a preferred figure invites the lower end
  • After every conversation: a two-line email summarising what was agreed and the next date; this is the habit that turns a conversation into a result

Mistakes that cost assistants money

The same errors appear in almost every conversation about pay that goes badly.

  • Accepting the first number because saying thank you feels right; a calm counter is expected in Dubai and does not offend
  • Comparing yourself to a colleague instead of to the market frame; the colleague's package is not an argument, the scope is
  • Negotiating by message; pay is a conversation, then an email
  • Not asking about the basic and allowances split, then discovering it later in a contract-based calculation
  • Mixing the raise request with a complaint about the workload; those are two meetings, see our boundaries and burnout article
  • Expecting the principal to notice; the job is invisible by design, so the record is your responsibility
  • Taking a verbal promise as an agreement; if it matters, it goes into the offer letter or the contract

FAQ

How much salary should I ask for as an executive assistant in Dubai?

Use the market estimate as the frame: executive assistant 12,000 - 20,000 AED per month total package, 20,000 - 30,000 with a C-level executive or a private family office; personal assistant 5,000 - 10,000, senior 12,000 and above; administrative assistant 5,000 - 8,000, senior 8,000 - 11,000. Place yourself inside the range by scope, languages and references, name one number rather than a range, and ask for the split between basic salary and allowances in the offer letter.

How do I ask my boss for a raise as a personal assistant?

Collect six months of evidence first: how your scope grew, what you prevented or saved, a few quotes from people you worked with. Ask for a 20-minute meeting with a clear subject at a calm moment, not during a crisis or after a mistake. Present scope, results and the market estimate, name the figure you want and the date, and follow up with a two-line email. If the answer is not now, agree the criteria and the date for the next conversation in writing.
