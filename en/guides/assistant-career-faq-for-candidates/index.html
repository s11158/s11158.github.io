Assistant career: candidates' questions answered fifteen honest answers for people who hesitate

The same questions reach us from almost every person who considers becoming an assistant in Dubai, and they rarely get a straight answer from a vacancy text. We collected the fifteen most frequent ones and answered each in a few lines, honestly and without selling the profession to anyone. The figures are market estimates in AED per month, total package, basic salary plus allowances; the employer also pays the visa, health insurance and gratuity provision. This article is part of our School of assistants series; the linked guides go deeper on each topic.

Talk to us

About the profession itself

The first group of questions is about status: what the job is and what it is not.

  • Is being an assistant a dead end? No. It is one of the few jobs where a person without a management title sees the whole business from the inside in the first year. The ladder goes from administrative assistant to executive assistant, to a C-level or family office role and for some to chief of staff, with side moves into operations, office management, events, HR administration and clinic or salon management. A dead end is a job where nobody notices your work; an assistant's work is noticed daily by the person who matters most
  • Will I be a servant? Only if you agree to be one. A professional assistant manages time, communication, documents and projects, and agrees the boundaries of the role in the first week: what is in it, hours, reachability, rules for money and access. Personal errands are part of personal and hybrid roles and not part of executive roles, and this is agreed before the offer, not discovered after
  • Is it respected? In Dubai, yes, and increasingly so. Owners and executives know that a strong assistant is hard to find and expensive to lose. The respect is earned by competence: the assistant who runs a week without a single dropped loop becomes the person whose opinion is asked, including about people and decisions
  • Is it only for young women? No. Employers in Dubai hire assistants of both genders and of all working ages; what decides the offer is written English, the leader's other languages, tools, judgement and references. Mature candidates often win executive and family office roles precisely because of life experience and calm; men are common in executive assistant, chief of staff and house manager roles. Any vacancy that filters by age or gender instead of skills is a vacancy to skip

Getting in

The second group is about the entrance: experience, English, and where you can work from.

  • Can I start without experience? Yes, at the first step. Administrative assistant, coordinator, receptionist and junior personal assistant roles (market estimate 5,000 - 8,000) take people from hospitality, teaching, sales, admin and household management, if they show reliability, tools and written English. Our guide on starting without experience has a 30-day self-training plan and a first CV
  • What English level do I need? Business level in writing for every role, because you write on someone else's behalf. As orientation: B1 to B2 is workable for an administrative or personal assistant to a Russian-speaking principal, B2 strong for an executive assistant, C1 for C-level and chief of staff roles. Employers rarely ask for a certificate; they test on a writing task and a language switch mid-interview. Our guide on the English level for assistants has a 60-day plan
  • Can I do it remotely? Partly. Virtual assistant roles (3,500 - 8,000 part-time or remote, up to 14,000 full-time senior, orientation) are real, mostly for inbox, calendar, research and bookings. Executive, hybrid and household roles in Dubai are in person or hybrid, because the leader needs someone in the room and in the city. Many assistants start remotely and move to an in-person role later
  • Do I need a degree? No specific degree is required for most assistant roles; a family office or C-level role may prefer business, finance or law. What is checked instead: two years or more serving a demanding person or team in any field, references that confirm discretion, and a practical task at the interview

Money and time

The third group is about the numbers and the hours.

  • What will I earn? Administrative assistant 5,000 - 8,000, senior 8,000 - 11,000; personal assistant 5,000 - 10,000, senior 12,000 and above; executive assistant 12,000 - 20,000; executive assistant to C-level or a private family office 20,000 - 30,000; hybrid multi-assistant 10,000 - 18,000, senior 18,000 - 25,000; chief of staff 25,000 - 40,000 (orientation). Total package, basic salary plus allowances; visa and health insurance are paid by the employer. Salaries in the UAE are not taxed as personal income, which changes the comparison with other cities
  • How long does it take to grow? As orientation, one to two years at the first step, two to three years to a solid executive assistant role, and five years or more to a C-level, family office or chief of staff role. People move faster when they bring a specialism (finance, legal, medical administration) or when a leader's business grows with them. The ladder is about scope and trust, not years served
  • Will the boss call at night? Sometimes, in roles that support one person's whole life; rarely, in administrative roles. The difference between a good and a bad employer is not whether an emergency happens but whether reachability is agreed in advance: what counts as an emergency, which channel, and how the time is compensated. Agree it in the first week and write it down; working hours and rest follow the contract and current UAE labour rules
  • What about family? Many assistants in Dubai have families and children; executive and administrative roles usually keep office hours with occasional peaks around travel and events. Personal and hybrid roles need more flexibility. Say your constraints plainly at the interview; a role that cannot live with them is the wrong role, not a reason to hide them

The leader and the daily reality

The fourth group is about the person you will work with.

  • What if the boss is difficult? Separate demanding from disrespectful. Demanding leaders are the ones who teach you the most and pay the most; disrespectful behaviour after an honest conversation about boundaries is a reason to leave, and the Dubai market has many leaders who treat their assistant as a partner. Our guide on why assistants leave shows what good employers do differently
  • What if I make a mistake? You will, and the job survives it if you report it first and with a solution. A missed booking reported immediately, with two alternatives, is a normal Tuesday; the same mistake found by the leader is a trust problem. Our guide on mistakes that lose trust lists the ones that actually end careers, and they are about hiding, not about errors
  • Will I be stuck doing coffee and bookings? In a role defined that way, yes; in most roles, only for the first weeks while trust is built. The way out is to take one task the leader does not want to do, return it finished, and repeat. Our guide on the first 90 days shows how scope grows month by month
  • Is confidentiality a burden? It is the core of the role and the reason it pays. You will see money, health, family and deals, and you keep them inside, including from friends and future employers. In exchange you are trusted with decisions years before peers. Our guide on confidentiality, NDA and access explains the rules that protect both sides

Where to start tomorrow

Three practical steps if the answers above made the profession look right for you.

  • Write a CV in assistant language: whom you supported, what you owned, your tools and your languages with levels; our assistant CV guide and the free CV builder show the structure
  • Pick the first step honestly: administrative, coordinator or junior personal assistant if you have no assistant experience, executive assistant if you have run a demanding person's week before
  • Prepare proof: an itinerary template, a weekly summary format, two referees who can speak about reliability and discretion
  • Send your CV to us through the candidates page; we keep it on file and let you know when an employer has a matching vacancy. We cannot promise a job, and candidates never pay
  • Be careful with any offer that asks for a payment, deposit or fee from the candidate; a legitimate employer or consultancy does not charge you
