Assistant interview questions for Dubai a question bank and a practical skills assessment

A good interview for an assistant checks four things: judgement, reliability, communication and discretion. Questions about strengths and weaknesses rarely show any of them. Scenario questions and a short practical task do. Below is a question bank by block, a 30 to 45 minute skills assessment built from real assistant work, and the red flags to listen for. Use the same questions with every finalist so you can compare fairly.

Talk to us

Question bank: background and motivation

Open with facts, then move to behaviour. Take notes in the same format for every candidate.

  • Walk me through your last two roles: whom you supported, for how long and why you left
  • What did a normal week look like, and what was the hardest week you handled?
  • What is your notice period, visa status and expected package?
  • What are you looking for in the next role that your current one does not give you?
  • Which languages do you work in daily, and in which do you write most confidently?

Question bank: calendar, communication and judgement

These questions test how the person thinks when the situation is not in the manual.

  • Two senior people ask for the same time slot and your executive can only attend one. What do you do, step by step?
  • How do you decide which emails the executive must see today and which can wait?
  • Your executive is on a flight and an urgent decision is needed. What do you do?
  • Describe a time you pushed back on a request from your executive. How did you do it and what happened?
  • How do you write a polite refusal to a client on behalf of your executive?
  • Tell me about a mistake you made and how you fixed it before it caused damage

Question bank: confidentiality, tools and working style

Discretion and tools are the two areas where good-looking CVs hide the biggest gaps.

  • What kind of confidential information did you handle, and how did you protect it?
  • A colleague you like asks what your executive's schedule looks like next week for a personal reason. What do you say?
  • Which tools do you use daily: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, calendar sharing, a CRM or task tool, travel platforms? Show one example of a process you improved
  • How do you keep track of follow-ups so nothing is lost?
  • How do you handle working in a small team with several demanding people at once?

Practical skills assessment on real tasks

Run this for finalists only, with a clear time limit of 30 to 45 minutes, and tell the candidate in advance what it is. Do not use real confidential data; use made-up names and numbers.

  • Email triage: give 12 to 15 sample emails (an investor, a supplier invoice, a family request, a meeting change, a spam-like message). Ask for a priority order and a short written reply to the three most important
  • Calendar conflict: give a week with three overlapping meetings, a flight and a doctor appointment. Ask what the person would move, whom they would inform and the exact wording of one rescheduling message
  • Confidentiality scenario: a vendor calls and asks for your executive's home address and travel plans. Ask what the person says and does next
  • Travel plan: ask for a simple itinerary with times, visa and document reminders and one backup option
  • Score each task from 1 to 5 on accuracy, speed, tone and initiative. Compare the same tasks across finalists and keep the notes

Red flags and what to listen for

Not every flag is fatal, but several together should stop the process.

  • Speaks negatively about a former executive in detail, or shares their private matters in the interview
  • Vague answers about confidentiality, or comfortable sharing information casually
  • Cannot give a concrete example for the scenario questions and speaks only in general terms
  • Careless writing in the sample reply, errors in names, dates or amounts
  • Cannot explain gaps or very short tenures, and gives different answers to the same question
  • Strong signs of the opposite are a calm ranked order of tasks, an alternative offered when saying no, and asking good questions about how you work

FAQ

What questions should I ask in an executive assistant interview?

Ask scenario questions about calendar conflicts, prioritising an inbox, an urgent decision while the executive is travelling, a time the person pushed back, and how they protect confidential information. Add a short practical task so you see the work, not only the answers.

How do I test an assistant's skills before hiring?

Give the same 30 to 45 minute task to every finalist: email triage with a priority order and three written replies, a calendar conflict with a rescheduling message, and a confidentiality scenario. Use made-up data, tell the candidate in advance and score each task on accuracy, speed, tone and initiative.
