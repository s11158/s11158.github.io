Assistant for an investor or a wealthy family in Dubai family office assistant, lifestyle manager, house manager: one person or a small team

A private investor or a family that has moved its base to Dubai quickly collects moving parts: two or three properties with tenancy renewals and service charges, several cars, schools, a nanny and a driver on the family's visa, bankers who want documents, travel every month and a calendar that mixes a board seat with a school play. Nobody on the bank's or the lawyer's side runs this. This guide explains what hurts without a dedicated person, what a family office assistant, a lifestyle manager and a house manager each take over, when one person is enough and when you need a small team, what it costs, how to check people whose main qualification is discretion, and where the boundaries are.

A typical week without an assistant

The family is well organised in theory; in practice everything runs through one spouse's phone and a folder of PDFs nobody can find.

  • Monday: the tenancy of the second apartment renews in ten days; the Ejari, the cheque and the agent's call are all pending and the tenant is asking
  • Tuesday: the bank's compliance team wants updated documents for two accounts; the originals were scanned on three different phones
  • Wednesday: the car registration has lapsed, the driver cannot take the children to school in that car, and the insurance renewal was quoted twice at different prices
  • Thursday: the housekeeper's visa medical, the nanny's leave request and the gardener's unpaid month land on the same evening
  • Friday: a trip to Europe next week has no visas for the staff travelling with the family, and the private school wants a signed form before the holidays
  • Every week: a service charge paid late, a maintenance visit missed, a family member's appointment double-booked, and a quiet feeling that someone else should be doing all this

What the assistant takes over

Three roles share this work; one person can hold all three in a smaller household. Nothing here is investment advice or a decision: the assistant files, schedules, gathers documents and reminds. Agree the three lists in writing.

  • Business and family office: calendar of bankers, lawyers, accountants and board or fund meetings; documents gathered for KYC and compliance requests; statements and contracts filed so that any document is found in a minute; meetings minuted; the family's annual renewal calendar (visas, Emirates IDs, insurance, licences, memberships)
  • Properties: tenancy renewals, Ejari and cheques prepared for your signature, service charges, maintenance contractors, handovers, keys and access, a file per property
  • Cars and travel: registration, insurance quotes, service bookings, fines; flights, hotels, visas, charters where needed, itineraries that account for staff and children
  • Personal: schools and tutors, doctors, memberships, gifts, reservations, personal shopping within a rule
  • Household: the staff roster, visas and medicals, leave, salaries paid on your approval, suppliers and contractors supervised, inventory and household budget tracked; this is the house manager's core
  • Keep to yourself: investment decisions and instructions to bankers; transfers and signing; passwords and tokens; trusts, wills and family matters; conversations with children's schools about anything beyond logistics; staff dismissals

Which type fits and what it costs

A family office assistant works on the business and document side and reports to the principal or the family office head. A lifestyle manager runs travel, bookings, memberships and personal requests. A house manager runs the home, the staff and the contractors. One hybrid person is enough for one home, up to three or four household staff and one or two properties. A small team of two or three is needed with several properties, frequent travel with staff, five or more household employees, or when the principal wants the business side kept separate from the home. How to find and check

These people are rarely on job boards. They come through other families, family office heads, private banks' client teams, five-star hotel and private aviation operations, and from being a senior personal assistant to one principal for many years. Checking takes longer than for an office role; do not shorten it.

  • Discretion test: ask about previous principals; a good candidate describes the work and declines to describe the people; a bad one tells you where they live
  • Profession-specific task: a tenancy renewal, a car registration and a school trip form all fall in the same week the family flies to London with the nanny; ask for the plan, what needs your signature and in which order
  • Staff task: build a leave and cover roster for a housekeeper, a nanny and a driver over the summer, with the family away for five weeks and the house still needing care
  • Documents task: a bank asks for proof of address, source of funds documents and a company certificate; ask how they would organise the request, what they would never send without you and how they would store the files
  • References: speak to at least two previous principals or family office heads, and ask specifically about money handling, confidentiality and how the person left, see our reference checks guide
  • Red flags: name-dropping; social media that shows previous employers' homes or children; vague answers on who held the cards and keys; expects to be paid in cash; no written sample in English
  • Probation plan: month one, calendar and documents only, no money and no keys; month two, properties and cars; month three, staff and travel; a written scope and a limits sheet by day 90

Risks and boundaries

In a private household the risks are personal, not just commercial: safety of the family, the home and the children, money and reputation. Boundaries are part of the job description, not an insult to the candidate.

  • NDA and confidentiality clause before any access; a clause on social media and photographs; a clear rule on speaking to press, agents or anyone asking about the family
  • Background: identity, previous employment and police clearance where the law allows, references spoken to, not just emailed
  • Money: a card with a monthly limit, salaries of staff paid by you or the accountant on the assistant's list, no access to investment or main accounts, a monthly reconciliation reviewed by a second person
  • Keys and access: logged, duplicates counted, alarm and smart home codes changed when any staff member leaves
  • Dependence: one family file (documents, renewals, contracts, contacts) in a shared system you own; a monthly handover note; nothing critical only in the assistant's head or phone
  • Backup: who covers during leave, usually the house manager or a trusted travel adviser; decided before the first absence
  • Boundaries for the family too: hours, days off, a private phone that is not answered at night except by agreed rule; the people who stay are the ones whose limits are respected

FAQ

Does a private investor or a wealthy family in Dubai need an assistant?

If the family has more than one property, several household staff on its visa, regular travel and bankers or lawyers asking for documents, the admin already takes one person's time; today it is usually a spouse's. A family office assistant, a lifestyle manager or a house manager, or one hybrid person in a smaller household, takes this over and keeps one organised file of the family's affairs.
