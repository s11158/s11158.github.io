Office staff salaries in Dubai what an employer pays in 2026

A small company, clinic or family office in Dubai usually needs the same core office team: someone at the front desk, an office manager, an assistant to the owner, a PRO for visas and government paperwork, HR and a bookkeeper or accountant. This guide gives market estimates of monthly salaries for each role, explains what moves the number up or down and shows the cost on top of salary, so you can set a budget before you start the search.

What moves the number

Two candidates with the same job title can differ by 50% or more. These are the factors that usually decide where in the range a person sits.

  • Languages: Arabic for government work and Arabic-speaking clients, Russian for Russian-speaking owners and clients; a second working language usually adds to the offer
  • Scope: one person who is office manager, PRO and HR at once costs more than a coordinator, but less than three hires
  • Who they support: an assistant to the owner of a family business or to a C-level director is paid for trust and availability, not for typing speed
  • Local experience: a PRO or HR officer who already knows MOHRE, the immigration system and your free zone saves weeks in the first months
  • Systems: Zoho, Odoo, QuickBooks or a CRM the company already uses; a person who can run it from day one sits higher
  • Sector: clinics, real estate and finance often pay office roles more than small retail or trading companies

Basic salary and allowances

In the UAE the package is usually split into basic salary and allowances such as housing and transport. The split matters for the employer, because end-of-service gratuity is calculated from basic salary only.

  • Write basic salary, each allowance and any bonus or commission as separate lines in the offer and the contract
  • A very low basic salary next to large allowances is noticed by candidates and reduces trust in the offer
  • Commission for office roles is rare; if you pay a bonus, describe the rule in writing so both sides understand it
  • For mainland companies salaries are paid through the Wage Protection System (WPS), so the amounts in the contract and in payroll must match

The cost on top of salary

The monthly salary is not the full cost of the role. When you compare candidates, add these items to the budget.

  • Visa and Emirates ID: paid by the employer; the amount depends on the company type and the free zone, so check it with your PRO
  • Health insurance: in Dubai the employer must provide it; the premium depends on the plan and the person's age
  • Gratuity provision: about 5.83% of basic salary per month in the first five years, see our gratuity guide
  • Annual leave: 30 calendar days a year after the first year, plan who covers the front desk and the PRO work
  • Equipment, software licences and a desk; for an EA also travel and a phone if the role needs it

How to set an offer that candidates accept

Good office people in Dubai often have more than one offer. A clear, fair offer closes faster than a higher number that arrives late.

  • Decide the range before interviews, using the table above as a starting point and your own budget as the limit
  • Describe the job in tasks, not in titles: who the person supports, which systems, how many visas and employees
  • Ask for the current package split into basic and allowances, so you compare like with like
  • Agree the terms with the candidate yourself; a written offer within a few days after the final interview prevents losing the person
  • Plan probation and notice in the contract, see our guide to probation and notice in the UAE

FAQ

How much does an office manager earn in Dubai?

Market estimate: about 8,000 - 14,000 AED per month for an office manager who runs the office, 5,000 - 8,000 AED for an office administrator or coordinator and 14,000 - 18,000 AED for a senior office or admin manager. Languages and the scope of the job move the number.

What is a normal salary for an executive assistant in Dubai?

Market estimate: about 12,000 - 20,000 AED per month for an executive assistant to an owner or director, and 20,000 - 30,000 AED for an assistant to a C-level executive or a private family office, where trust, travel and confidentiality are part of the job.

Is it cheaper to hire one person for office, PRO and HR work?

In a small company one experienced office manager often covers office, PRO and basic HR, and costs less than three separate hires. Pay for the wider scope, check that the person really has PRO and HR experience in the UAE, and plan who covers the work during their leave.
