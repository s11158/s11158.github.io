Emiratisation for small clinics and salons: what to check

Owners of small clinics and beauty salons often hear about Emiratisation and are not sure whether it applies to them. The answer depends on the size of the company, its sector and its location, and MOHRE updates the targets and the contributions for missed targets. This guide shows what to check and how to plan; it does not quote quotas or amounts, so confirm the current rules with MOHRE before you decide.

What Emiratisation means for a private employer

Emiratisation is the UAE policy of increasing the number of UAE nationals working in the private sector. For employers it appears as targets set by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE), together with support programmes for Emirati jobseekers and for the companies that hire them.

  • Targets are set by MOHRE and apply by company size, sector and category of the role
  • Larger private companies have had annual targets for skilled roles for several years
  • The scope has been widened over time to reach some smaller companies in selected sectors
  • Support programmes such as Nafis exist for Emirati jobseekers and for employers, and their terms change

How to check whether it applies to your clinic or salon

Do not rely on a rule you heard from another owner. Check your own company against the current MOHRE guidance.

  • Count your employees as MOHRE counts them: the headcount on your establishment card and in the wage system, not only the people at the front desk
  • Check your trade licence activity and sector against the sectors named in the current MOHRE rules
  • Check whether you are mainland or in a free zone, because the rules can differ between them
  • Ask MOHRE or your PRO officer to confirm in writing what applies to your establishment and from which date
  • Keep the answer in your company file, so the next review is quick

Which roles suit Emirati hires in a clinic or a salon

Targets usually relate to skilled positions. In small facilities the roles where employers most often meet Emirati candidates are on the client-facing and administrative side, which is also where the language and local knowledge help.

  • Front desk, patient relations and clinic coordinator roles
  • Office, HR, PRO and insurance claims roles; see our guide on office staff salaries
  • Clinic operations and customer experience roles in a growing clinic
  • Licensed clinical roles for Emirati doctors and nurses where your facility can offer the training and the case mix

What to plan before you hire

An Emirati hire is an ordinary employment relationship with its own rules and benefits, so plan it like any other senior hire.

  • Define a real role with real tasks, a manager and a development path; a position that exists only on paper creates problems with MOHRE
  • Check the pay and benefits rules for UAE nationals with MOHRE and with your accountant before you set the offer, because they differ from those for expatriates
  • Use the usual contract, probation and notice rules, see our guides on the employment contract and on probation and notice period
  • Allow for a longer search for a specific profile, since the pool of candidates for a given role can be small
  • Where the job needs a licence from DHA, DOH or MOHAP, confirm the licence route first

FAQ

Does Emiratisation apply to a small clinic or beauty salon?

It can. The rules depend on the number of employees, the sector of the trade licence and the location, and MOHRE has widened them over time. Check your own company with MOHRE or your PRO officer and ask for the answer in writing.

Which roles are easiest to fill with an Emirati employee in a clinic?

Employers most often hire UAE nationals for front desk, patient relations, coordinator, office and HR roles. Licensed clinical roles are possible too, but they depend on the candidate's licence and on what your facility can offer in training.

Can TLNT promise an Emirati candidate or a MOHRE result?

No. We help you define the role, search and assess candidates, but we do not promise a candidate and we do not give legal advice on MOHRE targets. The decision to hire is yours.
