Clinic receptionist and coordinator salaries in Dubai: monthly pay and full cost

The front office decides whether a lead becomes a booked, insured and returning patient. This guide gives owners market estimates for receptionists, patient coordinators and front office managers in Dubai, how bonuses are usually built and what the role really costs the clinic per month.

Receptionist, coordinator or both

Owners often ask one person to do everything. It works in a clinic with one or two doctors; with more traffic the roles split, because answering the phone and selling a treatment plan need different skills.

  • Receptionist: calls, WhatsApp, booking, check-in, payments, eligibility check for insurance patients
  • Patient coordinator: consultations that turn into treatment plans, follow-up of patients who did not book, packages
  • Insurance desk: prior authorisations and claim follow-up, often a separate insurance officer in larger clinics
  • Front office manager: schedule, scripts, conversion and no-show numbers, the team's shifts and training

How bonuses are usually built

A fixed salary pays for the desk being covered. A bonus pays for results the person can influence. Keep the rules simple and written, and pay on numbers you can check in the CRM or the clinic system.

  • Receptionist: small monthly bonus for booking rate from calls and messages, low no-shows, review score
  • Patient coordinator: bonus or commission on treatment plans that were paid, not on consultations
  • Front office manager: quarterly bonus on conversion from lead to visit, no-shows and patient feedback
  • Avoid bonuses that reward pressure on patients or discounts given to close a sale

What the role costs the clinic

Salary is not the whole cost. When you compare candidates or plan a budget, count the full package for the year and divide by twelve.

  • Residence visa and work permit: sponsored by the employer; amounts depend on the case and the free zone or mainland setup
  • Health insurance: in Dubai the employer provides it for the employee
  • End-of-service gratuity under the UAE Labour Law, calculated from basic salary, so the split between basic and allowances matters
  • Annual leave of 30 calendar days per year after the first year, and cover for the desk while the person is away
  • Training on your clinic software, scripts and insurance rules, usually two to four weeks before full speed

What to check before you hire

A front desk mistake costs a patient or a rejected claim. Check the work itself, not only the CV.

  • A role play: a price question on WhatsApp, a patient who wants to cancel, an angry call
  • Clinic software and insurance: which systems they used and whether they checked eligibility themselves
  • Languages your patients actually speak, tested in the interview
  • References from a clinic manager about punctuality, accuracy with payments and patient feedback
  • Visa status: own visa, transferable or new sponsorship by your clinic

FAQ

How much does a clinic receptionist earn in Dubai?

Market estimate: about 4,500 - 7,000 AED per month for a receptionist and 7,000 - 10,000 AED for a senior receptionist with insurance experience. Languages, clinic software and eClaimLink experience move the figure up.

What is the salary of a patient coordinator in Dubai?

Market estimate: about 6,000 - 10,000 AED per month plus a bonus on paid treatment plans, and 10,000 - 15,000 AED plus bonus for a senior or medical tourism coordinator. A front office manager is usually 10,000 - 15,000 AED.

What does a clinic pay on top of the salary for a receptionist in Dubai?

The employer sponsors the residence visa and work permit, provides health insurance, pays the end-of-service gratuity under the UAE Labour Law from basic salary and covers annual leave. Visa amounts depend on the case, so count the full yearly package and divide by twelve.
