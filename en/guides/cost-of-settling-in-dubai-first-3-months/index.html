What it costs to settle in Dubai: the first 3 months one-off costs, who pays what, a sample starter budget and the savings to bring

Most anxiety about moving to Dubai is about money in the first weeks: the deposits, the cheques, the furniture, the moment before the first salary lands. The honest picture is simpler than the fear. The one-off costs are known in advance, several of them are paid by the employer, and the rest can be planned to the dirham before you say yes. This guide lists every landing cost of a single specialist who moves for a job (beauty master, nurse, assistant, sales), says who usually pays, gives a sample starter budget clearly marked as an orientation example and explains how much to have in savings as a runway. Figures are orientation ranges for autumn 2026; the way to check them yourself is in every section.

Who pays what: the employer's part and yours

Before counting your own money, separate the costs that belong to the employer. In a normal Dubai job offer the employer sponsors the work visa and the steps around it; the right to work is free for the candidate. Ask for this in writing in the offer letter.

  • Employer: work visa and residence permit, the medical fitness test and the Emirates ID application that are part of the visa process, health insurance for the employee (mandatory for employers in Dubai)
  • Employer, often: the flight to Dubai, a hotel or staff accommodation for the first days or weeks, sometimes a relocation allowance or a salary advance against the first rent; it varies by employer and role, so ask
  • You: your passport, degree attestation if the role requires it, personal documents and their translation; the employer or its PRO tells you which ones
  • You: rent and everything inside the home, transport, phone, food, personal spending until the first salary
  • Grey zone to clarify before signing: who pays for the flight back if either side ends the contract in probation, whether accommodation is provided or paid as an allowance, when the first salary is paid (end of the first month is common)
  • Where to check: the offer letter and the employment contract, our employment contract guide, and the current rules on MOHRE for what an employer may and may not charge an employee

A sample starter budget for one person

An orientation example, not a quote: a single specialist, a studio at 60,000 AED a year paid in 4 cheques, unfurnished, found through a broker. Orientation, autumn 2026, check current prices. Replace every line with your own numbers; the structure is what matters.

  • First rent cheque 15,000; security deposit 3,000; agency fee 3,000; Ejari a few hundred: about 21,300 for the home itself
  • DEWA deposit and connection about 2,100; internet installation and first month about 600; SIM 100: about 2,800 for utilities and connectivity
  • Simple furniture 5,000; first groceries and household 1,200; Nol card and first rides 150: about 6,350 to start living
  • Total one-off landing cost in this example: roughly 30,000 to 31,000 AED, of which 5,000 (security and DEWA deposits) comes back when you leave
  • Same person in a furnished room with monthly payment and bills included: no cheques, a deposit of about one month, no furniture, no DEWA; the landing cost drops to roughly 5,000 to 8,000 AED; many newcomers start this way for 3 to 6 months and move to their own studio after the first salaries
  • If the employer provides staff accommodation (common for some salon and clinic roles) or a housing allowance, the first block disappears or shrinks; ask exactly what is included, how many people share and where it is

Monthly costs while you wait for the first salary

The second number to plan is the monthly run rate between arrival and the first full salary, which is often paid at the end of the first month and sometimes later for the first cycle. Orientation, autumn 2026, check current prices.

  • Groceries for one person cooking at home: 800 to 1,500 a month depending on the store tier (budget supermarkets versus premium chains)
  • Transport by metro and bus with a Nol card: 200 to 400 a month; add taxi rides at 15 to 30 for a short trip if you use them
  • Phone plan 50 to 150; home internet 300 to 400 if not included in the rent
  • DEWA for a studio with sensible air conditioning: 200 to 500 a month, higher in summer; a municipality housing fee calculated from the annual rent is added to the DEWA bill monthly, check the current rate
  • Eating out and coffee: from 15 to 30 for a cafeteria meal to 60 to 120 per person in a mid-range restaurant; this line is the one that decides whether a month is cheap or expensive
  • A realistic monthly run rate for one careful person in a studio, excluding rent: 2,000 to 3,500; in a room with bills included, 1,500 to 2,500 plus the room

How much to bring: the savings runway

The runway is not a fixed number; it is a formula you fill in with your own choices. The rule is simple: one-off landing costs plus two to three months of living costs, so that a late first salary or an unexpected expense is an inconvenience and not a crisis.

  • Formula: landing costs (the table above with your choices) plus 2 to 3 times your monthly run rate including rent if you pay monthly
  • Room option in the example: about 7,000 landing plus 3 months of 4,000 to 5,000 all-in: roughly 20,000 AED in savings
  • Studio option in the example: about 30,000 landing plus 2 to 3 months of 3,000 excluding rent (the rent is already in the cheques): roughly 37,000 to 40,000 AED
  • If the employer provides accommodation for the first month and pays the flight, the runway can be a third of these figures; confirm in writing before you count on it
  • Keep the runway in a card that works abroad plus some cash in AED for the first days; open a local bank account as soon as the Emirates ID is issued, the employer's HR or PRO will tell you which documents the bank needs
  • The responsible decision: if the runway is not there yet, choose the room option, negotiate staff accommodation for the first months, or move the start date; a well-prepared move is the one that stays a good adventure

How to check these numbers yourself

Prices move, and your situation differs from any example. Check before you decide, not after you land; it takes an evening.

  • Rent: search Bayut, Property Finder and Dubizzle for the area and type you consider, filter by annual price and note how many cheques the listing asks for; read our guide on renting in Dubai for a newcomer
  • Deposits and fees: the Ejari fee and the DEWA deposit are published on the official Dubai REST app and the DEWA website; the agency fee and the security deposit are written in the listing or confirmed by the broker in writing
  • Groceries: fill a sample basket on Carrefour and Lulu online for your weekly list, then compare it with a premium chain; the difference is your choice, not a Dubai fact
  • Transport: the RTA website and the RTA app show the current Nol fares by zone and the monthly passes; taxi fares are on the RTA site too
  • Phone and internet: the two operators publish prepaid and postpaid plans; home internet is often bundled into a room or furnished rental, ask
  • Salary side: compare the offer with our salary index 2026 and the role salary guides so that the budget starts from a realistic income, not from a hope

FAQ

How much money should I bring to Dubai to start working?

As an orientation for autumn 2026: roughly 20,000 AED if you start in a furnished room with monthly payment, and roughly 37,000 to 40,000 AED if you rent your own studio with yearly cheques, in both cases including two to three months of living costs as a runway. If the employer provides accommodation for the first weeks and pays the flight, a third of that may be enough. Check current prices and your own offer before you decide.

Does the employer pay for the visa and medical insurance when I move to Dubai?

Yes, in a normal job offer the employer sponsors the work visa and residence permit, pays for the medical fitness test and the Emirates ID application that are part of the visa process and provides health insurance for the employee. Candidates do not pay for the right to work; ask for these points in writing in the offer letter and check the current employer obligations on MOHRE.
