Assistant for an owner of several salons or clinics in Dubai several branches, one right hand: what to delegate and what it costs

With one salon or clinic the owner still sees everything. With two, three or five branches the owner sees nothing in time: each manager reports in a different format, each location has its own licence and supplier calendar, hiring happens in parallel, and marketing asks for approvals from three places at once. Our guide on an assistant for a clinic or salon owner covers a single location; this page is about the owner of several. It explains what hurts without an assistant, what one person can take over across branches, where the line between an assistant and an operations manager runs, what it costs as a market estimate, how to check a candidate and how to limit the risks.

A typical week without an assistant

Multiplying locations multiplies small failures. A week of an owner with several branches, without an assistant, usually contains most of these.

  • Monday: three managers send weekly numbers in three formats, one is late, and the owner spends the evening building a table instead of reading it
  • Tuesday: the municipality or the health authority renewal for one branch is discovered two weeks before the deadline because it lived in that manager's phone
  • Wednesday: two branches interview candidates for the same role with different salary ranges, and the stronger candidate accepts elsewhere while the owner is asked to decide by WhatsApp
  • Thursday: the marketing agency waits a day for an approval of a post, a supplier of one branch is paid twice, a supplier of another is not paid at all
  • Friday: the owner drives between locations to sign papers and collect documents that could have been collected by one person on one route
  • Weekend: the family schedule is rebuilt again around a staff shortage in one salon, and the owner's own medical appointment moves for the third time

What the assistant takes over

An assistant to a chain owner is the owner's single point of coordination, not a boss of the managers. Write the split of business, personal and household tasks and the limits for each.

  • Business: one reporting format for all branches, reports collected on time, a short weekly summary for the owner with the open issues and the numbers that changed
  • Business: a supplier and licence calendar per branch: trade licence, health or municipality permits, staff licences where applicable, inspections, lease dates, insurance; documents collected for the PRO or the consultant who files them
  • Business: hiring coordination: one vacancy text and one salary range per role for all branches, interview scheduling, collecting managers' feedback, reference calls on the owner's checklist; the owner and the managers decide
  • Business: marketing coordination: a content and promotion calendar for all branches, approvals routed to the owner once a day, agency and photographer scheduling, collecting consent for photos; claims and posts follow the regulator's advertising rules
  • Business: the owner's meetings with managers, partners, landlords, the accountant and advisers, with agendas and follow-up lists
  • Personal and household: the owner's calendar and travel, family appointments, home and car maintenance, coordination with household staff
  • What should NOT be delegated: clinical and treatment decisions in clinics, signing leases and contracts, money transfers without a written rule, salary and commission decisions, dismissals and confidential HR matters, final answers to patient or client complaints

Which type fits and what it costs

With several branches the owner usually needs an executive assistant or a hybrid multi-assistant, not an administrative one. When the branches need daily operational control, that is an operations manager or a chief of staff, a different role next to the assistant, not instead of it. Salaries are a market estimate in AED per month, total package, basic salary plus allowances; the employer also pays visa, health insurance and gratuity provision.

How to find and check

Candidates come from executive assistant roles in multi-site businesses, salon or clinic operations coordination, franchise support offices and hospitality groups. Test on tasks that cross branches.

  • Email and WhatsApp triage: 15 messages from managers, a landlord, the marketing agency, a candidate, the PRO and the family; sort by urgency, draft three replies
  • Calendar conflict: a municipality inspection in one branch, an interview in another and a partner meeting at the same hour; what moves, who is told, what the owner must attend in person
  • Profession-specific task: build a renewal and inspection calendar for three branches from a messy list and a one-page weekly report template that all managers can fill in ten minutes
  • Numbers and judgement: three managers' reports with inconsistent definitions of "revenue" and "no-show"; the person has to spot it and propose one definition
  • Red flags: wants to manage the managers, promises to "handle the licence" or the authority alone, no questions about approval limits, talks loosely about a previous employer's numbers
  • Probation plan: weeks 1-2 calendars and the reporting format, weeks 3-4 licence and supplier calendars per branch, weeks 5-8 hiring and marketing coordination with a weekly summary, then a written scope and limits
  • Candidates can send a CV through our candidates page; owners describe the business to us in one message

Risks and boundaries

An assistant to a chain owner holds the whole picture: revenue by branch, salaries, supplier terms, candidates and the family's life. The boundaries protect both sides.

  • Confidentiality: a clause in the employment contract or an NDA covering numbers, salaries, supplier terms, candidates, patient and client data and family matters; patient data only in the systems where it belongs
  • Access: role-based access to reporting, calendars and the shared mailbox; a password manager; bank access read-only or none, approvals and transfers with the owner or the finance person
  • Money: a written rule for what the assistant may order or pay, by branch and by month, with a weekly summary
  • Authority: managers report to the owner, not to the assistant; the assistant asks, collects and reminds, decisions are visibly the owner's
  • Dependence on one person: a handover file per branch with licences, dates, suppliers, contacts and recurring tasks; a manager or the accountant as backup for leave and sickness
  • Growth: when the assistant's week is full of operations, hire an operations manager or a chief of staff instead of stretching the assistant

FAQ

Does an owner of several salons or clinics in Dubai need an assistant?

Usually yes, from the second or third branch: reports arrive in different formats, each location has its own licence, inspection and supplier dates, and hiring and marketing need one point of coordination. An executive or hybrid assistant gives the owner one picture and one calendar. When the branches need daily operational control, that is an operations manager or a chief of staff, a separate role.

Market estimate: an executive assistant 12,000 - 20,000 AED per month, a hybrid multi-assistant who also covers the owner's personal and household tasks 10,000 - 18,000 AED, senior 18,000 - 25,000 AED; a chief of staff is an orientation 25,000 - 40,000 AED.
