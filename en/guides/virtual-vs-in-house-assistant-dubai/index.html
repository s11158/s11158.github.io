Virtual or in-house assistant in Dubai cost, risks and which one fits

A virtual assistant works remotely, often for fixed hours or a set of tasks. An in-house assistant sits in your office or travels with you and is employed directly by your company. Both can be right, for different businesses. This guide compares what each does well, what they cost, how confidentiality and tools differ, where the risks are and how to decide.

What each option actually does

The difference is not only location. It is scope, availability and how much the person can act on your behalf.

Cost comparison

Costs below are market estimates and orientation ranges. Compare the full cost of each option, not only the monthly number.

  • Virtual: the fee for hours or tasks; often no visa, insurance or gratuity provision for you, depending on the arrangement, so check how the person is engaged
  • In-house: salary plus visa and Emirates ID, health insurance, gratuity provision, annual leave and a desk and equipment, see our cost of hiring guide
  • Compare cost per useful hour: a cheaper virtual assistant who covers 15 hours a week may cost more per task than a full-time employee at 40 hours
  • Consider the cost of your own time: a weak handover to a remote person can eat the savings

Confidentiality and tools

Whoever you choose, an assistant sees your email, calendar and often your finances and private life. Treat access as a decision, not a default.

  • Give access by role: a shared inbox or delegate access to the calendar instead of your personal passwords
  • Use a password manager and two-step verification; never share one login among several people
  • Put confidentiality in writing: a clause in the employment contract for an in-house person, a written agreement for a remote one
  • Decide what stays with you: bank approvals, signing and sensitive documents
  • Common tools: a shared calendar, a task board such as Notion or Trello, a team chat, a document store and a password manager

Risks to plan for

Both options fail in predictable ways. Knowing them early makes the choice easier.

  • Remote: slower response outside agreed hours, mismatched time zones, harder to build trust, no help with physical tasks
  • Remote: the status of a person who works from abroad or as a freelancer is a question for the employer to check; this guide does not give legal advice on it
  • In-house: higher fixed cost, leave and sick cover, a single point of failure if the person leaves
  • Both: unclear tasks. Write a one-page list of what the person owns and what they must ask you about
  • Both: dependence on one person. Keep a handover document of logins, contacts and recurring tasks

When each fits

A simple rule of thumb, adjust it to your business.

  • Virtual fits when you need relief with diary, inbox, bookings and research, up to part-time volume, and nothing needs a physical presence
  • In-house fits when guests, originals, couriers, errands, the office and in-person support are a daily part of the job
  • In-house fits when the assistant is the owner's right hand with access to private and financial matters and must be available at short notice
  • A mix works too: an in-house assistant for the office and a virtual one for overflow or research
  • If you are unsure, start with a defined list of tasks and test it on a short project before deciding the format

FAQ

Is a virtual assistant cheaper than an in-house assistant in Dubai?

The monthly number is often lower, as an orientation 3,500 - 8,000 AED against 5,000 - 14,000 AED for an in-house admin or owner's assistant, but the scope is narrower. Compare cost per useful hour and the full employment cost of an in-house role, including visa, insurance and gratuity provision.

Can a virtual assistant handle confidential information?

Yes if you control access: use delegate access or a shared inbox, a password manager and two-step verification, and put confidentiality in writing. Keep bank approvals and signing with yourself. For a person who works remotely, check the arrangement before you sign.
