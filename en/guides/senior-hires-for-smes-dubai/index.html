Senior hires for SMEs in Dubai CFO, COO, country manager, head of sales

The first senior hire changes a small company more than any other. A good CFO, COO, country manager or head of sales takes a whole area off the owner's desk; a wrong one costs months, money and sometimes key people. This guide is for founders and managing directors of SMEs in Dubai: when a full-time senior hire makes sense and when a fractional or interim option is better, how to write the brief, how to assess with a case, references and a 100-day plan, how to keep a search confidential when someone is still in the role, and how to onboard so the person delivers.

Full-time, fractional or interim

A senior title is not always a full-time job. Start from the problem you need solved and how long it will take, then choose the format.

  • Full-time makes sense when the area needs daily ownership for years: a CFO when there are several entities, investors or a bank to report to; a COO when delivery, suppliers and teams need one owner; a country manager when a foreign company opens or grows a UAE entity; a head of sales when revenue depends on a team, not on the founder
  • Fractional (a few days a month) fits when the need is real but not daily: a fractional CFO to set up reporting, budgets and the relationship with auditors while a financial controller or accountant does the daily work
  • Interim (full-time for a fixed period) fits a gap or a project: a departure, a restructuring, a system change or a market launch with a clear end date
  • A strong second-line hire is often better than a premature senior one: a financial controller before a CFO, an operations manager before a COO, a sales manager before a head of sales
  • Whatever the format, write down what the person must own and decide, and what stays with the owner

How to write a senior brief

Candidates at this level read a brief as a signal of how the company is run. A vague brief attracts generalists; a specific one attracts people who have solved your problem before.

  • Why the role exists now: the problem in one or two sentences (growth, a second market, investor reporting, the owner stepping back)
  • Outcomes for the first year and for the first 100 days, in business terms, not duties
  • Scope: entities, markets, team size and who reports to the person, budget authority
  • Who they report to and who else decides: founder, board, investors, a parent company abroad
  • Must-have experience (for example, a CFO who has prepared a company for a bank or investor process; a head of sales who has built a team, not only sold) versus nice-to-have
  • Budget as a range for the full package, checked against the role pages and the Salary Index before the search, and the terms you are open to discuss
  • Confidentiality level and the interview steps with dates

How to assess a senior candidate

Interviews alone favour people who present well. At senior level the assessment should show judgement on your real situation.

  • A case on your business: a short, anonymised pack (numbers, org chart, a current problem) and a presentation of what they would do and in what order
  • A 100-day plan: what they would learn in the first weeks, which quick wins they would aim for, which decisions they would not take before they understand the business
  • Structured references: former managers, peers and people who reported to them, with the same questions about results, decisions under pressure and how they leave a team
  • A meeting with the people who will work with them most: the founder, a key team member, sometimes a board member or investor
  • For a CFO or financial controller: how they would set up management reporting and cash control, and how they work with auditors and tax advisers in practice; for a head of sales: a pipeline review and a hiring plan for the team
  • Check the fit with the owner honestly: the most common reason a senior hire leaves an SME is a disagreement on who decides what

Confidential search while someone is in the role

Sometimes the owner needs a new senior person while the current one is still working. This needs care for both sides and for the business.

  • Keep the circle small: decide who knows and how information is shared
  • Use an anonymised brief in the first contacts and name the company only to shortlisted candidates who have agreed to confidentiality
  • Plan the handover before the new person starts: access, client relationships, open projects, bank and signatory matters
  • Treat the current employee fairly and according to the contract and the law; take advice from your lawyer or HR on the steps, see our guide to employment contracts
  • Avoid public job ads for the exact role until the change is announced

Onboarding a senior hire

Senior people fail in the first months more often from a weak start than from weak skills. Plan the first 100 days together, before day one.

  • Access and introductions in the first week: systems, bank, key clients, suppliers, advisers, the team
  • A written agreement on decisions: what they decide alone, what with the owner, what stays with the owner
  • Agreed first targets for the first 100 days, reviewed in a short meeting every week or two
  • Time with the founder: a regular slot, not only when something goes wrong
  • A clear probation review with the criteria agreed at the start; see our guide to probation and notice in the UAE

FAQ

Does an SME in Dubai need a full-time CFO?

Not always. A full-time CFO makes sense when there are several entities, investors or a bank process and finance needs daily senior ownership. Many SMEs start with a financial controller or accountant for the daily work and a fractional CFO a few days a month for reporting, budgets and auditors.

How do I assess a COO or head of sales before hiring?

Give finalists a short anonymised case on your business and ask for a presentation and a 100-day plan, then run structured reference calls with former managers, peers and people who reported to them. Add a meeting with the people they will work with most and agree honestly who decides what.

Can I search for a new manager confidentially while the current one is still working?

Yes, with care: keep the circle small, use an anonymised brief in first contacts, name the company only to shortlisted candidates who agreed to confidentiality and plan the handover in advance. Treat the current employee according to the contract and take advice from your lawyer or HR on the steps.
