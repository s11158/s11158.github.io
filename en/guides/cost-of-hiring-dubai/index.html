What it costs to hire in Dubai

What the cost of hiring is made of for the employer — so you can budget upfront.

Salary isn't the only line. By law the employer pays for the employee's onboarding and may not deduct it from their pay. Here's what the budget is made of.

Cost components

  • Salary — the largest, recurring item; depends on role and market (there's no single expat minimum)
  • Work permit / labour card (MOHRE)
  • Entry permit and residence visa
  • Medical fitness test
  • Emirates ID
  • Mandatory health insurance (required in Dubai and Abu Dhabi)
  • Optionally — an agency fee (usually a % of salary)

End-of-service gratuity

By law — 21 days of basic salary per year for the first 5 years, then 30 days per year thereafter; calculated on basic salary (excluding allowances) and capped. It's a future but real line worth keeping in mind.

About the numbers: specific fees and insurance premiums depend on company category, emirate, employee age, plan and year — these are estimates, not fixed tariffs. An exact budget is best calculated for your case; we'll help estimate it.

Frequently asked questions

What makes up the cost of hiring in Dubai?

It usually includes the work permit and visa, medical test and Emirates ID, health insurance, and other legally required employer costs. Exact amounts depend on company type, emirate and role, and are best calculated per case.

Can visa costs be deducted from the employee's salary?

No. Under UAE labour law the employer bears the cost of the work permit and visa; these cannot be passed to the employee. It's important to budget for this in advance.
