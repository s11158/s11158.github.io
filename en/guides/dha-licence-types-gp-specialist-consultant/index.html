DHA licence types GP, specialist, consultant, nurse, allied health

In Dubai the job title on the contract is not the same as the licensed title. DHA assigns the title from the qualification, the tier of that qualification and the years of experience, and the professional may only practise within it.

Talk to us

The main categories

  • Physicians: General Practitioner, Specialist, Consultant (plus Intern and Resident inside approved training programmes)
  • Dentists: General Dentist, Specialist, Consultant
  • Nurses and midwives: Registered Nurse, Assistant Nurse, Registered Midwife
  • Allied health: physiotherapists, radiographers, lab technologists, pharmacists, dental hygienists, beauty therapists, laser hair reduction technicians and many other titles
  • Traditional, complementary and integrative medicine (TCIM) practitioners

How the title is decided

The rules are the Unified Healthcare Professional Qualification Requirements (PQR), shared by DHA, DOH Abu Dhabi, MOHAP and the Sharjah Health Authority. Qualifications are grouped into Tier 1, 2 and 3 by country and issuing body. The figures below are from the April 2025 edition for non-UAE nationals; check the current edition before applying.

  • General Practitioner: medical degree of at least five years of study, a one-year internship and two years of clinical experience. Without evidence of internship, two more years of experience are required
  • Specialist: a recognised specialty qualification (generally a full-time programme of at least three years). Tier 1 and Tier 2: no further experience required. Tier 3: three years of experience after the main specialty
  • Consultant: Tier 1 needs two years after the main specialty, Tier 2 five years. A Tier 3 qualification normally cannot lead to the Consultant title
  • Experience must be hands-on, licensed and paid clinical work. Teaching and non-clinical roles do not count
  • A gap in practice over three years adds CME and supervised training; over five years a non-UAE national generally cannot apply

Nurses and allied health

  • An Assistant Nurse cannot work independently and works under a Registered Nurse
  • Nurses and midwives need a valid BLS certificate as a minimum
  • Assistant allied professionals work under a licensed allied professional of the same field
  • A beauty therapist working inside a clinic holds a DHA allied title, and aesthetic procedures are limited by the DHA non-surgical cosmetic standard

What it means for the employer

  • Match the licensed title and specialty to the job: a GP cannot be used as a dermatologist, even with aesthetic courses
  • Check the title and status of the registration or licence before the offer
  • A part-time licence at a second facility needs the approval of the full-time facility's medical director
  • Upgrading a title (for example GP to Specialist) is a separate application with its own requirements

FAQ

What is the difference between a specialist and a consultant in Dubai?

Both hold a recognised specialty qualification. The Consultant title needs more experience after it (two years with a Tier 1 qualification, five with Tier 2 under the April 2025 PQR) and is normally not available with a Tier 3 qualification. DHA assigns the title; the doctor does not choose it.

Which licence does a nurse need in Dubai?

A DHA licence under a nursing title, usually Registered Nurse or Assistant Nurse. An Assistant Nurse works under a Registered Nurse. The title depends on education and experience under the PQR.

Can a GP do aesthetic injections in Dubai?

Only under the DHA non-surgical cosmetic standard: at least two years of clinical experience before cosmetic training, a recognised in-person training certificate, one year of mentorship under a dermatologist or plastic surgeon, and privileges granted by the facility.
