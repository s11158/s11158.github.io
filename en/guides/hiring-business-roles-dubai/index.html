Hiring for business roles in Dubai finance, sales, marketing, tech and operations

A growing company in Dubai rarely hires one person at a time by plan. Usually the owner covers too much, something breaks (cash flow, a key client, a website, a visa deadline) and a hire is made in a hurry. This guide is a practical map for founders and managing directors: which business roles usually come in which order, how to assess each family of roles with a real work sample instead of a conversation, the mistakes that cost the most and how long to plan for each search.

Which roles a growing company hires, and in what order

There is no single correct sequence, but most small and mid-size companies in Dubai follow a similar path. The rule of thumb: hire first for the work that the owner does badly or that blocks revenue, not for the title that sounds most senior.

  • Stage one, the owner does everything: an office manager or executive assistant to take admin and calendar, a bookkeeper or accountant to keep the books and VAT records clean, a PRO officer (often part-time or shared) for visas and renewals
  • Stage two, revenue depends on the owner: a sales executive or business development manager to open new accounts, a customer service agent to keep existing clients answered, a social media manager or marketing manager to bring leads
  • Stage three, processes start to break: an operations manager to own delivery and suppliers, an HR manager or HR business partner for contracts, onboarding and policies, a CRM administrator to keep sales and service data in one place, an IT support specialist
  • Stage four, the owner steps back from daily management: a finance manager or financial controller, a head of sales or sales manager, a general manager, COO or country manager, a chief of staff who runs the owner's agenda
  • Specialist roles at any stage when the business needs them: legal counsel, compliance officer, data analyst, product manager, software developer, ecommerce manager, logistics coordinator

How to assess each family of roles

An interview tells you how a person talks about their work. A short work sample shows how they do it. For every business role choose one task that looks like a real week in your company, give clear instructions and the same time limit to every finalist, and score it against criteria written before you see the answers.

  • Finance and accounting (accountant, financial analyst, financial controller, CFO): a month-end file with errors to find, a cash flow forecast from raw data, a short memo on how they would organise VAT and corporate tax records in practice
  • Sales and account management (sales executive, key account manager, business development manager): a role-play of a first call or a difficult renewal with someone playing your client, plus a written follow-up email and a simple pipeline plan for the first quarter
  • Marketing (marketing manager, digital marketing manager, performance marketing specialist, content manager): a critique of your current website and ads, a one-page plan for a launch with channels and how success is measured, or a sample post and landing page text
  • Tech and data (software developer, data analyst, business analyst, product manager, IT manager): a small code or data task close to your stack, a written requirements document from a messy brief, or a walkthrough of a past project with the decisions they made
  • Operations and HR (operations manager, project manager, logistics coordinator, HR manager, HR business partner): a process map of one workflow with the bottlenecks marked, a supplier or shipping problem to solve, an onboarding plan for a new hire
  • For every role: structured reference calls with former managers, with the same questions for each candidate

Typical mistakes when a company hires business roles

Most bad business hires in Dubai do not fail on skills. They fail because the role was not defined, the wrong person decided, or the offer took too long.

  • Hiring a title instead of a job: "we need a marketing manager" when the real need is someone to run paid ads every day
  • One person for three different jobs (sales, account management and marketing at once), then disappointment that none of them is done well
  • No work sample, so the most confident speaker wins over the strongest doer
  • Several decision makers who never agreed on the profile, so finalists get contradictory interviews and drop out
  • A budget that was not checked against the market before the search; look at the role pages and the Salary Index first
  • Slow feedback and a late offer: good business candidates in Dubai usually talk to more than one employer
  • No onboarding: a senior person left alone in the first month with no access, no introductions and no clear first targets

How long to plan

Time depends on how specific the role is, how many people decide and how fast you give feedback. Plan the search as a project with dates, not as an open vacancy.

  • Common roles with a wide market (sales executive, accountant, customer service agent, office manager): plan a few weeks from brief to accepted offer when the brief is clear and interviews happen quickly
  • Specialist and manager roles (financial controller, digital marketing manager, HR business partner, project manager): plan more time, because the shortlist is smaller and finalists often have a notice period with their current employer
  • Senior roles (CFO, COO, country manager, head of sales): plan the longest, include a case presentation and reference calls, and keep the search confidential if needed
  • Candidates from abroad: add time for the move and the visa that the employer sponsors; see our guide to hiring from abroad
  • Book interview slots with every decision maker before the search starts; this one step usually saves the most time

Where to start this week

If you are not sure which role to hire first, write down the tasks that take most of your own time, the tasks that block revenue and the tasks that create risk if nobody owns them. The overlap is usually the first hire.

  • Open the role page for the job you have in mind and compare the duties with your list
  • Check the market range for the role in the Salary Index before you name a budget
  • Write a one-page job brief with the outcomes for the first 90 days; see our job brief template
  • Decide who makes the final decision and who only gives an opinion
  • Prepare the work sample before the first interview, not after

FAQ

What business roles should a small company in Dubai hire first?

Usually the work that the owner does badly or that blocks revenue: an office manager or executive assistant for admin, a bookkeeper or accountant for clean books and VAT records, and then a sales executive or business development manager. Operations, HR and finance management come when processes start to break.

How do I assess a sales or finance candidate beyond the interview?

Give a short work sample that looks like a real week in your company: a role-play of a client call and a follow-up email for sales, a month-end file or cash flow forecast for finance. Score every finalist against the same criteria written in advance and add structured reference calls.

How long does it take to hire a manager for a business in Dubai?

It depends on the role, the number of decision makers and how fast you give feedback. Common roles can be closed in a few weeks with a clear brief; specialist and senior roles take longer because the shortlist is smaller and finalists often have a notice period. Plan the search as a project with dates.
