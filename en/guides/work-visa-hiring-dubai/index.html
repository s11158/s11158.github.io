Work visa & permit when hiring

Who sponsors, the stages an employee goes through, and how mainland differs from a free zone — briefly, for the owner.

For an employee to work legally in the UAE, a work permit (labour card) and a residence visa are needed. The employer, as sponsor, arranges and pays for them.

Who's responsible

  • The business is the sponsor: it initiates and pays for the permit and visa
  • It needs a valid company licence, an establishment card and a visa quota
  • By law these costs can't be deducted from the employee's salary

Stages (for a candidate from abroad)

  • Work permit / quota at MOHRE
  • Entry permit → entry
  • Medical fitness test at an approved centre
  • Biometrics and Emirates ID
  • Residence visa and contract registration

If the candidate is already in the UAE on a visit visa, a status change without leaving the country is often possible.

Mainland vs free zone

Mainland: processed via MOHRE + immigration, work across the country. Free zone: handled by the zone, work usually within it. The number of visas is typically tied to office space.

Exact fees, timelines and quotas depend on company category, emirate and year — confirm via official sources. We'll give an estimate for your case and help find a candidate whose paperwork goes more smoothly.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between hiring in mainland and free zone?

On the mainland, visas are handled by MOHRE plus immigration (GDRFA/ICP), and the employee can work nationwide. In a free zone, the zone handles processing and work is usually limited to that zone. Visa quotas are often tied to office size.

What steps does a work visa go through?

Typically: offer → MOHRE work permit and quota approval → entry permit → arrival and medical test → biometrics and Emirates ID → residency visa → contract registration with MOHRE. Salary is then paid through the WPS system.

Is medical insurance mandatory for an employee?

Yes, health insurance is a requirement for a UAE residency visa and is provided by the employer. Coverage requirements vary by emirate, so it's best to check the current rules.
