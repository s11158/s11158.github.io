English level for an assistant in Dubai what each role really needs, how employers test it and how to prepare in 60 days

"My English is B1, is that enough?" is the question that stops more people from applying than any other. The honest answer is: it depends on the role, and less on grammar than most candidates think. An administrative assistant to a Russian-speaking owner and an executive assistant to a C-level executive in an international company need different English, and both are real jobs in Dubai. This article from our School of assistants series explains what level each assistant type actually needs, how employers test it (rarely with a certificate), how to prepare in 60 days, what the mixed-language reality of Russian-speaking employers looks like, why Arabic is a plus and which phrases carry most of the daily work. Levels are given on the CEFR scale (B1, B2, C1) as orientation, not as a rule.

What level each assistant type really needs

English in this profession is mostly written and mostly on someone else's behalf: your email is the principal's email. That is why writing matters more than accent and why a short, correct message beats a long fluent one. The table gives orientation levels; a specific employer may ask for more or accept less.

Assistant typeOrientation levelWhat you actually do in English
Administrative assistant (team or office)B1 strong to B2Emails to suppliers and colleagues, phone calls, documents, data entry, visitors; templates cover most of it
Personal assistant to a Russian-speaking principalB1 strong to B2Bookings, clinics, schools, landlords, government portals, vendors; the principal speaks Russian with you
Personal or lifestyle assistant to an international principalB2Daily spoken and written communication with the principal and their circle, restaurants, travel, events
Executive assistantB2 strongWriting in the principal's voice to partners, banks and authorities, meeting notes, calendar negotiation with other assistants, calls
Executive assistant to C-level or a family officeC1Board papers, sensitive correspondence, legal and financial vocabulary, negotiation by email, presentations
Virtual assistantB2 (depends on clients)Written communication almost entirely: inbox, research summaries, bookings, social media admin
Chief of staffC1Leading meetings, writing strategy notes, representing the principal with partners and the team

How employers test English

Employers in Dubai almost never ask for an IELTS or TOEFL certificate for an assistant role. They test on the job, in the interview, in four predictable ways.

  • The CV and the first message: a cover note with mistakes in the first two lines ends the process before it starts; have both proofread
  • A writing task: a rescheduling message, a polite refusal, a thank-you note on the principal's behalf, usually 10 to 15 minutes; short, correct and warm wins
  • A language switch mid-interview: the interviewer moves from Russian or Arabic to English without warning and watches whether you keep the same confidence and structure
  • A phone or video call in English about logistics: book this, change that, explain why; they listen for clarity and for whether you confirm what you heard
  • A reading check: a contract clause, an invoice, a government letter, and the question "what does this mean and what should we do"
  • What they forgive: an accent, a missing article, a slower pace. What they do not forgive: a message that could embarrass the principal, a misunderstood instruction, a promise to send something that never arrives

How to prepare in 60 days

Two months of focused daily practice move a working B1 to a solid B2 for this specific job, because the vocabulary of an assistant is narrow and repetitive. The plan is about an hour a day.

  • Days 1 - 15, templates: collect 20 real assistant situations (reschedule, confirm, decline, chase, apologise, introduce, thank, ask for a document) and write your own template for each, under 60 words; have them corrected by a teacher or an AI tool and learn them by heart
  • Days 16 - 30, inbox practice: every day sort 15 sample emails and answer the three most important in writing within 20 minutes; time yourself and keep the replies
  • Days 31 - 45, speaking: record a 60-second structured update every day (what happened, what I did, what I need); make three real phone calls a week in English to hotels, clinics or airlines with an actual question; listen to one business podcast episode a day
  • Days 46 - 60, vocabulary of the principal's world: banking and payments, contracts and renewals, travel and visas for trips, property and tenancy, schools and medical appointments; 10 new terms a day used in a sentence the same day
  • Throughout: read one business article a day and summarise it in three lines; switch your phone and calendar to English; write your own to-do list in English
  • Use AI tools to correct and explain, not to write for you at the interview: the test is live, and a message you cannot reproduce by hand is a message you cannot send at 8 am on a Monday

Russian-speaking employers and the mixed-language reality

A large part of the assistant market in Dubai is Russian-speaking owners, executives and families. Their assistants live in two or three languages at once, and the role is realistic for a candidate whose English is still growing.

  • The principal speaks Russian with you; the outside world (clinics, schools, banks, landlords, government portals, most vendors) speaks English; your job is to be the bridge in both directions, including translating documents and calls in plain words
  • Write "Russian-speaking, fluent Russian" in the CV as a language skill with a level; the employer is looking for the language, and a line about nationality does not help you
  • Expect WhatsApp in Russian, email in English and a phone call that starts in one and ends in the other; practise switching mid-sentence without losing the thread
  • Many Russian-speaking principals are themselves improving their English and want an assistant who writes English better than they do; this is where your written English earns your salary
  • Arabic is a plus for every role and close to a must for some: government offices, Arabic-speaking partners, front-desk duties, a family with Arabic-speaking staff; even basic courtesy Arabic and reading a form opens doors, and a candidate with English plus Russian plus working Arabic is rare and in demand
  • A third or fourth language rarely raises the salary on its own, but it widens the set of employers who will consider you; our guide on the Russian-speaking assistant in Dubai covers the employer's side

Scripts for the most common situations

Most of an assistant's English is twenty situations repeated daily. Learn these shapes and adapt the names. Keep every message short, specific and with a next step.

  • Reschedule: "Good morning, Sarah. Mr Ivanov's 3 pm today needs to move because of a travel change. Would Thursday at 10 or 11 work for Mr Haddad? Thank you for your flexibility."
  • Decline politely with an alternative: "Thank you for the invitation. Mrs Petrova is not able to attend on the 14th. She asked me to say she would be glad to meet in the week of the 21st if that is convenient."
  • Chase a reply: "Dear Mr Khan, a quick follow-up on my email of Monday regarding the tenancy renewal. Could you confirm the date by Thursday so I can arrange the payment? Many thanks."
  • Confirm what you heard on a call: "So that I have it right: two rooms, 12 to 15 November, late check-out on the 15th, invoice to the company. Is that correct?"
  • Report a mistake with a solution: "I need to flag that the Geneva flight was booked for the 9th instead of the 10th. I have two options: a change for 450 AED on the same airline, or an evening flight at the original fare. Which do you prefer?"
  • Protect the principal's privacy: "I am not able to share Mr Ivanov's schedule, but I will be happy to pass your request on and come back to you today."
  • Ask for a document: "Could you please send the signed copy of the agreement as a PDF by end of day? We need it for the bank tomorrow morning."
  • A short daily update: "Today: Geneva trip confirmed, tenancy renewal in progress (reply expected Thursday), school meeting moved to Monday 9 am. One decision needed from you: the insurance renewal quote, details attached."

FAQ

What English level do I need to work as an assistant in Dubai?

As orientation on the CEFR scale: B1 strong to B2 for an administrative assistant or a personal assistant to a Russian-speaking principal, B2 for a personal or lifestyle assistant to an international principal, B2 strong for an executive assistant, C1 for an executive assistant to C-level or a family office and for chief of staff. Written English matters most, because you write on the principal's behalf. Employers test it on tasks in the interview, not with a certificate.

Can I work as an assistant in Dubai with B1 English?

Yes, in some roles: administrative assistant in a team, or personal assistant to a Russian-speaking owner or family, where the principal speaks Russian with you and English is used with clinics, schools, vendors and portals. Prepare templates for the twenty most common situations, practise for 60 days and be honest about the level in the interview. Executive and C-level roles need B2 strong to C1.
