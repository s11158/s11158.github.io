Specialist doctor salaries in Dubai by specialty and licence tier, 2026

What a private clinic in Dubai pays a specialist or consultant doctor depends first on the specialty and the DHA licence tier, then on the patient base the doctor brings. GP, specialist, consultant: why the licence tier sets the number

The DHA issues a doctor's licence in tiers, and the tier decides both what the doctor may do in your clinic and what the market pays. Two doctors in the same specialty can sit 20,000 AED apart for this reason alone.

  • General practitioner: a medical degree and a few years of experience; sees undifferentiated patients, refers complex cases on; the entry level of a clinic's medical team
  • Specialist: a recognised postgraduate qualification in the specialty plus the years of experience the DHA requires; works independently within the specialty and is the core of most private clinics
  • Consultant: a specialist with a longer track record and, usually, a senior qualification; leads a department, takes complex cases and second opinions, trains the team and often brings referrals and a patient base
  • The tier is confirmed during licensing: the DHA checks the qualification and experience against its current requirements, so ask for the candidate's expected tier before you agree the salary
  • A doctor already licensed in Dubai at the right tier starts faster through a licence transfer; a doctor from abroad goes through DataFlow verification and, if required, an assessment, and the start date moves by weeks

Fixed salary, revenue share or sessions: how clinics pay doctors

A specialist's package in Dubai is rarely a flat salary only. The model you choose changes your fixed cost and the kind of doctor you attract.

  • Fixed salary: predictable for the doctor, suits a new department that is still building its patient flow; the clinic carries the risk of an empty diary
  • Fixed base plus revenue share: the doctor receives a share of what the clinic collects for their consultations and procedures above an agreed threshold; the most common model for specialists with an own patient base
  • Fixed minimum or revenue share, whichever is higher: protects the doctor in slow months and rewards a full diary; define what counts as collections and how insurance rejections and refunds are treated
  • Sessions or per case: visiting specialists such as a neurologist, an allergist or a surgeon are often paid per clinic day or per procedure before a full-time role is justified
  • Write the rule in the contract: the threshold, the percentage, the payment date and how materials, implants and lab costs are treated; do not leave it to a verbal agreement

What moves the number within a specialty

Inside one specialty the range is wide. These factors usually decide where a particular doctor sits.

  • Procedures and volume: an interventional cardiologist, an endoscopy-heavy gastroenterologist or a surgeon with a full list earns more than a purely consulting colleague
  • Own patient base and referrals: a doctor who brings patients, referring GPs or a strong public profile is paid more, usually through the revenue share
  • Type of facility: a hospital or a large multi-speciality group pays differently from a boutique clinic; day-surgery and premium clinics often pay more for the same tier
  • Country of training and board certification: Western or recognised boards and fellowships sit at the upper end; the licensing route also depends on them
  • Languages: Arabic for local and GCC patients, Russian for the Russian-speaking community, Hindi or Urdu for other groups often matter as much as a year of extra experience
  • Insurance mix: a doctor who knows how to work with insurance approvals and documentation saves the clinic rejected claims

The cost on top of salary

Salary is not the full cost of a specialist. Add these items when you compare candidates and plan a new department.

  • Visa and Emirates ID: paid by the employer; the amount depends on the company type and the free zone
  • DHA licensing: DataFlow verification, the assessment if required and activation of the licence under your facility; amounts depend on the case and the tier
  • Malpractice insurance for every licensed doctor and health insurance for every employee; the premium depends on the specialty, the plan and the person's age
  • Gratuity provision: about 5.83% of basic salary per month in the first five years of service, see our gratuity guide
  • Annual leave of 30 calendar days after the first year, plus the time a diary takes to fill when a fixed minimum is paid without matching collections
  • Equipment and consumables for the specialty: an endoscopy suite, a cardiac ultrasound or an allergy testing panel is a separate budget line, not part of the salary

FAQ

Which doctors earn the most in Dubai?

Market estimate: surgical and procedure-heavy specialties sit at the top. A consultant plastic surgeon earns about 90,000 - 150,000+ AED per month, a consultant anesthesiologist 65,000 - 100,000+, consultant radiologists and orthopedic surgeons 60,000 - 95,000+, consultant cardiologists and ophthalmologists 55,000 - 85,000+. Consulting specialties such as allergy, endocrinology and neurology earn about 28,000 - 45,000 AED at specialist level and 42,000 - 65,000 AED at consultant level.

How much does a specialist doctor earn per month in Dubai?

Market estimate: a specialist licence in most medical specialties pays about 30,000 - 55,000 AED per month in a private clinic in Dubai, and a consultant licence about 45,000 - 85,000 AED. A general practitioner earns about 15,000 - 28,000 AED. The exact figure depends on the specialty, the procedures the doctor performs, the patient base they bring and the facility type; many packages combine a fixed base with a revenue share.

Does a small clinic need a consultant or is a specialist enough?

For most private clinics a specialist licence is enough: the doctor works independently within the specialty and costs 15,000 - 30,000 AED a month less than a consultant. A consultant pays off when you need complex cases, second opinions, a department lead or a name that brings referrals. Many clinics start with a specialist on a fixed base plus revenue share and add a visiting consultant for sessions before moving to a full-time consultant.
