Assistant for a family relocating to Dubai the first months, which type fits and what it costs

A family that moves to Dubai lands in a new city with a list of forty tasks and no routine: housing viewings, school places, Emirates ID and bank appointments, utilities, cars, a nanny and a housekeeper to find, a new doctor, a new everything. One parent usually starts a demanding job the same month. A relocation assistant does not do the paperwork for the family and does not replace the employer's visa process, but coordinates all of it so nothing is missed and the parents are not the project managers of their own move. This guide explains what such an assistant takes over, when the role turns into a house manager or a hybrid assistant, what it costs as a market estimate, how to check the person and where the boundaries are.

Talk to us

A typical week without an assistant

The first months of a move are a sequence of appointments that depend on each other. Without one person holding the plan, the week looks like this.

  • Monday: three housing viewings across the city, two of them for apartments already taken, because nobody called the agent in the morning to confirm
  • Tuesday: the school asks for a document that is still in a shipping box; the assessment slot is lost and the next one is in six weeks
  • Wednesday: an Emirates ID or bank appointment is missed because the time clashed with a work meeting and no one moved either of them
  • Thursday: the apartment is signed but utilities, internet and the chiller account are not connected, and the move-in day becomes a day of calls
  • Friday: the car purchase waits for an insurance quote, the insurance waits for a document, and the family keeps paying for taxis
  • Weekend: interviews with nannies and housekeepers happen without a plan or references, and the decision is made from tiredness, not from facts

What the assistant takes over

The role is coordination, not representation. The family and the employer sign, attend and decide; the assistant makes sure the right documents, times and people come together.

  • Business and paperwork coordination: a single relocation plan with dates; appointment booking and reminders for Emirates ID, medical, bank and school steps, the family attends in person and the employer or the family handles its own visa paperwork
  • Housing: a shortlist of areas and buildings against the family's criteria, viewings arranged in a sensible route, questions to the landlord and the agent prepared, the move-in checklist
  • Schools: a list of schools with curriculum, fees published by the school, waiting lists and assessment dates; documents collected; visits and assessments scheduled
  • Utilities and services: electricity and water, chiller, internet, television, building access, deliveries, a cleaning service until the household is set up
  • Cars: dealers and used car options, test drives, insurance quotes, registration appointment, a driver if the family needs one
  • Household: finding a nanny, a housekeeper, a private driver or a cook with the family, interview scheduling, reference calls, a first-month schedule for each
  • Personal: doctors and dentist, pharmacy, gym, clubs for the children, pets, a map of the neighbourhood, the family's calendar
  • What should NOT be delegated: signing the tenancy and school contracts, bank access and transfers, decisions on which school or home, the children's medical decisions, anything that requires the family member to appear in person

Which type fits and what it costs

In the first months a personal or relocation assistant is enough. Once the move is done, the role either ends, shrinks to a part-time personal assistant, or grows into a house manager or a hybrid assistant who also supports one parent's business. Salaries are a market estimate in AED per month, total package, basic salary plus allowances; the employer also pays visa, health insurance and gratuity provision.

How to find and check

Look for people who have already done a Dubai relocation for a family or an executive: personal assistants of expatriate families, relocation coordinators, hotel or residence concierges, house managers. Language matters: the person must write to schools and landlords in business English and, for a Russian-speaking family, speak Russian fluently with the parents and the children.

  • Email and WhatsApp triage: 15 messages from an agent, two schools, the bank, a nanny candidate and a relative; sort by urgency, draft three replies
  • Calendar conflict: a school assessment, a viewing in another part of the city and a bank appointment on the same morning; what moves and who is told
  • Profession-specific task: plan the first four weeks after arrival from a list of tasks (home, school, Emirates ID and medical steps, utilities, car), with dependencies and the order that avoids wasted trips
  • Local knowledge: ask the person to compare two areas for a family with children of a given age and explain the trade-offs honestly
  • Red flags: promises to "sort out the visa" or the bank for you, no questions about budgets and limits, vagueness about which agents or schools they worked with
  • Probation plan: weeks 1-2 the plan and the calendar with every outgoing message approved, weeks 3-4 viewings and school steps coordinated independently, weeks 5-8 household recruiting with the family and a weekly summary, then a decision on whether the role continues

Risks and boundaries

A relocation assistant sees passports, salaries, bank letters and the children's routines. The family controls what is shared and how.

  • Confidentiality: a written agreement before any document is shared; copies of documents only for a specific appointment, deleted after it
  • Access: a shared family calendar and a shared mailbox for the move, not personal passwords; no bank access, the family makes every payment
  • Money: a small float for small expenses with receipts, agreed in writing; deposits and rent are paid by the family
  • Children: the assistant coordinates schools and activities, but the nanny, the parents and the school are the ones with the children; background and reference checks for household staff are done with the family, see our reference checks guide
  • Dependence on one person: a shared relocation file with contacts, contracts, renewal dates and logins by role, so the family can continue without the assistant
  • End of the role: agree from the start whether the job ends after the move, continues part-time or becomes a house manager or a hybrid assistant role with a new scope and salary

FAQ

Does a family moving to Dubai need an assistant?

For the first three to six months, often yes, if both parents work or one starts a demanding job on arrival: housing, schools, Emirates ID and bank appointments, utilities, cars and household hiring all land at once and depend on each other. A single coordinator saves wasted trips and missed slots. After the move the role can end, shrink to part-time or become a house manager or hybrid assistant.

How much does an assistant for a relocating family cost in Dubai?

Market estimate: a personal or relocation assistant 5,000 - 10,000 AED per month and 12,000+ for a senior one; a house manager 10,000 - 20,000 AED as an orientation; a hybrid multi-assistant who also supports a parent's business 10,000 - 18,000 AED, senior 18,000 - 25,000 AED.
