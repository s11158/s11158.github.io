How to hire real estate brokers in Dubai: a guide for brokerages

RERA card, commission or salary, how many months to a first deal, and how to test a salesperson before the offer, not after three empty months.

Dubai real estate has set transaction records for the third year running, and with it the main problem of every brokerage has grown: people who can actually sell, not just post listings, are still scarce. In our experience a typical brokerage loses or drops up to half of its new brokers within a year. The cause is almost always the same - a wrong profile or a weak check at the entry point. Here is how to build hiring so that people reach deals, not a probation period with nothing to show.

Which roles a brokerage needs

"Broker" in Dubai covers several different jobs, and mixing them up is expensive.

  • Secondary market and leasing broker - works with listings, landlords and buyers of ready homes. Needs a patch, a landlord database, patience and the ability to carry a deal through to DLD transfer.
  • Off-plan sales consultant - sells developer projects. Speed, handling paid leads, knowing payment plans and selling at launches matter more here.
  • Property consultant - a generalist role in firms where brokers cover both secondary and off-plan. Often the entry point for strong salespeople from other industries.
  • Mortgage advisor - gets the buyer bank approval, compares offers and shortens the deal cycle. A good one lifts conversion for every broker in the firm.
  • Relationship manager and investment advisor - for firms serving high-net-worth investors and property portfolios. Background in private banking or wealth management and long client relationships count most.

Licences and regulators: what the employee needs

In Dubai, brokerage is regulated by RERA, part of the Dubai Land Department. A broker may show property, negotiate and earn commission only after being registered with DLD under a licensed brokerage. The candidate completes the course and exam at the Dubai Real Estate Institute, and the company then issues the broker card. Property ads require a Trakheesi permit, and ads without one are fined at the company level.

What this means for hiring:

  • The RERA card is tied to the company. When a broker moves it has to be transferred, so "I have a card" means they passed the exam before, not that they can start tomorrow.
  • A candidate without a card is not a lost cause, but plan 3-6 weeks for training, exam and registration. Who pays for the course and card is your call; usually it is a company cost.
  • Abu Dhabi and the other emirates have their own regulators and rules. An Abu Dhabi broker cannot start in Dubai straight away.
  • A mortgage advisor works for a company holding the right licence. Advice on securities and funds requires a separate financial regulator licence - check this before hiring for such a role.

The employer arranges the work visa, insurance and MOHRE contract registration; see our guide on the work visa and permit.

Pay: salary, commission and split

Almost all of a Dubai broker's income is commission. Market standard: 2% of the price on a secondary sale, about 5% of the annual rent on leases, and in off-plan the developer pays, usually 3-7% and more on promotional launches. The company then splits that commission with the broker.

  • Broker with no basic: a 50/50 or 60/40 split in the broker's favour; strong brokers with their own leads get 70/30 and above.
  • Broker with a basic: 3,000-8,000 AED a month plus a lower split, usually 20-40%. Typical for people new to the market and off-plan teams working company leads.
  • Off-plan consultant: often 4,000-10,000 AED basic for the first 3-6 months plus a bonus per sale. A strong consultant can clear 400,000 AED a year in 2026; a weak one does not cover the basic.
  • Mortgage advisor: 8,000-18,000 AED salary plus a share of the bank's fee.
  • Relationship manager and investment advisor: 18,000-40,000 AED fixed plus bonuses, depending on portfolio size.

Seasonality and ramp-up

High season in Dubai runs from October to April: investors return, Cityscape takes place, developers launch projects. Summer is slower, and that makes it the best time to hire and train: by September the broker has a card, knows the communities and meets the season ready. Hire in November and the first deals will land closer to spring.

Realistic ramp-up: on leasing a first deal in 1-2 months, on secondary sales in 2-4, in off-plan an experienced consultant can close in month one with a good lead flow. No deals after 4-5 months despite leads means the issue is the person or their setup.

How to test a broker before the offer

A "tell me about yourself" interview does not test a broker: almost everyone can sell themselves. Practical checks work.

  • Deal review: ask them to walk through their last three deals - property, price, lead source, timeline, what went wrong. Strong brokers remember the numbers; weak ones stay vague.
  • Call role-play: a call on an inbound off-plan lead from an ad. Watch for questions on budget, timing and payment method, not eloquence.
  • Market knowledge: price per square foot and rental yields in two or three communities they claim to know.
  • CRM and discipline: how they keep their pipeline, calls and viewings per week, experience with Property Finder, Bayut and a company CRM.
  • Languages: English is a must; Russian, Arabic, Hindi, Mandarin or other languages open access to specific buyer flows. The criterion is the language, not origin.
Red flag: a candidate promises to "bring their database" but cannot name a single client who bought from them twice. A real client base shows in repeat deals and referrals.

Common mistakes when hiring brokers

  • Hiring in bulk on commission only and waiting for the strong ones to survive. The strong ones leave first because they need lead and marketing support.
  • Not writing down how commission is earned, what happens to deals in progress and payment timelines. Most disputes on exit come from here.
  • Putting an off-plan consultant on secondary or the reverse without retraining.
  • Not counting the entry cost: visa, RERA card, training, desk, portal subscriptions. A broker who does not close costs the firm tens of thousands of dirhams a year.
  • Judging by confidence in the interview rather than by past deal numbers.

Step-by-step hiring plan

  • 1. Profile. Market (secondary, leasing, off-plan), communities, price segment, languages, whether a RERA card is required now.
  • 2. Pay structure. Basic or not, split, who pays for leads, when commission is earned.
  • 3. Search. Portals and LinkedIn bring volume, but strong brokers are rarely actively looking - they have to be approached directly.
  • 4. Practical checks. Deal review, call, market knowledge, as above.
  • 5. Offer and documents. Contract, visa, DLD registration, DREI training if needed.
  • 6. First 90 days. A monthly plan for calls, viewings and deals and a mentor from the team.

Checklist before the offer

  • Numbers on the last three deals and references from past managers checked.
  • Clear whether there is a RERA card and how long a transfer will take.
  • Basic, split and rules on deals in progress agreed.
  • Lead source and 90-day plan understood.
  • Full entry cost of the employee calculated.

Frequently asked questions

Can a broker work in Dubai without a RERA card?

No. Only a broker registered with RERA (Dubai Land Department) under a licensed brokerage can show property, negotiate and earn commission in Dubai. The broker completes the Dubai Real Estate Institute course and exam first. Until the card is issued, the person can only do support tasks.

Salary or commission only: what works better for brokers?

For people new to the market and for off-plan teams, a small basic salary or stipend for 3-6 months plus a commission split usually works better. Experienced brokers with their own client base often prefer a high split with no basic. Either way, write down in the contract when commission is earned and what happens to deals in progress if the broker leaves.

How long does a new broker take to close a first deal?

On the secondary market a first deal usually comes in 2-4 months, faster on rentals. In off-plan, during active developer launches, an experienced consultant can close a first sale in month one. If there is no deal after 4-5 months with a normal lead flow, the issue is no longer the market.
