Assistant without experience: how to start transferable experience, a 30-day plan, a first CV and the roles to apply for first

"No experience" is almost never true for someone who considers becoming an assistant. A hotel receptionist has handled demanding guests at 2 am, a teacher has run a calendar for thirty families, a sales coordinator has chased payments and kept a CRM clean, a parent who runs a household has managed budgets, schedules, suppliers and crises for years. What you lack is the assistant vocabulary, a few tools and proof that an employer can check. All three can be built in a month. This article from our School of assistants series shows which experience transfers, a 30-day self-training plan, a first CV without assistant experience, the roles to apply for first and how to show reliability in an interview.

The experience you already have

Employers do not buy a title; they buy the ability to run someone's week. Translate your past into that language. Below are the most common backgrounds and what each one has already taught you.

  • Admin, reception, secretarial work: documents, filing, phone and email in two languages, visitors, suppliers, a boss's calendar. You already do half of an administrative assistant's job; what to add is travel planning and a principal's inbox
  • Hospitality (hotels, airlines, restaurants): demanding people, service under pressure, complaints, VIP logistics, discretion about guests. This is the classic route into personal and lifestyle assistant roles; what to add is office tools and written business English
  • Teaching and education: a calendar of lessons and events, communication with dozens of parents, reports, patience, structure. What to add is business vocabulary and tools; teachers often become excellent family office and personal assistants
  • Sales and customer support: CRM, follow-ups, writing to clients, handling refusals, targets. What to add is confidentiality habits and calendar management; sales people make strong executive assistants because they close loops
  • Motherhood and household management: budgets, school and medical schedules, staff or helpers, suppliers, travel with children, emergencies at any hour. This is operations experience, and employers who run a family office or a household know it; what to add is the professional vocabulary for it and references from outside the family (a school, a doctor, a community role)
  • Own small business or freelancing: invoices, clients, taxes, marketing, everything at once. What to add is the habit of working inside someone else's priorities rather than your own
  • Write each of these as results, not duties: "ran a schedule of 30 families with zero missed events in two years" says more than "was a teacher"

A 30-day self-training plan

One month of daily practice, about an hour a day, turns adjacent experience into assistant skills you can demonstrate. Keep everything you produce: it becomes your portfolio.

  • Week 1, tools: set up a Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 account; learn shared calendars, recurring events, time zones and invitations; build a spreadsheet with basic formulas and a filter; make one travel booking end to end on a real platform without paying (stop at the payment screen)
  • Week 2, inbox and writing: every morning sort your own inbox into three groups (needs a decision today, I can handle, can wait); write three short business emails a day in English on someone else's behalf: a rescheduling, a polite refusal, a thank-you; keep them under 60 words
  • Week 3, planning: build an itinerary template and fill it for a two-city trip with a connection under two hours; make a renewals list (passport, ID, insurance, licences, memberships) with reminders 60 days ahead; plan a dinner for eight with three venue options and a budget
  • Week 4, judgement and proof: write your one-page confidentiality rules as if for a successor; practise a 60-second structured update (what happened, what I did, what I need from you); ask two people who have seen you reliable to be referees and tell them what the role is
  • Every day: read one business article in English and write a three-line summary; this builds the vocabulary employers listen for
  • Use an AI assistant for drafts and corrections, and check every fact and name yourself; employers now ask about this habit

A first CV without assistant experience

A junior assistant CV is read in a minute. Make the first screen answer one question: can this person run my week. Our assistant CV guide covers the format in detail; these points are for the person with no assistant title yet.

  • Headline under your name: the role you want and your languages, for example "Administrative assistant (entry level), English B2, Russian native, Arabic basic"
  • A three-line summary: your background, what it taught you in assistant terms, what you offer now; no adjectives, only facts
  • Experience rewritten as ownership and results: whom you served, how many, what you kept running, what never went wrong
  • A skills line with levels: tools, languages, typing speed if strong, driving licence if relevant for personal roles
  • A portfolio line: "templates available on request: itinerary, weekly summary, renewals tracker"; attach them when asked, anonymised
  • References: two names ready, told in advance; write "references available on request", never list private phone numbers in a public CV
  • No age, photo or marital status requirements to meet; describe skills. The free CV builder on our site gives a clean structure in minutes

Which roles to apply for first

Aim one step below your ceiling and win it fast. Figures are market estimates in AED per month, total package, basic salary plus allowances; the employer also pays the visa, health insurance and gratuity provision.

  • Administrative assistant in a company or a clinic, 5,000 - 8,000: the broadest entry door; you learn documents, suppliers and a team's calendar with less exposure to a single principal
  • Receptionist or coordinator in a clinic, salon or office: often the path to the owner's assistant inside the same company within a year
  • Junior personal assistant to a Russian-speaking family or owner, 5,000 - 10,000: your language is the advantage; the role teaches travel, renewals and household coordination
  • Virtual assistant, 3,500 - 8,000 part-time or remote (orientation): a way to collect experience and references while you are abroad or alongside another job
  • Avoid as a first job: executive assistant to a C-level executive, family office roles, hybrid multi-assistant to a founder; these need trust that only comes with a track record, and a failure there costs more than a year at a lower step
  • Check the vacancy for red flags: a payment or deposit requested from the candidate, no company name, a salary far above the market for a junior role, a role that filters by age or gender; a legitimate employer or consultancy does not charge you and hires for skills

How to show reliability in an interview

Without a track record, the interview is where you win or lose. Employers look for evidence, not promises. Bring it.

  • Arrive ten minutes early, with the itinerary of your own trip to the interview written down; it sounds small, and it is exactly the skill
  • Bring your portfolio: the itinerary template, the renewals tracker, the weekly summary format; offer to walk through one in two minutes
  • Have a real story of a mistake: what happened, how you caught it, what you changed; people without this story look either inexperienced or dishonest
  • Have a real story of a difficult person you served well, with what you did, not what you felt
  • Expect a practical task: 12 to 15 emails to rank, a calendar conflict, a short message to write on the principal's behalf; do it calmly and say your reasoning aloud
  • Ask three questions about the role: what good looks like in 90 days, how the principal prefers updates, what the rules for reachability and money are; this shows you already think like an assistant
  • After the interview, send a short thank-you the same day with one line that confirms what was agreed; it is the first follow-up you do for this employer

FAQ

Can I become an assistant in Dubai with no experience?

Yes, at the entry level: administrative assistant, receptionist or coordinator, junior personal assistant, virtual assistant. Employers accept people from admin, hospitality, teaching, sales and household management when they show written English, tools, reliability and references. Rewrite your past as results, practise for a month on real tasks and bring a portfolio of templates to the interview.

How much does a junior assistant earn in Dubai?

As a market estimate, an administrative or junior personal assistant earns 5,000 - 8,000 AED per month total package, basic salary plus allowances; a junior personal assistant to a family or owner 5,000 - 10,000; a virtual assistant 3,500 - 8,000 part-time or remote (orientation). The employer also pays the visa, health insurance and gratuity provision. Senior administrative assistants reach 8,000 - 11,000 and executive assistants 12,000 - 20,000.
