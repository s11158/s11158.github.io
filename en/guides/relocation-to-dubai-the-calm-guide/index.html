Relocation to Dubai: the calm guide the honest picture, the money, the decision, life and career, and a roadmap from the offer to day 90

You have an offer, or a strong feeling that Dubai could be your next city, and a long list of questions that nobody answers in one place: what it really costs to settle, whether your salary will be enough, what happens to your family, what you are signing up for and what you get in return. This is the entry page of our relocation series for Russian-speaking specialists: beauty masters, nurses and doctors, assistants and office staff, sales people. It gives the honest picture in one paragraph, then points you to the detailed guides on money, on the decision and on everyday life, and ends with a 12-step roadmap from the offer to day 90. No hype and no scare stories: moving to Dubai is a serious decision which, prepared well, becomes a good adventure.

Talk to us

The honest picture in one paragraph

Dubai is a working city where people from everywhere come to earn, grow and live well, and where almost everything is available if you know the places. Salaries are paid without personal income tax, employers normally provide health insurance and sponsor the work visa for the person they hire, public transport is affordable and groceries vary a lot by store. The other side is also true: housing is the biggest cost and the first months need a reserve, the climate is hot for half the year, the pace is fast, contracts matter more than promises, and everything you build here you build from scratch, including friends. People who prepare the money, the documents and the family side before saying yes tend to describe the move as the best decision they made. People who jump without a plan describe the first three months as the hardest. The series below exists to put you in the first group.

  • Five things that are true at once: good money is possible, the first months cost more than you expect, your contract and your licence are your protection, the city is safe and well organised, and the decision is yours and nobody else's

Relocation roadmap: 12 steps from the offer to day 90

One possible order, from the moment an employer wants you to the end of your first quarter. Dates and procedures change: check the current rules on official portals (ICP, GDRFA, MOHRE) and follow your employer's instructions, since the employer sponsors the visa.

  • Step 1, the offer: read the offer letter as a whole (basic salary, allowances, housing, insurance, flights, probation, notice, start date); ask every question now, in writing, not after you have resigned
  • Step 2, the numbers: run the salary vs cost of living worksheet with your real offer and your eight line items; decide whether the reserve you have covers the first three months
  • Step 3, the family talk: decide who moves and when, what happens to the spouse's work, schools, parents, pets and the home you leave; write the decision down
  • Step 4, the documents: passport validity, attested diploma and certificates, references, police clearance where required, medical records, photos; start attestation early because it takes time
  • Step 5, the contract: receive and read the employment contract before you give notice at home; match it with the offer; see our live guide on the employment contract in Dubai
  • Step 6, the visa process with the employer: the employer sponsors the work visa and tells you which steps are yours (entry, medical, Emirates ID, biometrics); you follow their checklist and keep copies of everything
  • Step 7, notice at home: resign only when the contract is signed and the visa process has started; finish well, take references, close accounts and subscriptions you will not need
  • Step 8, the first housing: book a short-term place for the first two to four weeks instead of signing a year lease from abroad; choose the long-term area after you have seen your commute
  • Step 9, arrival week: SIM card, bank account when your documents allow it, Emirates ID steps, RTA card, a walk around the area you consider living in; sleep and water, the climate is real
  • Step 10, the lease: once the salary is confirmed and the area is chosen, sign the lease, register Ejari, connect utilities and internet; keep the deposit receipt and the agent's licence details
  • Step 11, days 30 to 60: build the routine (clinic in your insurance network, groceries by store, transport pattern, a community), review the budget against reality and adjust the reserve
  • Step 12, day 90: review the probation period honestly with your manager, write down what you learned about the city and the job, and decide what year one in Dubai should look like

FAQ

Is moving to Dubai for work a good idea?

For many specialists it is, when it is prepared: an offer read as a whole, the numbers run against real prices, documents attested early, the family side decided and a reserve for the first months. Dubai gives a salary without personal income tax, employer-paid health insurance, a safe and well-organised city and a career market where good work becomes visible quickly. It is a serious decision rather than a reckless adventure, and the series on this page helps you decide with open eyes.

How much money do I need to move to Dubai?

Enough to cover the one-off start (deposit, agent fee, Ejari, first furniture, first groceries) plus two to three months of your expected costs while the first salary and any housing allowance arrive. There is no single number: it depends on whether you rent a room, a studio or a one-bedroom and whether the employer provides housing or a hotel for the first weeks.
