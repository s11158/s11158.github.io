Moving to Dubai with a family sponsorship, schools, insurance, housing and the order in which to move

A job offer in Dubai becomes a family decision the moment you have a partner or children. The questions are practical: can I bring them, where will the children study and what does it cost, who pays for their health insurance, what kind of home can we afford, will my partner find work, and who should fly first. This guide answers them calmly and honestly: what is known for sure, what you must check on official portals yourself, what it costs as an orientation range, and why thousands of Russian-speaking families who went through it would do it again. The move is a serious decision. Prepared well, it becomes a good adventure rather than a reckless one.

Can I bring my family: what to check and where

In the UAE an employee holding a residence visa can usually sponsor a spouse and children once their employment and salary meet the conditions set by the immigration authorities. The exact salary threshold, the documents (attested marriage and birth certificates, a tenancy contract, medical tests for adults) and the processing steps change from time to time, so treat every number you see in a chat or a forum as outdated until you check it.

  • Check the current family sponsorship conditions on the official portals: ICP (icp.gov.ae) for the UAE and GDRFA Dubai (gdrfad.gov.ae) for Dubai residence matters; your employer's PRO or HR usually knows the current practice too
  • Ask the employer before you sign: will the company handle the family's visas, or only yours; does the offer include any family allowance; is family health insurance included or at your cost
  • Attest the documents at home before you fly: marriage certificate, children's birth certificates, diplomas; attestation takes weeks and is far easier to do from your home country
  • Plan the order: in practice the employee's own residence visa and Emirates ID come first, then a tenancy contract in the employee's name, then the family applications; budget six to twelve weeks from your first day to the family's residence visas as a realistic orientation, longer if documents are missing
  • TLNT does not give immigration advice; we help you meet an employer who sponsors your work visa and we give you a step-by-step checklist. Rules are the authorities' domain, and they are the only reliable source

Nurseries and schools: tiers and what they cost

Dubai has hundreds of private schools with British, American, IB, Indian, French, German and Russian-language curricula, and every one of them is private and paid. Fees are regulated and published: the KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority) directory lists each school's fees and inspection rating, which makes comparison easy and honest. The ranges below are an orientation, autumn 2026, check current fees in the KHDA directory and on the school's website.

Admission is usually by assessment and documents, and good schools have waiting lists for some year groups; apply as soon as the offer is firm, even before you fly. The school year runs from late August or September to June with a short winter and spring break; children can join mid-year if there is a place, but the September start is calmest. Fees are usually paid per term, which spreads the cost; ask about sibling discounts and whether the employer offers an education allowance (common for senior medical and management roles, rare for junior ones). Russian-language and bilingual schools and nursery groups exist; most Russian-speaking families still choose a British or IB school and keep Russian at home and in weekend classes. Visit two or three schools on the first trip, with the child if possible; the atmosphere decides more than the rating.

Health insurance for dependants

Employers in Dubai are required to provide health insurance for their employees, and the employee's own cover is normally part of every offer. Dependants are a separate question: by Dubai rules the sponsor must insure the people they sponsor, and whether the employer pays for them is a matter of the offer, not of the law.

  • Ask directly: does the package include family cover, for how many dependants and at which plan level; for senior roles it often does, for junior roles it usually does not
  • If you pay yourself, there are basic plans designed for dependants that cover the required minimum, and comprehensive plans with wider networks and maternity; the price difference is several times, so compare three quotes from licensed insurers or a broker before you buy
  • Maternity cover usually has a waiting period; if a pregnancy is planned, read this part of the policy first
  • Keep insurance cards for every family member on your phone; a clinic visit without the card means paying and claiming back
  • Our guide on healthcare, transport and daily life explains how the employee's own insurance works day to day

Housing for a family: areas, sizes, how rent works

Rent is the largest line of a family budget in Dubai and the one where knowing the places matters most. The same two-bedroom apartment costs twice as much in a marina tower as in a quiet family community twenty minutes away. Annual rent is the norm, usually paid in one to four cheques, with a refundable deposit and an agent's commission at signing. Check current prices on Bayut, Property Finder or Dubizzle, and take the ranges below as an orientation, autumn 2026.

A spouse's job search

A partner who moves on a family visa is not locked out of work. In practice the spouse can take up employment when an employer arranges the work permit under the current MOHRE rules; check the procedure for your case, it is simpler than many people fear. The real question is the job itself.

  • In TLNT's fields Dubai employers regularly look for Russian-speaking specialists: beauty masters, nurses and doctors, assistants and office staff, sales and real estate, household roles; our career guide for Russian-speaking specialists lists what each field asks for
  • Start before the move: a CV in the Dubai format, a portfolio, references, and for medical roles the licensing steps (DataFlow verification and the DHA process take months and can start from abroad)
  • Be realistic about the first job: it may be a step below the role at home while the local track record is built; the second job in Dubai usually corrects that
  • Remote work for a company abroad from Dubai is common among spouses; check the tax and visa implications for your own situation, we do not advise on them

Timeline: who moves first, and the honest cost picture

Most families that settle smoothly follow the same pattern: the person with the job offer flies first, the family follows once the home and the school are ready. Rushing everyone onto one flight to a hotel with no school place and no tenancy is the most common source of stress.

  • Month 0: offer signed, documents attested at home, school applications sent, a temporary apartment or hotel booked for the first weeks
  • Month 1: the employee arrives, residence visa and Emirates ID in progress, bank account opened, SIM card, a first salary date confirmed; evenings spent viewing homes in the chosen area
  • Months 1 to 2: tenancy signed, DEWA connected, school place confirmed, family applications filed; the family flies in, ideally two to four weeks before the school term
  • Months 2 to 3: family residence visas and Emirates IDs, insurance cards, a second car or a transport plan, routine
  • Cash to have before you fly, as an orientation: the first rent cheque or the deposit plus the first month of a short let, the agent's commission, school registration, flights, and two to three months of living costs; for a family of three or four this is a serious sum, which is why the decision deserves a spreadsheet, not a mood
  • The upside is real: safety that lets children move around on their own, eight pleasant months of outdoor life a year, international schools in every curriculum, no personal income tax on salaries, a Russian-speaking community with its own schools, doctors and shops, and a travel hub three to six hours from most of Europe and Asia

FAQ

Can I bring my family to Dubai if I get a job there?

Usually yes: an employee with a residence visa can sponsor a spouse and children once their employment and salary meet the conditions set by the immigration authorities, and the documents (attested marriage and birth certificates, a tenancy contract) are in place. The conditions change, so check the current rules on ICP or GDRFA Dubai and ask the employer whether they handle the family's visas. Realistic orientation: six to twelve weeks from your first day to the family's residence visas.

How much does it cost to move to Dubai with a family?

Plan the move as a spreadsheet, not a guess: the first rent payment or deposit, the agent's commission, school registration and the first term, flights, furnishing and two to three months of living costs. As an orientation, autumn 2026, a two-bedroom apartment in a family community rents at roughly 75,000 - 120,000 AED per year, a middle-tier school costs roughly 35,000 - 70,000 AED per child per year, a full-day nursery roughly 2,500 - 6,000 AED per month. Check current prices on Bayut, Property Finder and the KHDA directory; the figures depend heavily on the area and the school.
