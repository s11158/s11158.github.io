Assistant boundaries and burnout how to agree working hours and emergency rules, say no professionally, notice burnout early and raise it with the boss

The most common fear of people who could become assistants is not the workload, it is the loss of themselves: "the boss will call at night", "I will be available forever", "I will become a servant". The fear is reasonable because it happens, and it happens for one reason: boundaries were never agreed, so they were set by whoever pushed harder. This article from our School of assistants series is about setting them like a professional: how to agree working hours and emergency rules at the offer stage, how to say no on your own behalf and on your principal's, which signs of burnout to take seriously, how to raise it with the boss in a way that gets a result, and when leaving is the right decision. Boundaries are not the opposite of service; they are what makes service last for years.

Talk to us

Boundaries are part of the job, not a threat to it

An assistant with no boundaries is not a better assistant; they are a tired one who will leave in a year, and the principal will start again. Good employers know this. The ones who do not can usually be shown it, because the argument is in their interest.

  • Your attention is the product: a rested assistant catches the wrong date in a contract, an exhausted one does not
  • Availability without rules is unpredictable for the principal too: they do not know when you will answer, so they message more
  • Written rules protect both sides: what counts as an emergency, which channel is for what, what happens on days off
  • Working hours, overtime, rest days and leave are governed by your written contract and current UAE labour rules; read the contract before signing and check the current rules on the MOHRE portal; nothing on this page replaces them
  • Self-respect is visible in interviews: candidates who ask calm questions about hours and emergencies are usually read as experienced, not difficult

How to agree working hours and emergency rules

The best moment is before you accept the offer; the second best is the first week; the third is now. Ask as a professional who wants to serve well, not as someone negotiating a favour.

  • Ask about the real rhythm: "What does a normal week look like for you, and how often does something come up in the evening or at the weekend?" The answer tells you more than the job description
  • Define an emergency together and write it down: a flight or visa problem today, a medical situation, a payment that fails before a deadline, a security matter; everything else is tomorrow
  • Agree channels: one channel for urgent (a call), one for next-day items (a message you answer in the morning), email for everything that can wait
  • Agree the late-message rule: "If it is for tomorrow, I will confirm with one line and act in the morning; if it is urgent by our list, call me"
  • Agree days off and how they are covered: who the principal calls when you are off, and what you leave them (a one-page handover, access to the calendar, the vendors' numbers)
  • Agree travel: when you travel with the principal, which hours are working hours and how the extra time is compensated or rested; put the answer in writing, in the contract or an email
  • For hybrid roles agree the split between business, personal and household tasks and the rule for new personal requests: "ask, and I will say whether it fits"
  • Write the summary of what was agreed in an email after the conversation; a written agreement survives a stressful month, a verbal one does not

How to say no professionally: scripts

An assistant says no many times a day on the principal's behalf and sometimes on their own. The form is the same: acknowledge, decline, offer an alternative, keep the relationship.

  • To a late request that is not urgent: "Got it, thank you. I will take care of it first thing tomorrow and confirm by 09:30. If it cannot wait, call me and I will do it now."
  • To a task outside the agreed scope: "I can help with this today as a one-off. If it is going to be regular, can we talk about how it fits with the rest of my week so nothing else slips?"
  • To a request for the principal's time, on their behalf: "The director's week is full. I can offer 15 minutes on Thursday or pass him a written summary today, whichever works better for you."
  • To a request for confidential information: "I am not able to share that. If you tell me what you need, I will pass it on and come back to you."
  • To a weekend message that arrives at the wrong time: a short acknowledgement and a plan, not an apology: "Seen, thank you. Monday 09:00 I will have it done."
  • To a stressed principal who is short with you: say nothing in the moment; later, calmly: "This morning was tense. I would like to go through the open items for ten minutes so we are aligned."
  • To yourself, when you want to say yes to everything: a request accepted that you cannot deliver is a broken promise, which is worse for the principal than a clear no with an alternative

Warning signs of burnout to take seriously

Burnout in this job rarely arrives as a collapse. It arrives as a slow change in how you work and feel. Catch it early, when it is still a conversation, not a resignation.

  • You check the phone every few minutes on days off and feel anxious when it is quiet
  • Small mistakes multiply: a wrong time zone, a forgotten reminder, a message answered too sharply
  • You stop proposing improvements and only react; the job feels like a queue that never ends
  • You feel irritation towards the principal's ordinary requests, not only unreasonable ones
  • Sleep, meals and exercise have given way to availability, and you have not taken real leave in a long time
  • You cannot remember the last time you felt satisfied by something you finished
  • Two or three of these for several weeks are a signal to act, not a character flaw; the earlier you raise it, the more options you have

How to raise it with the boss

Principals are usually not aware of the load because the assistant's job is to make the load invisible. Raise it as an operational problem with a proposed solution, not as a complaint.

  • Prepare facts, not feelings: for two weeks note the hours worked, the evening and weekend messages, the tasks added beyond the agreed scope
  • Ask for a short meeting with a clear subject: "I want to review how my week is working so I keep the quality where you expect it"
  • Open with what is working and your commitment to the role; then show the facts in one page; then propose two or three concrete changes
  • Concrete changes that principals accept: an emergency list, a late-message rule, moving routine tasks to a tool or another team member, a fixed weekly review, a real day off covered by a handover
  • If the scope has grown permanently, link it to the role: a new title, a review of the salary against the market frame, or a second person; see our article on negotiating an assistant salary
  • Agree a date to check whether the changes happened; send a two-line summary of the meeting afterwards
  • If the principal listens and nothing changes twice, that is information about the role, not about your effort

When to leave, and how to leave well

Most boundary problems are solved by a conversation. Some are not, and staying in a role that is damaging you is not loyalty. Decide calmly and leave professionally; your references are your career.

  • Reasons to go: agreed rules broken repeatedly after two honest conversations; disrespect that does not stop; pressure to do something unethical with money, documents or people; health that is clearly suffering
  • Reasons to stay and fix: a hard season with a visible end, a principal who did not know and now acts, a scope problem that a tool or a second person solves
  • Before you decide, update your CV and your record of achievements while you are calm; our Dubai CV format guide and the free CV builder help
  • Notice and probation follow your written contract and current UAE labour rules; read the contract and check the current rules on the MOHRE portal rather than relying on what colleagues say; our guide on probation and notice in the UAE explains how to read those clauses
  • Leave the way you worked: a complete handover file, vendors introduced to your successor, passwords returned through the employer's system, no copies of anything taken
  • Resign in person or by call first, then in writing, in neutral and warm words; a principal you leave well often becomes your best reference and sometimes a future employer

FAQ

How do I set boundaries as a personal or executive assistant without looking difficult?

Agree them as a professional who wants to serve well: ask about the real weekly rhythm at the offer stage, define an emergency list together, agree one channel for urgent calls and one for next-day messages, and write the summary in an email. Say no with an alternative and a time, never with an excuse. Candidates who ask calm questions about hours are usually read as experienced, not difficult.

What are the signs of burnout in an assistant and what should I do?

Checking the phone compulsively on days off, multiplying small mistakes, only reacting instead of improving, irritation at ordinary requests, lost sleep and no real leave for a long time. Two or three of these for several weeks mean it is time to act: collect two weeks of facts about hours and extra tasks, ask the principal for a short review, propose concrete changes and agree a date to check them. If nothing changes after two honest conversations, start planning a professional exit.
