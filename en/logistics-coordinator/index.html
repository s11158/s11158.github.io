Who we help you hire

A logistics coordinator who knows where every shipment is, catches document errors before the goods reach the port and solves delays with options, not excuses.

  • Shipments: bookings with forwarders and transporters, tracking from supplier to warehouse to customer, updates in the system or tracker every day
  • Documents: commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or airway bills and certificates checked against the order before shipping
  • Forwarders and clearing agents: quotes compared, instructions sent clearly, charges checked against agreed rates
  • Warehouse link: inbound and outbound plans with the warehouse team, stock movements and damages recorded
  • Customers and sales: realistic delivery dates, early warning on delays, answers to where is my order without a chain of emails
  • Costs and reporting: freight and delivery costs per shipment tracked, a weekly report on open shipments, delays and claims

What to check before hiring

Logistics coordinators are tested by exceptions, not by routine. The checks below use situations from your supply chain.

1
Shipment exception case: a container is held because the invoice and packing list do not match, a customer delivery is due in three days and the forwarder is not answering. The candidate writes what they do in the first two hours, who they contact and what they tell sales and the customer.
2
Document check: a set of shipping documents with three planted errors. The candidate finds them and explains what each error would cause.
3
Spreadsheet task: a list of open shipments with dates and costs. The candidate builds a simple tracker, marks the late ones and calculates cost per shipment.
4
References from logistics or supply chain managers: calls to the person's last manager: accuracy, how they handled forwarders and delays, how they worked with sales and the warehouse; the shipments, routes and systems they will own agreed before the start.

Typical hiring mistake: one person is asked to do both procurement and logistics. A procurement officer chooses suppliers and negotiates prices; a logistics coordinator moves the goods once they are bought. Combined in a small company, the role needs clear priorities, or logistics loses to price negotiations. Another mistake: hiring for general office skills when the person needs to read shipping documents and talk to forwarders from day one. A logistics coordinator is also not an operations manager: they own shipments, not the whole daily business.

FAQ

How much does a logistics coordinator earn in Dubai?

Market estimate for 2026: about 4,500 - 6,500 AED per month for a junior logistics coordinator, 6,500 - 9,000 AED for a logistics coordinator and 9,000 - 13,000 AED for a senior coordinator or team lead. Allowances and a bonus change the full package.

Logistics coordinator or procurement officer: who does a trading company need first?

If your problem is buying at the right price and from reliable suppliers, start with a procurement officer. If goods are bought but arrive late, with document problems or unclear costs, start with a logistics coordinator. Many trading companies have both: procurement decides what to buy and from whom, logistics brings it in and delivers it.
