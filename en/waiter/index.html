Who we help you hire

A waiter who takes the order right the first time, times the courses with the kitchen and leaves guests wanting to come back.

  • Service sequence: greeting, water and drinks, order taking, course timing, clearing, bill and farewell, in the venue's own standard
  • Menu knowledge: every dish, its ingredients and allergens, how it is cooked; drinks, wine or mocktail pairing according to the concept
  • Selling: recommending signature dishes, offering desserts and drinks naturally, reading when a table wants to be left alone
  • Section management: several tables at once, coordination with runners, bar and kitchen, orders entered in the POS without mistakes
  • Guest care: special requests, children and large groups, complaints handled politely and passed to the manager in time
  • Languages: English for all guests; Russian, Arabic or another language is a plus where the venue's guests speak it

What to check before hiring

A CV shows where a waiter worked, not how they serve. The assessment happens at a test table with your team playing guests, not as a shift with real guests.

1
Test table service: the venue's team plays a table of four: the candidate greets, presents the menu, takes the order without notes, serves and clears a two-course set and presents the bill. We look at the sequence, carrying technique, posture and eye contact.
2
Menu and allergens questions: after fifteen minutes with the menu, the candidate answers questions: which dishes contain nuts or gluten, what they would recommend to a vegetarian, which drink goes with the signature dish.
3
Difficult guest moment: during the test one guest says the dish is cold and the bill is wrong. We listen to the words and the tone, and check whether the candidate calls the manager at the right moment.
4
References: a call to the previous restaurant manager or head waiter about punctuality, teamwork during peaks, guest feedback and how the candidate followed the cash and POS rules.

How it differs from a host or hostess: the host runs the door, the reservations, the seating and the flow of the whole room; the waiter serves the tables of one section from order to bill. In a small cafe one person may do both. Typical hiring mistake: hiring for appearance and languages without a test table, then finding out after a week that the person cannot carry three plates or still does not know the menu. For fine dining, test wine service and plate presentation separately.

FAQ

How much does a waiter earn in Dubai?

Market estimate for 2026: about 2,500 - 4,000 AED per month for a waiter in a cafe or casual dining, 4,000 - 6,000 AED for an experienced waiter in fine dining, a hotel or a beach club, and 5,500 - 8,000 AED for a head waiter or captain. Housing, transport, service charge or tips can change the full package.

How do I test a waiter before hiring?

Set up a test table with your own team playing guests: a short set menu, one complaint and the bill. Then ask allergen and pairing questions from your menu. This shows service technique and attitude within an hour and does not involve real guests. Finish with a reference call to the previous manager.
