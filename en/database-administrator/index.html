Who we help you find

A specialist whose backups are proven by restores, who finds slow queries before users complain and grants access on least privilege.

  • Databases: Oracle, Microsoft SQL Server, PostgreSQL or MySQL to match your landscape, plus MongoDB or Redis where needed
  • Backup and recovery: an RPO and RTO strategy, regular restore tests, not just backups that exist
  • High availability: Always On, Data Guard, PostgreSQL replication, a rehearsed failover to the standby site
  • Performance: execution plans, indexes, locking, monitoring and advice to developers
  • Security: roles and permissions, encryption, access audit, data masking for test environments
  • Cloud and migrations: AWS RDS, Azure SQL, moves with minimal downtime, scripted automation

What to check before you hire

A DBA mistake is expensive: lost data or hours of downtime. So the check runs on scenarios rather than documentation questions, and your technical specialist scores it.

1
Slow query review: the candidate gets a query and an execution plan with a problem: they explain the bottleneck, which index or rewrite helps and what risks the change carries.
2
Recovery scenario: at 2 pm someone dropped the orders table. The candidate describes how they restore data to the right point in time, how long it takes and what to change so it does not happen again.
3
Access and audit conversation: how to separate permissions for developers, analysts and applications, how to audit access and what to do with personal data in test copies.
4
References: a call with the IT manager: size and criticality of the databases, real incidents and how the person handled them, quality of documentation and runbooks.

Typical mistake: assuming a cloud database needs no administrator because backups are on by default. Without restore tests, performance tuning and access control the problems surface at the worst moment. Another one: hiring a PostgreSQL DBA when most of the load sits on Oracle in the ERP, or the other way round.

FAQ

How much does a database administrator earn in Dubai?

In 2026 a junior database administrator in Dubai earns about AED 9,000 - 15,000 per month, a mid-level DBA AED 15,000 - 25,000 and a senior DBA or database architect AED 25,000 - 40,000. Banks and government entities running Oracle pay at the top of the range.

Can I hire a DBA part-time?

Yes, a small company with one or two databases often manages with an external DBA on contract or an engineer who combines the role with DevOps. A bank or a company with a 24/7 ERP needs an in-house specialist and cover for holidays. We help you choose the format and find candidates for it.
