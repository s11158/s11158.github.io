Who we help you hire

A project manager who keeps scope, dates and money visible on one page, chases vendors before they are late and gives you bad news on Monday, not on the deadline.

  • Planning: scope, milestones, dependencies and a realistic timeline built with the people who do the work, not only for the steering meeting
  • Budget and vendors: quotes compared, contracts and change requests tracked, invoices checked against delivered work
  • Status and risks: a weekly one-page report with what is done, what is late, the top risks and the decisions needed from the owner
  • Stakeholders: owner, department heads, external agencies and contractors kept aligned; meetings with agendas and written actions
  • Methods to fit the project: waterfall for a fit-out or ERP, agile sprints with a product owner for software, a simple Kanban board for small internal projects
  • Handover: the project ends with training, documentation and a clear owner for the system or space after go-live

What to check before hiring

Certificates show that a person knows the vocabulary. They do not show whether the person can bring a late project back under control. The checks below use your type of project.

1
Project plan with a slipped milestone: we give a short plan of a real-type project (a CRM rollout or a fit-out) where the vendor has just moved a key milestone by three weeks. The candidate rebuilds the plan, shows what moves with it, what can run in parallel and what they tell the owner today.
2
Status report task: a messy set of notes from a project meeting. The candidate writes the one-page status for the owner: progress, risks, decisions needed, next steps. We look for clarity and honesty about the delay.
3
Vendor and budget case: a change request from an agency that adds cost and two weeks. The candidate explains what they check in the contract, what they negotiate and how they present the choice to the owner.
4
References from sponsors: calls to the director or owner who sponsored the person's last projects: delivered on time or not, how they handled bad news, how teams worked with them; an agreed first project with a date and a budget before the start.

Typical hiring mistake: hiring a project manager for work that has no end date. If the job is to run the daily business, you need an operations manager; a project manager delivers one change at a time and then moves on. Another mistake: hiring a PM from a large corporate PMO into a 40-person company where there is no tool, no template and no team, and the PM is expected to do part of the work too. Ask what size of projects and teams the candidate really ran.

FAQ

How much does a project manager earn in Dubai?

Market estimate for 2026: about 10,000 - 15,000 AED per month for a project coordinator or junior project manager, 15,000 - 26,000 AED for a project manager and 26,000 - 40,000 AED for a senior project manager or PMO lead. Allowances, a bonus and the sector change the full package.

Project manager or operations manager: which one does my company need?

If you have a change with a start and an end, such as a system rollout, a new branch or a launch, you need a project manager. If the problem is the daily running of the business, with delays, schedules and suppliers every week, you need an operations manager. Many SMEs hire a project manager for a large one-off change and keep operations with the operations manager.
