Who we help you hire

A key account manager who knows every decision-maker in each big account, sees the renewal or the listing review coming months ahead and grows the account without giving the margin away.

  • Account plans: a written plan per key account with revenue and margin targets, the buying centre, open opportunities, risks and the next three moves
  • Retail and distribution accounts: listings, planograms, promotions calendars, rebates and trade terms with supermarket chains, pharmacy groups and hotel procurement
  • Renewals and annual negotiations: price increases, volume tiers, payment terms; preparation with finance before the meeting, not after
  • Cross-selling and upselling: new categories, brands or modules into the same customer, coordinated with product and supply teams
  • Internal work: forecasts, stock and delivery issues, credit limits and overdue payments chased together with finance and logistics
  • Typical background: senior sales executive or KAM in the same sector; English for business, Arabic or Russian where the customers need it

What to check before hiring

Many CVs say "key account management" when the person was a field sales executive with a few large clients. The checks below separate an account owner from an order-taker.

1
Account plan task: we give the candidate a one-page profile of a real-looking account (a retail chain or a contractor) with last year's sales, margin and a competitor entering. The candidate writes a short account plan: goals, people to win, risks, actions for the next quarter. We look at the logic and the numbers, not the design.
2
Price negotiation role-play: the customer's procurement head asks for a discount and longer payment terms at renewal. The candidate negotiates with an interviewer playing the buyer. We watch whether they trade (volume, listing, term) or simply give in.
3
Pipeline and forecast review: the candidate walks us through their current or last portfolio: top accounts, revenue trend, what was lost and why. Vague answers about "relationships" without numbers are a warning sign.
4
Structured reference calls: calls to a former sales director, and where possible to a former customer, about retention, margin discipline and how the candidate handled a lost tender or a supply problem.

How it differs from neighbouring roles. A sales executive and a business development manager open new customers; a key account manager keeps and grows the few customers that already bring most of the revenue. A sales manager runs the sales team and its targets. Typical hiring mistake: promoting the best hunter into key accounts and then wondering why renewals slip. Another: a commission plan paid only on revenue, so the KAM buys volume with discounts. Pay part of the variable on margin and retention.

What is the difference between a key account manager and a sales executive?

A sales executive finds and closes new customers. A key account manager owns a small number of large existing customers and is measured on retention, margin and growth inside each account. If most of your revenue comes from a handful of chains, groups or contractors, you need a KAM; if you need new customers, you need a sales executive or a business development manager.
