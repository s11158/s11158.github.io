Logistics, procurement and warehouse in Dubai: how to hire the team

Jebel Ali, free zones, RTA licences, forklift permits, WMS and customs: who to look for, what it costs in 2026 and how to keep one vacancy from stopping the warehouse.

Dubai runs on logistics: Jebel Ali port, the JAFZA free zone, the Dubai South logistics district next to Al Maktoum airport, warehouses in Al Quoz, Ras Al Khor and DIP, and fast-growing e-commerce with same-day delivery. That is why several companies compete at once for a strong storekeeper, a driver with the right licence or a buyer who knows SAP. A bad hire here costs money quickly: the warehouse ships the wrong items, a container sits at customs because of a wrong HS code, a van stands idle without a driver. Below is a practical breakdown for owners and managers of trading, distribution, e-commerce and logistics companies.

Who companies usually hire: three levels

Procurement and supply chain management. A procurement manager runs suppliers, tenders and contracts and owns price and lead times. A supply chain manager ties demand forecasting, purchasing, warehousing and delivery into one plan and owns inventory turnover and service level. What matters for these roles is experience in your industry (FMCG, building materials, healthcare, electronics), hands-on ERP work and the ability to negotiate in English, and often in Arabic or Russian when suppliers and customers speak it.

Warehouse. A warehouse manager is responsible for receiving, storage, picking, stock counts, safety and the people on shift. A storekeeper keeps stock records, works with the scanner and the WMS and books goods in and out. Forklift operators and pickers belong here too.

Transport and trade. A delivery driver on a motorbike or van, a fleet manager who owns vehicles, insurance, Salik, fines and telematics, a freight forwarder for sea, air and road shipments, and an import-export coordinator who prepares documents and moves cargo through customs.

Licences, permits and systems: what to check before the offer

  • The right UAE licence class. RTA issues licences by class: motorcycle, light vehicle, heavy vehicle, buses. Foreign licences can be exchanged only for citizens of certain countries; everyone else has to train at a Dubai driving school, which means weeks and costs. Check that the licence is already in hand and valid, not "in progress".
  • RTA training for delivery riders. Motorbike delivery in Dubai comes with mandatory training and RTA requirements for riders and equipment. Confirm the status with the candidate and with the operator if you work through an aggregator.
  • Forklift permit. A forklift operator in Dubai needs a separate RTA permit for mechanical equipment, and inside JAFZA and other zones often a pass and zone safety training as well. A certificate from another country does not replace the local one on its own.
  • WMS and ERP. For the warehouse: experience with SAP EWM, Oracle WMS, Infor, Manhattan or cloud systems for e-commerce; for procurement: SAP MM, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, Odoo. Someone who spent years in Excel can be a strong practitioner, but needs ramp-up time on a mature system.
  • Customs. Declarations go through Dubai Customs and the Mirsal system; some goods need permits from the relevant authorities. A coordinator should understand HS codes, Incoterms, certificates of origin and free zone regimes (import, re-export, transfers between zones).
  • Procurement qualifications. CIPS is valued by large companies and government buyers, but it does not replace real tender and negotiation experience.
Important: RTA, Dubai Customs and free zone rules change, and access requirements depend on the zone and type of cargo. Check current conditions on the regulators' portals or with your PRO before making an offer.

Mainland or free zone

If the company is registered in JAFZA, Dubai South, DAFZA or another zone, visas go through the zone's portal and the employee usually works on the zone's territory and licence. On the mainland the visa runs through MOHRE and GDRFA. For a warehouse inside a zone, plan ahead for site passes and the visa quota tied to your floor space. The general steps are in the guide on work visas and permits.

Salaries in Dubai: a 2026 guide

Ranges in AED per month, total package, based on our data from closed and open roles. The full cost of an employee with visa, insurance and housing is covered in the guide what it costs to hire in Dubai.

  • Supply chain manager - 22,000-45,000; procurement manager - 18,000-35,000
  • Warehouse manager - 12,000-25,000, higher at large DCs and cold stores
  • Fleet manager - 12,000-22,000
  • Freight forwarder (operations or sales) - 6,000-15,000, sales often with a margin bonus
  • Import-export coordinator - 5,000-10,000
  • Storekeeper - 3,000-6,000; forklift operator - 2,500-4,500 plus housing and transport
  • Van driver - 3,000-5,000; truck driver - 4,000-7,000; motorbike rider - 2,500-4,500 including per-order pay

Seasonality

The peak is the fourth quarter: White Friday sales, the run-up to Dubai Shopping Festival and New Year. Another spike comes before Ramadan and Eid, when food and gift purchasing grows. If your peak is in November, look for key people in August-September and for a temporary shift by October. From 15 June to 15 September the midday break bans outdoor work from 12:30 to 15:00, which affects loading in open yards and shift planning.

Typical employer mistakes

  • Hiring a driver "with a licence" that turns out to be foreign or the wrong class.
  • Not checking the forklift permit and finding out during a zone inspection or after an incident.
  • Searching for a buyer without a clear spend category and comparing a building materials specialist with an FMCG buyer.
  • Not testing real WMS experience: "has seen SAP" and "ran receiving and stock counts in SAP EWM" are different levels.
  • Saving on the package for warehouse staff and drivers, then losing people in peak season to a competitor that offers housing and overtime.
  • Not allowing time for zone passes, medical and Emirates ID, so the employee starts a month after the offer.

What to ask in the interview

Ask for numbers, not general statements. Ask a warehouse manager about floor area and SKU count, orders shipped per day, stock count accuracy and how they improved it. Ask a buyer about annual spend, number of suppliers, their last difficult tender and the savings they delivered. Ask a forwarder or coordinator which cargo and routes they handled, how they resolved a customs delay and how they build a rate. Ask a fleet manager how many vehicles they ran, the downtime rate and how they cut fines and fuel use.

Check languages separately: English is usually the working language, Hindi, Urdu or Arabic help in the warehouse and fleet, and if your suppliers or customers are from the CIS, a Russian-speaking buyer or coordinator saves hours of correspondence. Test the language in a short work conversation, not by a line on the CV.

A step-by-step hiring plan

  • Step 1. Role profile. Tasks and numbers (orders per day, SKUs, spend), required licences and permits, systems, languages, shift pattern, package budget.
  • Step 2. Sources. Candidates already in the UAE with local licences start sooner; from abroad the choice is wider but processing takes longer. The steps are covered in the guide how to hire staff in Dubai.
  • Step 3. Document screening. Licences and permits, certificates, regional and industry experience, references.
  • Step 4. Practical check. For a storekeeper, a receiving and stock reconciliation task; for a driver, a route and a test drive; for a buyer, a case comparing supplier quotes; for a coordinator, a document pack for a sample shipment.
  • Step 5. Offer and onboarding paperwork. The employer arranges the visa, insurance and passes and covers these costs.
  • Step 6. Ramp-up. Warehouse safety induction, WMS access, clear KPIs for the first 90 days.

Checklist before the offer

  • UAE licence class or forklift permit checked against the original
  • WMS or ERP experience confirmed with a practical task
  • Knowledge of customs procedures and Incoterms tested on a case
  • References from the last two employers received
  • Package agreed: base, housing, transport, overtime, bonus
  • Visa, medical and zone pass timelines built into the plan
  • Shift pattern and summer midday break accounted for

Frequently asked questions

What licence does a delivery driver need in Dubai?

A motorbike rider needs a UAE motorcycle licence plus the RTA delivery rider training, a van driver needs a light vehicle licence and a truck driver a heavy vehicle licence. Foreign licences can be exchanged only for some countries; everyone else has to train at a Dubai driving school, which takes from a few weeks to a couple of months.

How much does a logistics or procurement professional cost in Dubai in 2026?

Market guide: import-export coordinator 5,000-10,000 AED a month, procurement manager 18,000-35,000 AED, supply chain manager 22,000-45,000 AED. The final figure depends on the industry, spend under management, ERP and UAE experience.
