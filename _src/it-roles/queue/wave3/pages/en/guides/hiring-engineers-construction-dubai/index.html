Engineers and site staff in Dubai: how to hire without delays

Construction, MEP and facilities management: which approvals matter, what specialists cost in 2026, and how to run hiring so your site never stands still.

Construction and building operations in Dubai are growing faster than the market can train people: new districts, D33 infrastructure, Expo City and thousands of handed-over towers that need permanent service. A bad hire costs more here than in an office. An engineer without the right approvals cannot sign a drawing, an HSE officer without the right certificate fails the client audit, and an idle crew burns the budget in days. This is a practical guide for owners and managers of contracting, consultancy and FM companies.

Who companies usually hire: three groups of roles

Engineers and the project team. Civil engineers, site engineers and MEP engineers, plus specialists such as a mechanical engineer for HVAC and plumbing design and an electrical engineer for power and low-current systems. The group also includes quantity surveyors, who own the BOQ, variations and payment applications, and the construction project manager.

Design and fit-out. Architects for consultancies and developers and interior designers for fit-out, restaurant and villa projects. Portfolio, knowledge of local codes and the ability to steer a project through approvals decide the hire.

Operations and maintenance. Facility managers, maintenance technicians, HVAC technicians, electricians and plumbers. In FM contracts response time, SLA discipline and CAFM experience matter, and in summer air conditioning becomes a critical service.

The HSE officer deserves a separate mention: a large site will not open without one, and on smaller projects the role is often combined with another job, which then surfaces in the client's audit.

Approvals and regulators: what to check before the offer

  • Attested degree. To get an engineering title on the visa, the degree is usually legalised in the issuing country and by the UAE MOFA. This takes 2 to 6 weeks, so ask the candidate for documents early.
  • Society of Engineers UAE. Registration of the engineer in their discipline. Licensed companies and authorities ask for it, and a candidate with active registration starts work faster.
  • Dubai Municipality. Accreditation of consultant and contractor engineers by category: these are the people who sign documents and answer to the municipality. Changing the responsible engineer affects the company licence.
  • Dubai Civil Defence. Fire and life safety work (sprinklers, alarms, evacuation) needs specialists and contractors approved by Civil Defence. Experience of handing over buildings to Civil Defence is a strong plus on a CV.
  • DEWA, Trakhees, free zones. Electrical connections go through DEWA; areas under Trakhees and some free zones have their own rules for site personnel.
  • HSE certificates. For an HSE officer the market baseline is NEBOSH IGC, often with IOSH and incident reporting experience. Projects in Abu Dhabi add OSHAD requirements.
Important: rules and category lists change, and requirements depend on the emirate and zone. Before making an offer, confirm current conditions on the regulators' portals or with your PRO.

Salaries in Dubai: 2026 guide

  • Construction project manager - 25,000-50,000, often with housing or a transport allowance
  • MEP engineer - 12,000-25,000; mechanical and electrical engineers - 10,000-20,000
  • Civil engineer - 10,000-22,000; site engineer - 8,000-15,000
  • Quantity surveyor - 10,000-22,000, higher with FIDIC and major developer experience
  • Architect - 12,000-28,000; interior designer - 8,000-18,000
  • Facility manager - 18,000-35,000; HSE officer - 6,000-12,000
  • HVAC and maintenance technicians - 3,500-7,000; electricians and plumbers - 2,500-6,000 plus company housing and transport

Seasonality and site realities

From 15 June to 15 September the UAE enforces a mandatory midday break: outdoor work in direct sun is banned from 12:30 to 15:00, and violations are fined per worker. Shift patterns, shade, water and rest areas must be planned before summer, not in July.

The best hiring window is September to March: peak season, more candidates in the country and fewer relocations. In summer some specialists travel, while FM companies urgently need HVAC technicians and competition for them rises. If your project starts in autumn, begin searching for key engineers in July or August.

Typical employer mistakes

  • Hiring from a CV without checking approvals and discovering the missing registration when a signature is needed.
  • Ignoring the time needed for degree attestation and the visa, so the site waits an extra month.
  • Writing a vague "engineer for everything" profile and then comparing candidates who cannot be compared.
  • Not checking real project scale: "worked on a tower" and "led MEP on a tower" are different levels.
  • Combining HSE with another role to save money and collecting findings in the client audit.
  • Underpaying technicians and losing them after 3 months to a competitor that offers housing and overtime.

What to ask in the interview

Engineering CVs in the region often look alike, so build the conversation around specifics. Ask the candidate to describe their last project: building type and height, contract value, their scope and who they reported to. Ask how they closed consultant comments, how many variations they handled and how they agreed them with the client. For an MEP engineer, check whether they personally handed systems over to Civil Defence and DEWA or only attended. For a facility manager, ask how many buildings and technicians they ran, what SLAs applied and how they measured downtime.

Discuss languages too: English is usually the site language, while Hindi, Urdu or Arabic help with parts of the crew. If clients are Russian-speaking developers or villa owners, fluent Russian is a real advantage for an engineer or designer. Test language in a short working dialogue, not by a line on the CV.

Step-by-step hiring plan

  • Step 1. Role profile. Tasks, project types, required approvals, software (AutoCAD, Revit, Primavera P6, CAFM), working languages with the team and client, package budget.
  • Step 2. Sources. Candidates already in the UAE start faster; overseas candidates widen the choice but take longer to process. The full sequence is in the guide how to hire staff in Dubai.
  • Step 3. Document screening. Degree, registrations, certificates, regional experience, references from previous sites.
  • Step 4. Practical check. For engineers, a drawing or BOQ review and a variation or programme case; for technicians, diagnosing a typical fault.
  • Step 5. Offer and paperwork. The employer arranges and pays for the visa and work permit; steps and timing are in the guide work visa and permit.
  • Step 6. Site onboarding. Safety induction, passes, introduction to the team and clear goals for the first 90 days.

Checklist before the offer

  • Degree attested or attestation under way
  • Society of Engineers registration and required accreditations verified
  • HSE certificates (NEBOSH, IOSH) valid
  • Practical check passed and documented
  • References from the last two sites received
  • Package agreed: base, housing, transport, overtime
  • Onboarding plan and summer-break schedule ready

FAQ

Does an engineer in Dubai need Society of Engineers registration?

For an engineering job title on the visa the employer usually needs an attested degree, and to sign drawings or work for a licensed consultant or contractor the engineer typically needs Society of Engineers registration and Dubai Municipality accreditation in the right category. Requirements vary by role and zone, so check them before the offer.

How much does an engineer cost in Dubai in 2026?

Market guide: a site engineer earns 8,000-15,000 AED a month, an MEP engineer 12,000-25,000 AED and a construction project manager 25,000-50,000 AED plus housing or an allowance. The final figure depends on UAE experience, approvals and project scale.
