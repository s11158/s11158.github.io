Hiring tech talent in Dubai: an employer's guide

Developers, DevOps, data, AI and product: licences, visas, 2026 salaries, skills testing and a step-by-step plan.

Hiring a tech specialist in Dubai works differently from hiring a receptionist or a stylist. The market is global: one developer vacancy draws applicants from dozens of countries, half of them not yet in the UAE, and strong candidates often hold two or three offers at once. Below is how tech hiring works in 2026 - from company licence and visa to salary ranges, skills testing and the mistakes that cost employers their best candidates.

Which roles are in demand

Demand in the UAE rides on three waves: digitalisation of government and banking, product teams and startups relocating, and AI moving into everyday businesses - from clinics to logistics. In practice employers look for:

  • developers: generalist software developers, frontend developers on React and Vue, backend developers on Python, Node.js, Java and .NET;
  • infrastructure: DevOps engineers, cloud architects (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and in-country clouds), network engineers and system administrators;
  • data and AI: data scientists, machine learning engineers and AI engineers who build language models into products and workflows;
  • enterprise systems: ERP consultants (SAP, Oracle, Microsoft Dynamics, Odoo) and Salesforce developers;
  • product and quality: product owners, scrum masters, QA engineers and, for scaling companies, a CTO.

Where your company is licensed: mainland, Dubai Internet City, DIFC

Your licence decides who issues the visa and which law governs the job. On the mainland (DET licence) the work permit goes through MOHRE, employment is governed by Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, and salary is paid through the WPS. Companies with 50 or more employees also have Emiratisation targets to plan around.

Most tech companies sit in free zones. Dubai Internet City, Dubai Silicon Oasis, DMCC and other zones issue visas themselves, and the visa quota is usually tied to office space. Check your free quota before you start searching, or your chosen candidate will wait while you expand the office. DIFC is a separate jurisdiction with its own Employment Law (DIFC Law No. 2 of 2019) and the DEWS savings scheme instead of classic end-of-service gratuity. That matters for fintech: contract, probation and payments follow DIFC rules.

Golden Visa and other visa routes

Tech talent has several routes. The standard work visa is arranged and paid for by the employer, and these costs cannot be deducted from salary. Strong specialists can often qualify for the 10-year Golden Visa - for example under the skilled professionals category with a university degree and a documented salary level (a benchmark of AED 30,000 basic per month), or through the programme for digital and coding talent. A Golden Visa is not tied to a sponsor, so candidates who hold one value flexibility and can usually start faster. Criteria change, so confirm current conditions with ICP and GDRFA for the specific person. Step-by-step paperwork is covered in our guide to the work visa and work permit.

Remote or on-site

A remote specialist abroad and an employee in Dubai are different legal arrangements. Someone living in another country does not become your UAE employee just because you pay them from Dubai: that is a contractor agreement or employment through an entity in their country, with different tax and risk. If you need a person on the team with access to internal systems and client data, it is usually cleaner to employ them in the UAE. A common setup is hybrid: two or three days in the office, the rest remote. State it in the job description upfront - for tech candidates the work format often matters more than an extra AED 2,000.

Salary benchmarks for 2026

The ranges below are monthly basic plus allowances, before bonuses and equity. They move a lot with stack, industry and the candidate's previous employer.

  • Junior developer - AED 9,000-15,000; mid-level - AED 16,000-26,000; senior - AED 27,000-42,000; tech lead - AED 40,000-55,000.
  • DevOps and SRE - AED 22,000-45,000; cloud architect - AED 40,000-65,000.
  • Data scientist - AED 22,000-42,000; ML and AI engineer - AED 28,000-55,000, higher for rare profiles.
  • QA engineer - AED 12,000-26,000; system administrator - AED 10,000-20,000; network engineer - AED 14,000-30,000.
  • ERP consultant - AED 20,000-45,000; Salesforce developer - AED 22,000-40,000.
  • Scrum master - AED 20,000-34,000; product owner - AED 25,000-45,000; CTO - from AED 55,000, often with equity.

On top of salary, budget for health insurance, the visa, an annual flight and end-of-service. The full breakdown is in our guide to the cost of hiring in Dubai.

Seasonality and timelines

Peak months are September to December and January to March: budgets are approved and people are back from holidays. In July and August many are away and interviews stretch out. During Ramadan the working day is shorter and decisions slow down. Factor in notice periods too: in the UAE they are typically one to three months, and a candidate from abroad also needs to relocate. A realistic cycle from starting the search to day one is 5-10 weeks for a mid-level specialist and longer for senior and leadership roles.

How to test skills

A tech CV says little on its own: the same list of technologies is written by people who built the system and by people who touched it once. A structured check works better:

  • a short technical screen of 30-40 minutes based on your team's real problems;
  • a 2-4 hour practical task or a live code review with the candidate - not a multi-day unpaid project;
  • for architects and CTOs, a system design session: how they would design, scale and secure your product;
  • a language check: English as the working language, plus Russian or Arabic if your team or clients need it;
  • references from former managers with specific questions, not a formal "they were fine".

Common mistakes

  • A vague profile: "a programmer for everything" instead of a defined stack, level and six-month goals.
  • Five interview rounds over a month. A strong candidate accepts another offer in that time.
  • A budget below market, offset with "interesting challenges".
  • No free visa quota in the free zone at the moment of the offer.
  • A week-long unpaid test task: the best candidates simply decline.

A step-by-step hiring plan

  • Step 1. Describe the goals for 6-12 months, stack, level, work format and package budget.
  • Step 2. Check your licence and quota, decide who arranges the visa and how long it takes.
  • Step 3. Build a long list from databases, referrals and direct search, and filter on the must-haves.
  • Step 4. Run the screen and practical check, ideally within one to two weeks.
  • Step 5. Final meeting with the hiring manager and team, plus reference checks.
  • Step 6. Offer with the full package, contract, visa and onboarding with a 90-day plan.
Pre-launch checklist: stack and level written down; budget checked against the market; quota available and visa type clear; work format decided; assessment stages and owners assigned; no more than three meetings with each candidate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to hire a developer in Dubai?

A mid-level developer usually earns AED 16,000-26,000 a month and a senior AED 27,000-42,000. On top of that the employer pays for the visa, health insurance, an annual flight and end-of-service.

Can tech professionals get a UAE Golden Visa?

Often yes - for example under the skilled professionals category with a degree and documented salary, or through the programme for digital talent. Criteria change, so check current conditions with ICP and GDRFA for the specific person.

How long does it take to hire a tech specialist?

For a mid-level role a realistic cycle from search start to day one is 5-10 weeks, including notice period and visa. TLNT sends the first shortlist in 2 days on average.

Can I hire a remote developer based in another country?

Yes, but that is a contractor agreement or employment in their country, not a UAE employment contract. If you need the person on staff with access to internal systems, employing them in the UAE is usually cleaner.
