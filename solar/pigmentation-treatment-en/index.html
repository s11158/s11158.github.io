The method

What pigmentation is

Pigmentation is an uneven distribution of melanin in the skin. Melanin is the skin's own defence: ultraviolet light, inflammation or hormonal shifts tell the cells to produce more of it, and it settles in patches instead of evenly. In Dubai the trigger is almost always the same one - sun exposure through the long summer, often combined with heat and hormones.

1

Different types, different treatment

Sun spots, melasma, post-inflammatory marks after acne and age spots look similar in the mirror but sit at different depths and respond to different protocols.

2

Surface or deep

Superficial pigment responds well to peels and brightening care. Deeper pigment, especially melasma, needs a careful laser approach and patience, because aggressive settings can make it worse.

3

It is a chronic tendency, not a one-off stain

Skin that has made pigment once will make it again under the same conditions. That is why a course is always paired with daily sun protection.

Why SOLAR

Treatment starts with diagnostics, not with the laser

Pigmentation is the one skin problem where the wrong method makes things worse. So we begin by finding out what we are treating.

Pigmentation is not one condition

Melasma, post-inflammatory pigmentation after acne, sun-induced spots and age spots have different causes and different behaviour. A protocol that clears sun spots can darken melasma.

The protocol is chosen after examination

The doctor examines the skin, asks about hormones, medication and sun habits, and only then decides whether the course starts with peels, with the Harmony XL Pro laser or with brightening care.

Photo type decides the settings

Skin that tans easily is more likely to react with new pigment. For darker photo types we prepare the skin first, work with lower energy and longer intervals between sessions.

Progress is checked, not assumed

The 3D Skin Test is repeated during the course. If the pigment is not responding as expected, the protocol is adjusted rather than simply continued.

Evidence base

What the research says

Pigmentation is one of the most studied areas in dermatology, and the evidence is consistent on one point: combined protocols outperform single methods, and results hold only with sun protection.

Controlled studies

Chemical peels

Controlled trials of superficial and medium-depth peels in melasma and post-inflammatory pigmentation report improvement in pigment scores over a course of sessions, with better outcomes when peels are combined with topical treatment.

Comparative studies

Lasers and light devices

Comparative studies of laser and light-based treatment for pigmented lesions show clearance of sun-induced spots, while for melasma they emphasise conservative settings and a higher risk of recurrence or rebound pigmentation.

Reviews

Topical care and photoprotection

Reviews of topical brightening agents and daily broad-spectrum sunscreen consistently find that maintenance care is what keeps pigment from returning after in-clinic treatment.

The studies refer to the methods (peels, lasers, topical care), not to a specific clinic. Results are individual and depend on the type of pigmentation, photo type and how consistently sun protection is used.

The process

Step by step

Consultation (free)

The doctor examines the skin, discusses your history, medication and sun exposure, and explains what is realistic for your type of pigmentation.

3D Skin Test Janus III (30 minutes)

A full scan of the skin: surface and deep pigment, vascular component, texture and hydration. This is the baseline your course is measured against.

The course

Usually 3-6 sessions spaced 2-4 weeks apart: Harmony XL Pro laser, chemical peels, PRX-T33 or Dermapen mesotherapy, often with Heleo4 LED therapy to calm the skin afterwards.

Maintenance

Home care and daily sun protection between and after sessions, with a check-up scan to see what has changed.

Good to know: no sunbathing, no scrubs and no active acids without agreeing them with the doctor during the course. Broad-spectrum SPF every morning is not optional here - it is part of the treatment.

FAQ

Honest answers

How many sessions will I need?

Usually 3-6, spaced 2-4 weeks apart. Isolated sun spots can clear faster; melasma takes longer and is managed rather than erased. The doctor gives you a realistic number after the scan, not before it. The number of sessions, the area and the methods in your protocol are what the price depends on, and the full course plan with its price is agreed at the free consultation.

Will the pigmentation come back?

It can, and that is the honest answer. Skin that produces pigment once will do it again under the same triggers - sun, heat, hormones. Daily broad-spectrum SPF and maintenance care are what keep the result; without them the spots return after the next summer.

Can pigmentation be treated in summer?

Some methods yes, some no. Aggressive laser settings on sun-exposed skin are a poor idea in July. Peels, PRX-T33 and brightening care can be done year round with strict sun protection. September to April is the best window for the active part of the course, which is why we start now.

Is it safe for darker skin?

Yes, with the right approach. Darker photo types react more readily with new pigment, so we prepare the skin, use lower energy settings and longer intervals, and lean more on peels and topical care than on aggressive laser work.

What should I do at home between sessions?

Broad-spectrum SPF 50 every morning, reapplied through the day, plus the brightening and barrier care the doctor prescribes. Home care is not an add-on to the course - it is roughly half of the result.
