The method

What acne scar treatment is

Acne scars form when inflammation damages collagen in the deeper layers of the skin and the repair leaves a dent, a fibrous band or a pigment mark. No device removes that damage in one pass: the work is to trigger controlled remodelling of collagen again and again, so the edges of each scar soften and the surface levels out.

1

Controlled micro-injury

Microneedling and fractional laser create thousands of tiny channels that the skin repairs with new collagen. The intact tissue between the channels is what makes recovery quick.

2

Releasing the tether

Rolling scars are held down by fibrous strands underneath. Needling and radiofrequency energy loosen those strands so the depression can lift.

3

Surface and pigment

Chemical peels and PRX-T33 work on tone, stagnant post-inflammatory spots and the roughness that makes texture read as damaged skin even when the scars themselves are shallow.

Different scars need different methods

The word "post-acne" covers several distinct kinds of damage, and what helps one type does little for another.

Ice pick scars

Narrow and deep, like a pinprick that goes straight down. They need energy delivered into the depth rather than surface polishing, which is where radiofrequency microneedling is usually the main tool.

Boxcar scars

Round or oval depressions with sharp vertical edges. The aim is to soften the edge and build collagen in the floor of the scar, so fractional laser and needling are combined across the course.

Rolling scars

Wide, soft undulations held down by fibrous bands. Loosening the tether matters more than resurfacing, and improvement typically appears later in the course than with other types.

Post-inflammatory pigmentation

Brown or red marks that are flat, not indented. These are not scars in the structural sense and often respond to peels, PRX-T33 and consistent home care with sun protection. Most skin carries a mix of types, so the protocol, the number of sessions and the intervals are set after an examination and 3D skin diagnostics, not from a photograph.

Evidence base

What the research says

Atrophic acne scarring is one of the better-studied areas in dermatology, and the three methods we use are the ones most often examined in the literature.

Controlled trials

Microneedling

Studies of percutaneous collagen induction in atrophic acne scars report improvement in scar severity scores after a series of sessions, with the effect building across the course rather than after a single treatment.

Comparative studies

Fractional lasers

Comparative and split-face research on fractional resurfacing describes measurable improvement in atrophic scars, with the trade-off between ablative and non-ablative settings expressed mainly as more downtime for a stronger single-session effect.

Reviews

Chemical peels

Reviews of chemical peeling for acne scarring and post-inflammatory pigmentation report benefit for surface texture and tone, and note that repeated, correctly spaced sessions matter more than the strength of any one peel.

The studies refer to the methods (microneedling, fractional lasers, chemical peels), not to a specific clinic. Results are individual and depend on scar type, skin type and how consistently the course is completed.

The process

Step by step

Doctor's consultation (free)

The doctor examines the skin under magnification, identifies which scar types are present and checks whether acne is still active.

Skin diagnostics

3D Skin Test Janus III maps texture, pores and pigment and gives a baseline image to compare against later in the course.

The course

Usually 3 to 6 sessions with 3-4 week intervals, combining methods: RF microneedling or Dermapen for structure, peels and PRX-T33 for tone and surface.

Review and maintenance

The doctor compares the diagnostics, decides whether to extend the course, and sets home care - retinoids and sun protection do a large part of the work between sessions.

Good to know: strict daily sun protection is part of the treatment, not an optional extra. Without it, new pigment marks form faster than the course can improve them.

FAQ

Honest answers

How many sessions will I need?

Most people need 3 to 6, spaced 3 to 4 weeks apart. Deep ice pick and rolling scars often need the upper end of that range, and the doctor reassesses partway through rather than selling a fixed package up front. The number of sessions and the methods are what the price depends on, and the course plan with its price is agreed at the free consultation.

Will the scars disappear completely?

No. Acne scars are structural damage in the dermis, and honest treatment improves their depth, edges and visibility rather than erasing them. Most clients see a clear improvement in texture and in how the skin catches light, which is what makes scars noticeable in the first place.

Is there a recovery period?

Redness for one to three days after microneedling or RF, similar to sunburn, with some peeling after stronger peels. You can wear makeup again once the skin has closed, usually the next day, and sessions are scheduled around your plans.

Can it be done while acne is still active?

Not in the inflamed areas. Active breakouts need to be treated first, otherwise a procedure can spread inflammation and create new marks. The doctor may start with peels and cleansing while the acne is being brought under control.

When will I see the result?

Texture starts to look different after the second or third session, and the fullest effect appears 3 to 6 months after the course ends, because collagen keeps remodelling long after the last treatment.
